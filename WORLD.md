## What this document is for

This is the foundational world document. It exists to answer the question: what kind of world did this war grow out of, and what does it look like when the player arrives? Everything else — factions, characters, story — is downstream of what lives here.

Read this before writing any character, scene, or dialogue. If a detail you want to use contradicts something here, either change the detail or update this document deliberately. Do not let contradictions accumulate silently.

## The resource

At the centre of the world's history is a finite resource — call it **the Vein** for now [OPEN: rename when the world has more shape] — that exists at fixed physical sites scattered across the world: places where something in the deep geology of the land produces a concentration of resonant field energy and, over vast timescales, condenses that energy into physical matter. These sites can be mapped, contested, and controlled.

The Vein depletes as it is extracted and used. The rate of depletion varies by extraction method, and the Coalition's systematic industrial approach extracts faster than the Order's more measured instinctual use. The asymmetry is not merely one of scale but of kind: the Coalition removes the condensed physical material that sustains the field, which reduces field intensity at extraction sites far faster than drawing on the field's energy directly ever would.

The consequence that matters in the current era is not resource extinction — there is enough substrate remaining across all sites to sustain both factions for generations. The consequence is territorial. Reduced field intensity at a site means that site's surrounding territory becomes progressively more habitable for attuned people. The biological barrier that historically kept the Order out of Coalition land — the overexposure sensitivity that made high-concentration territories genuinely hostile to attuned practitioners — is directly tied to field concentration. Every generation of Coalition extraction lowers that concentration at worked sites, and with it the barrier. The Coalition has been, through the ordinary operation of its economy, slowly converting its own territorial advantage into contested ground.

This process is not a future threat. It is already in motion and already visible to anyone who tracks it. The Order's expanding operational range in territories it once had to cede is a direct product of Coalition industry reducing the phenomenon's intensity at extraction sites — but this causal connection is not necessarily what either faction has formally named. What military planners are measuring and responding to is the observable fact: the Order can now operate in places it previously could not, and that boundary is moving inward. Why it is moving, and what that implies about the long-term trajectory of Coalition territorial advantage, is not understood institutionally by either faction. The Coalition's industrial heartland — the sites most heavily extracted over the longest period — may already be within the Order's operational tolerance. Both sides are planning around the military reality. Whether either side has fully reckoned with what is producing it is a different question.

Neither faction has publicly named the depletion dynamic. No institution on either side has formed an official position on it. Order practitioners ranging further afield attribute the expansion to opportunity or to the shifting fortunes of war; Coalition extraction engineers track site yield decline as an operational logistics problem without connecting it to the barrier effect. The effects are rationalized within each faction's existing frameworks rather than recognized as symptoms of a common cause. A handful of individuals on both sides — engineers, practitioners, people who sit at the frontier of knowledge in their respective contexts — have independently arrived at a more complete picture of the mechanism and hold it privately. The crisis is slow and not yet acute, and that distributed private knowledge makes the mechanism discoverable by story characters without requiring any institution to have named it first.

The Order's institutional history gave them no basis to develop a concept of depletion. Their use of the Vein — drawing on the field directly, precisely, at the rates individual practitioners can sustain — produced no observable decline at any site within a human lifetime or across recorded generations. The lake looked the same as it always had. Depletion as a physical phenomenon, and extraction as its cause, were concepts the Order had no empirical reason to form. When Coalition industrial extraction began moving the needle at worked sites, what the Order observed was not a resource problem they lacked the framework to name — it was a territorial problem they could measure directly. The barrier was softening. Sites that had been operationally hostile were becoming accessible. The cause was invisible to them not because it was hidden, but because nothing in their history had taught them to look for it.

The Vein can only be interacted with meaningfully by people with attunement — but the two factions have developed fundamentally different relationships to that fact, and to the resource itself. See the faction descriptions and the Vein sections below.

## The Vein — physical nature and the two forms

The Vein is a single phenomenon expressed at different concentrations. Understanding this is the foundation of everything else in the world's history.

**The phenomenon.** At certain sites, conditions of pressure, depth, and the deep physical history of the land produce a concentration of resonant energy in the geology itself. This is not a field that exists separately from matter and then condenses into it — it is the same underlying phenomenon at different density levels. At low concentrations, it permeates the site and surrounding environment diffusely: present, real, physically consequential, but too sparse to be detected by instruments or perceived by most people. At high concentrations, it densifies into physical matter — the substrate — a tangible solid that can be extracted, processed, transported, and industrialized. The diffuse expression and the dense solid are not two different things. They are the same thing at different scales, the way steam and ice are both water.

What the non-attuned population observed during the development period were the secondary effects of the diffuse expression on their environment: instruments behaving strangely near high-concentration sites, materials responding in anomalous ways, the ecology measurably different from surrounding land. These effects scale with concentration — stronger near dense substrate deposits, weaker at the margins of a site. This gradient is what the Coalition's science was built on: a multigenerational study of what the phenomenon does at the concentrations instruments can detect.

**The extraction problem.** When the Coalition removes substrate from a site, they are removing the densest concentrations of the phenomenon. What remains is the lower-density diffuse expression — still present, still produced by the underlying geological conditions, but expressing at a fraction of the original intensity. The site is not dead. It is depleted. The geological conditions that produced the original concentration are still operating, slowly rebuilding density from the residual diffuse expression. But the rate of that rebuilding is geological: centuries to approach the concentrations that existed before systematic extraction began. The Coalition's extraction rate so far exceeds the rebuilding rate that recovery is functionally irrelevant in any human timeframe.

This is why extraction reduces field intensity at worked sites: not because the source has been switched off, but because the dense concentrations that produced strong field expression have been physically removed, leaving only the diffuse residual the source is slowly rebuilding from.

**The key asymmetry.** The Coalition can only work with the phenomenon at extraction concentrations — dense enough for their instruments to detect, measure, and manipulate. Their entire technological development was building apparatus sensitive enough to function at that threshold. Below that threshold, their instruments register nothing.

Attunement is sensitivity to the phenomenon across its full concentration range, including the diffuse low-density expression that falls below any instrument threshold the Coalition has developed. An Order practitioner working at a site is interacting with the same substance the Coalition extracts — not a separate thing called the field — but across concentrations the Coalition has no framework for, because their science was built entirely on the dense end and has no data on what the phenomenon does at very low concentrations. A worked-out Coalition site that reads as dead to every instrument they possess may still be legible, faintly, to an attuned practitioner. What they find there is diminished — thin, quiet, demanding — but present. This is one of the things the Coalition does not know it does not know.

**The energy dynamic.** The Vein does not generate energy. It is a finite concentration of energy that accumulated over geological timescales, and every interaction with it is energy moving — between states, between locations, between the stored concentration and the world drawing from it. Energy is never created or destroyed, only moved, concentrated, or dispersed. Both factions draw from the same reservoir; both contribute to its depletion, the Coalition faster and at greater volume, the Order more slowly and precisely, neither exempt from the physics.

The Vein is not a neutral medium that facilitates transformations, the way a catalyst does. It releases more energy in output than a practitioner expends in accessing it — not because it generates surplus, but because the practitioner is not the source. The concentration built up in substrate represents vast accumulation across timescales no human civilization can replicate. A practitioner using the Vein is opening a valve on a pressurized system that was pressurized long before anyone was alive to use it. The personal cost of using the Vein is biological — exposure load, attunement fatigue, the risk of damage to the biological structures that make perception possible. The civilizational cost is geological — depletion of concentration that will not rebuild on any timescale that matters to the people depleting it. These two costs operate on completely different timescales, which is precisely why the depletion problem has been possible to ignore. The person paying the personal cost never feels the geological cost. They feel the effort; they do not feel the world getting quieter.

**What the substrate looks like — stable state.** In its stable, unmanipulated form the substrate is a dense, matte black mineral. No luminescence. No visual strangeness from the outside. It reads as serious dark rock — the kind of thing that belongs deep in the ground and has been there for a very long time. Its weight is slightly wrong for its size, denser than it looks. From the outside, in rough unworked form, there is nothing that announces what it is.

When fractured — by extraction, processing, damage, or anything that breaks the surface — the break faces are flat and reflective, with the mirror quality of fresh galena cleavage. The interior revealed by fracture is structurally distinct from the exterior: the outside weathers and oxidizes like ordinary rock, but the interior faces are clean, geometric, and reflective in a way that looks almost manufactured. This is the first indication, visible to anyone, that the material is not ordinary.

The substrate has a thermodynamic tendency toward enclosure — the mirror faces exposed by fracture bleed concentration into the surrounding environment, and sealing over arrests that bleed. Left unattended, the surface resolves to the closed state not because it cools but because closure is the lower-energy configuration: the gradient driving it disappears the moment the surface is no longer exposed. This happens at any ambient temperature, driven entirely by the concentration gradient between the exposed interior and the surrounding environment.

The rate of closure scales with how much concentration the substrate retains: rich substrate closes quickly because the gradient driving closure is steep; depleted substrate closes slowly or barely at all because there is little concentration left to bleed and therefore little driver for the transition. A practitioner can read site history from this — fresh substrate holds open grudgingly and snaps back fast; worked material stays open with less resistance and closes slowly, the gradient that once made it dangerous now barely present.

Keeping substrate open and active requires continuous work that counteracts this tendency: either attunement maintaining the high-energy exposed state against the gradient, or Coalition apparatus doing the same job mechanically. Every use of substrate is a negotiation against the material's own physics.

Fully depleted sites — substrate that has lost the field energy that makes it substrate at all — retain their black mineral form but their interior faces go dark and still. The mirror surfaces no longer show anything that isn't there.

A separate and slower process governs whether depleted substrate can recover. The geological conditions that produced the original concentration are still operating — they did not stop when the substrate was removed. What they are now working with is the low-density diffuse residual that remains after extraction, slowly rebuilding concentration from that baseline the same way the original substrate built up from nothing over geological time. A worked-out site begins showing the earliest signs of this rebuilding over generations, not years — the faintest return of detectable concentration in the deepest remaining material. The Coalition's extraction rate so far exceeds this rebuilding rate that recovery is functionally irrelevant in any human timeframe. Whether anyone has kept records long enough to observe it, and what they have done with that knowledge, is [OPEN: story structure decision].

**What the substrate looks like — active state.** In active state, whether through natural field proximity at an intense site or through Order manipulation, movement and color emerge from the interior mirror faces. Not from the surface. Not as an emanation from the material's exterior, but from inside, through the reflective planes — as if the mirror faces are showing something that isn't a reflection of the present room. The color and its intensity are a direct readout of what the field energy is doing and how completely it is responding. See *The Vein — visual language of field manipulation* below.

**What the substrate looks like — refined.** Coalition processing does not produce one thing. It produces a range of grades, each defined by four independent axes: concentration (how much Vein per unit volume), size (the scale and form of substrate elements), shape (the internal geometric coherence of active mirror faces), and matter form (the resting phase state of the substrate before apparatus activation). Each axis has its own development history and can advance independently of the others. A given refined product has a position on all four simultaneously.

The one visual property consistent across all grades is weight anomaly. Every grade of refined substrate is wrong for what it looks like — the field energy stored in the material contributes mass. Raw substrate is denser than its volume suggests. Precision composite grades are lighter than their housing implies but still wrong for the material they appear to be. Weight is the tell that survives all visual disguise.

*Installation bulk grade* is the earliest and most produced form. Dense black granular material, irregular fragments from coarse sand to small gravel scale. Individual grains flash briefly with mirror-face quality when disturbed — the interior face catching light for a moment before settling. In bulk it reads as processed dark ore. An uninformed observer sees nothing unusual. An Order intelligence operative identifies it by weight and the brief mirror flash when the material is moved.

*Vehicle precision grade* is geometrically regular — dark rods or discs with clean edges, mirror faces visible and oriented on the end surfaces. Too regular for ordinary rock. Stored in fitted housings that maintain orientation during transport. To an uninformed observer: precision-cut dark stone. To an Order operative: immediately identifiable by geometric regularity and weight anomaly.

*Personal composite grade* stops looking like substrate entirely. The composite carrier is a dense black ceramic-like component — mirror faces internal, protected by Vein-phase matrix, no exterior visual indication of what it contains. Lighter than it appears. Looks inert. The liquid-phase ampoule that accompanies it at this grade is a sealed vial of dark viscous fluid with anomalous surface tension — it clings and pulls away from its container walls the way mercury does. Neither component is identifiable as substrate without destructive inspection. This is the first grade that defeats casual Order intelligence identification on sight.

The matter form axis — what phase state the substrate rests in before activation — is orthogonal to the visual grades above. It affects activation cost and cycle time at any scale but does not determine what a grade looks like or what scale it enables. Solid-phase substrate at any concentration, size, and shape grade performs more slowly than liquid-phase substrate of equivalent grade on the other three axes. Matter form refinement makes Coalition capability faster and more efficient at any scale; the other three axes determine what scale is reachable in the first place.

## The Vein — states of matter and faction mastery

The field's fundamental operation on matter is to move it along the energy continuum — toward greater energy and freedom of movement, or toward lesser energy and greater constraint. This is the spine of everything both factions can do with the Vein.

**The energy continuum.** States of matter exist on a spectrum determined by how much energy a substance's particles contain and therefore how much freedom of movement they have. From least energetic to most, the sequence runs: Bose-Einstein condensate, where particles have been stripped of almost all thermal energy and collapse into a single shared quantum state; superfluid, a quantum-coherent phase occupying roughly the same energy territory as liquid but with zero internal friction and collective behavior; solid, where particles are locked in a lattice; liquid, where particles can flow past each other; gas, where particles move freely and expand to fill available space; plasma, where particles have enough energy that electrons are torn free from their atoms entirely, producing a charged and intensely energetic state. Beyond plasma, at extremes of energy that neither faction has yet reached in practice, the behavior of matter becomes stranger still — [OPEN: the exotic states beyond plasma are theoretically reachable and will be defined when the Coalition's research frontier is developed].

**The Order's mastery.** An Order practitioner perceives the Vein phenomenon directly through their attunement biology and manipulates it as a skilled sense — they feel it across its full concentration range and push back against it, modulating the energy flowing through both dense substrate and the diffuse low-concentration expression that surrounds and underlies it. This gives them the ability to shift matter along the energy continuum directly. For an average Order practitioner, three states are accessible with consistency and depth: solid, liquid, and gas. Plasma is reachable only by exceptional individuals and those at the top of the Order's hierarchy.

The defining axis of Order hierarchy is not raw power but granularity — the precision, consistency, and resolution of field perception and manipulation. The difference between a novice and a master is not simply that the master can move more energy; it is that the master's coupling to the field is cleaner and more complete. A master working in any color register produces an intense, steady glow — the field responds fully and exactly where asked. A novice produces a faint, sometimes flickering expression — the field is partially answering, sluggishly, with some energy going places it wasn't directed. The master can also modulate deliberately: they can choose to work at low intensity for precision operations that require restraint, something a novice cannot do because restraint requires the control that low coupling prevents. At the top of the hierarchy, practitioners can do things like shift one small region of liquid substrate to gas while leaving the adjacent region untouched, or feel the field gradient across an entire site well enough to map its structure without instruments. The novice reaching for intensity beyond their coupling quality is the source of the accidents and atrocities the Order officially describes as tragedies — the field responds, but not only where asked, and the excess is not recoverable once it has propagated.

The Order's civilizational ceiling at three states is not purely complacency — it is institutionalized caution with deep historical roots. At some point in the Order's past, practitioners pushing into exotic states produced events catastrophic enough to become mythologized: the specific details have been retold until they function as parable rather than history, but the institutional wound they left is real and still shapes behavior. These founding catastrophes are invoked by senior figures in the hierarchy as the reason the ceiling exists — the weight of deep history, the founding lesson, the thing that must never be repeated.

More recently, smaller-scale events have accumulated to reinforce the same lesson: incidents within living institutional memory where practitioners who pushed beyond the established range produced outcomes that were damaging enough, and ambiguous enough in cause, for the institution to file them under confirmation of the ancient rule rather than investigate them on their own terms. These are the incidents enforcement-level figures invoke — more specific, more documentable, sufficient. The institution does not need the mythologized catastrophe to justify the ceiling. It has recent examples. The mythologized catastrophe is for when someone asks whether the ceiling is really necessary.

The taboo is cultural rather than formally codified. There is no written prohibition, no trial mechanism, no formal framework — because establishing one would require formally acknowledging what is being prohibited, which would mean acknowledging the full history, which the institution has never fully done. Enforcement is reputational and hierarchical. Being caught means losing standing, being sidelined, being quietly removed from positions of influence. This makes the taboo effectively unenforceable against anyone who has already decided to operate outside institutional view — and entirely effective against everyone else.

Exceptional practitioners still occasionally push into plasma and sense something beyond it. They are noted, sometimes celebrated, and the knowledge stays with them. What propagates is the fact that it happened, not the how, and usually framed as an accident or an involuntary extremity rather than deliberate exploration. Secret societies and isolated individuals do preserve and practice knowledge of exotic states — not as esoteric hobbyists but as custodians of a more complete account of what the Order has done and what it is capable of. They probably know more about both the mythologized catastrophes and the smaller recent incidents than the institution does, and what they know complicates the institutional account of both. They are not just practicing forbidden techniques. They are keeping a more accurate history, which is its own kind of power.

**The Coalition's mastery.** The Coalition's relationship to the Vein is scientific and external. Without attunement, there is no direct perception of the phenomenon — only its effects, which can be observed, measured, and eventually systematized. The Coalition works with the substrate in its dense form because that is what their instruments can detect, manipulate, and industrialize. Dense substrate is their baseline, their table stakes, the foundation their entire economy and military capability is built on.

But the Coalition's founding identity is the rejection of the claim that non-attuned people have inherent limits. The frontier is not a cultural threat to them; it is the point. Coalition researchers have been working outward from solid toward other states for generations, developing instruments sensitive enough to induce and measure state changes in substrate without attunement. Liquid substrate has been achieved at prototype scale. Gas remains theoretical but actively pursued. Plasma is a distant goal that serious researchers believe is reachable within a generation or two.

Beyond plasma, the Coalition has no institutional ceiling and no taboo. What they lack is the perceptual access that would let an individual feel their way toward exotic states directly. Their path is instrumental and incremental — each step requires building apparatus sensitive enough to detect and induce the next state, which takes time and compounds across generations. The Coalition understands the mechanism of what the Vein does in ways the Order often doesn't, because the Order's knowledge is embodied and practical rather than theoretical. An Order practitioner knows how to shift substrate to liquid the way a skilled craftsman knows how to shape wood — through feel, experience, and transmitted practice. A Coalition researcher knows why it happens.

The race to exotic states as deployable capability is genuinely open. Exceptional Order individuals have almost certainly perceived exotic states already — the secret societies are evidence of this. What the Order cannot do is compound that perception into institutional capability, both because the knowledge is embodied and hard to transmit and because the institution actively discourages the attempt. The Coalition lacks the perceptual shortcut but has no ceiling and compounds incrementally. Which arrives first depends on whether embodied perception in the hands of people operating outside institutional view can outpace systematic instrumental research with full institutional support. Neither faction has fully articulated this race to itself.

**The permanent asymmetry.** The Coalition's capability is always infrastructural — it requires apparatus, supply chains, installation, and support. The Order's capability is always embodied — it lives in the practitioner's biology and moves with them. The Coalition can eventually approximate any capability the Order has, but always at greater size, weight, logistical cost, and deployment time. The Order's advantage is not what they can do but where they can do it and how fast. Neither makes the other obsolete. This asymmetry is not a current gap that development will close — it is a structural consequence of how each faction accesses the Vein. The Coalition excels where scale is the variable. The Order excels where the individual is the variable.

## The Vein — visual language of field manipulation

When substrate is active — held open by attunement or apparatus, with field energy moving through it — the interior mirror faces express color and movement. This visual language carries two simultaneous pieces of information: what the energy is doing, and how completely the field is responding to the manipulation. These are independent axes and must be read together.

**Where the color lives.**

Color is a property of the substrate, not of the field energy itself and not of the practitioner. It appears in the interior mirror faces of active substrate — the fractured or held-open surfaces through which the field energy is expressing. The substrate is always the source. A practitioner working with no substrate present produces no color at all, only the environmental wrongness of the field's effects on ordinary matter: stone cracking without cause, air shimmering with sourceless heat, frost spreading across untouched surfaces. The uncanny effects are present; the visual language is not, because the visual language requires substrate to express through.

**The bleed.**

At low intensity, the color stays inside the substrate. The mirror faces show it; it does not propagate outward. As intensity increases, the color begins to bleed into whatever the field energy is actively affecting — the tissue being healed, the stone being fractured, the air being heated. The bleed is the field asserting itself into matter that isn't substrate, marking that matter with the color of what is being done to it. Green bleeding into a wound means the field is working with the tissue. Red bleeding into the same wound means the field is overdriving it. The color in the affected material is not the same intensity as the color in the substrate — it is proportional to how completely the field energy has taken hold of that material, which is always less than its expression in the substrate itself.

This means the visual language is directional and readable in space. An observer can look at what is showing color and understand not only what register the manipulation is in but what the field is currently doing to that specific thing. The substrate tells you the source and the nature. The bleed tells you the target and the degree of effect.

*Blue* — energy is being removed or contained. The field is drawing inward, cooling, slowing. Blue is the cold arm of manipulation: suppression, containment, the deliberate removal of thermal and biological energy from a target or environment.

*Green* — energy is being added in the beneficial register. The field is working with living systems, accelerating biological processes in ways that stimulate rather than damage. Healing, enhancement, growth. Green is the field and living matter cooperating.

*Red* — energy is being added at the overdriving register. The field has crossed the threshold where stimulation becomes damage — biological systems are being pushed past their regulatory limits from the inside. This is the combat range for most Order practitioners.

*Purple* — energy is being added at the structurally catastrophic register. The field at this intensity does not merely damage living systems — it dissolves the structures that make them what they are. This range is accessible only to Order masters and exceptional individuals. The shift into purple marks the boundary between harm and something closer to unmaking.

*White* — energy expression beyond what color can distinguish. The scale has exceeded the system's ability to express directionality. What is happening at this intensity is not a manipulation in any controlled sense.

*Black (absorbing)* — the cold extreme, distinct from inert stable substrate. At this end of the cold arm, the substrate is not merely dark — it appears to pull light from its surroundings. The space around it seems slightly darker than it should be, colder, as if the material is taking more than its share of what is present. An inert stable substrate is matte black in the way dark rock is matte black. A substrate being driven toward the condensate extreme is black in the way a hole is black. The distinction is perceptible before it is identifiable.

**Intensity — the quality of field coupling.**

Intensity is quantitative within any color register. It tells you how completely the field is responding to the manipulation — not how much energy is theoretically available, but how cleanly the practitioner's coupling is carrying the direction they have asked for.

*Faint* — the field is partially responding. The coupling is incomplete. The manipulation is real but sluggish, the practitioner reaching for something the field is only partially answering. This is the signature of low mastery in a given manipulation type, or of a master working deliberately below full coupling for precision work that requires restraint.

*Intense* — the field is responding at full capacity to exactly what was asked. The coupling is clean and complete. Maximum effect for the practitioner's current capability, with no energy going anywhere unintended.

The dangerous case is a practitioner reaching for high intensity beyond what their coupling quality can cleanly direct. The field responds — but not only where asked. The excess propagates. Where it goes and what it does is not controlled by the practitioner, and the consequences are not predictable from outside. This is the mechanism behind the accidents, the atrocities, and the things both factions officially describe as tragedies.

**Glow stability — control.**

The steadiness of the glow is the third readable element, and it is the most immediately legible indicator of whether a practitioner is in control of what they are doing.

*Steady* — the coupling is clean, the manipulation is directed, the practitioner knows exactly what is happening.

*Flickering or blooming* — the coupling is incomplete or the practitioner is reaching beyond what they can direct. The glow expresses in places it shouldn't. The field is responding but not only where asked. From the outside, this reads as visible wrongness before anything else goes wrong — the warning that precedes the loss of control.

**Reading the system.**

Color, intensity, and stability are always present simultaneously in any active substrate. A steady, faint green glow means careful beneficial manipulation at low coupling — a practitioner doing delicate healing work, or a master deliberately restraining their output for precision. A flickering, blooming red means damaging manipulation at a scale the practitioner cannot fully direct — something has gone wrong, or is about to. An intense, steady purple means a master working at the catastrophic register with complete control — the most dangerous legible state, because the control makes it worse in a specific way: it means someone chose this.

The cold arm's black extreme is the only state that resists this reading system from a distance. It looks like nothing. The wrongness reveals itself through proximity and effect — the air getting darker, colder, the sense of something being taken — before any explicit visual signature declares itself. This is the manipulation that arrives closest before anyone understands what is happening.

The color registers are not arbitrary labels on a continuous spectrum — they are the resolution at which a practitioner can perceive and work. A novice experiences the field in discrete steps: they are in one register and then suddenly in another, with no felt transition and no ability to arrest the shift. A master experiences it as continuous, perceiving the gradient between registers and able to hold position on the slope. This is why novice accidents arrive without warning and why masters working near register boundaries are doing something qualitatively different from what novices do near the same boundaries — one is navigating, the other is falling.

## The Vein — effects on the world

**The governing principle.** The Vein field moves matter along the energy continuum. This is the same operation whether the practitioner is working on Vein substrate directly or pushing energy through the ambient field into ordinary matter. The variety of effects comes from the variety of matter in the world, not from the field doing different things in different contexts. The same underlying operation produces different results on stone, water, metal, air, and living tissue because those materials are different and respond to energy addition and removal according to their own properties.

**Effects on inorganic matter.** Stone and earth respond to energy addition through thermal expansion, surface spalling at high intensity, and eventual liquefaction at the extreme end. Energy removal makes stone contract and embrittle — cold rock fractures under stresses that warm rock would absorb. Metal conducts added energy rapidly through its structure, which means it functions as a natural conduit: a practitioner who understands this can push energy into one end of a metal structure and have it express at the other. Metal stores no field energy — conduction stops when the source is removed, with no residual field charge in the worked metal. Any heat the metal has absorbed during conduction persists as ordinary thermal mass and dissipates normally, but this is the metal staying warm, not the field continuing to operate. Conduction efficiency is governed primarily by geometry rather than distance: a straight run carries energy cleanly at any practical length, while joints, bends, welds, and thin cross-sections are where intensity drops and failure initiates. A complex metal path loses energy at each geometric transition; a simple one loses almost none. This is why the location and character of failure points in apparatus are predictable — they concentrate at geometry changes, not at arbitrary positions along a run. Water is particularly consequential because of its presence everywhere — in soil, in air as humidity, in living things — and because of its dramatic volume change at state transition: liquid water becoming steam expands roughly seventeen hundred times in volume. Drawing energy from water produces ice. A practitioner operating near a river or in humid air has significantly more leverage than one working in dry stone, not because water is a Vein material, but because it is an efficient intermediary for expressing energy effects at scale. Air responds to localized energy addition through pressure differentials and the airflow that results, producing concussive effects at high intensity and creating conditions for combustion if fuel is present.

Crystalline minerals — and quartz in particular — occupy a distinct position in the taxonomy of inorganic field responses. Most materials respond to the field in one direction only: energy is added or removed, and the material responds passively. Quartz and related crystalline structures are bidirectional transducers. Field energy applied to quartz produces a proportional mechanical response — vibration, dimensional change — in the crystal itself. The reciprocal also holds: mechanical stress applied to quartz near active substrate perturbs the field in return. The same structure that receives field input as mechanical output also converts mechanical input back into field perturbation. No other common material does this. All other field interactions are one-way. The effect scales with crystalline order — quartz is the abundant baseline version, present in ordinary geology everywhere; tourmaline is rarer and produces a stronger response; beryl is weaker. The development-period population almost certainly interacted with this property before they had any framework for the Vein: quartz is in common stone, in building materials, in tools — wherever quartz contacted active substrate under mechanical stress, the field was being perturbed. The practical consequences for apparatus are significant: quartz is the sensing and feedback layer. Field energy produces a mechanical output readable as measurement; mechanical input generates a field perturbation readable as signal. It is the only material in the taxonomy that works in both directions, and the only one that can function as both detector and instrument without any separate measurement apparatus.

Environmental effects cascade in ways that are difficult to fully predict or control. A practitioner does not do a thing to one material and stop there — they do a thing to one material, which affects the adjacent material, which affects the next. This cascade behavior is what makes environment-aware use of the Vein genuinely demanding, and what makes the Order's instinctual, embodied knowledge so valuable. A master is not simply applying energy; they are reading a complex system and anticipating how their intervention will propagate through it. The Coalition, working from theory, has catalogued many of these cascades and in some cases understands the mechanism better than the Order does — though they cannot produce the same intensities or precision.

**What energy transfer looks like.** The field itself is invisible. A bystander without attunement has no direct sensory access to it — no perception of the field as field — which means they cannot see the mechanism of Vein use, only its consequences. The exception is indirect: where the field's operation produces a mechanical output in an intermediate material, that output is perceptible by anyone. Quartz vibrating near active substrate is the clearest case — the vibration is real and physically detectable regardless of attunement. This is the basis of the Coalition's entire instrument layer. What non-attuned people lack is not sensation of field-correlated effects but the biological coupling that would let them perceive the field directly, without a transducing material in between. Stone cracks without visible cause. Air shimmers with heat that has no apparent source. Frost spreads across a surface that nothing touched. From the outside, skilled Vein use reads as wrongness: matter behaving as if the rules changed, without any legible explanation for why. This is more uncanny than a visible energy beam, and the Order has understood for generations that this strangeness is part of what makes them feared.

When substrate is present and active, it expresses the field's operation directly through color, intensity, and glow stability — a visual language readable to anyone, attuned or not, that communicates what is happening and how much control the practitioner has over it. This system is described fully in *The Vein — visual language of field manipulation* above.

The Coalition's visual signature is different in kind. Their power looks like industry: the apparatus of extraction, the infrastructure of processing, the physical evidence of substrate being worked at scale. The absence of the uncanny wrongness that characterizes Order manipulation is itself significant. Coalition power looks like work, which is both accurate and, to the Order, somewhat contemptible.

**Effects on the ecology — surroundings and wildlife.** Vein sites are not dead zones or blasted wastelands. They are hyper-alive. The ambient field energy at a site represents a persistent surplus of energy flowing into everything in the vicinity. Over geological time, the ecology around high-concentration sites has adapted to that surplus — plants grow larger and faster, metabolic processes run hotter, animal behavior organizes itself around the site's rhythms. The non-attuned population that was pushed into Vein-dense territories during the pre-conflict era did not find wasteland. They found environments that were strange, intense, and in some ways extraordinarily productive alongside real danger. The Vein was feeding them, ecologically, long before they understood what the Vein was.

Wildlife near Vein sites has evolved over generations of ambient exposure. The ecology is measurably distinct from surrounding land — not in a uniform direction, but in intensity. Everything near a rich site tends toward heightened activity, unusual growth, and behavioral patterns organized around the field's presence. Whether this constitutes genuine biological attunement in some animal species — a sensitivity to the field that goes beyond mere adaptation to its effects — is an open question. The evidence suggests it is possible. Some animals near high-concentration sites exhibit behaviors that are hard to explain without positing some form of direct field sensitivity: navigation patterns that track field gradients rather than terrain, stress responses that anticipate field fluctuations before any physical change is detectable, the kind of herding behavior around extraction activity that looks less like response to noise or disturbance and more like response to the field itself changing. Whether this constitutes attunement in the biological sense established for humans — genuine perceptual coupling to the field — is [OPEN: to be decided when the ecology of Vein sites is developed further. The possibility is confirmed; the extent and mechanism are not yet established].

**Effects on living matter — active manipulation.** When a practitioner actively pushes field energy into or through a living creature rather than the inorganic environment, they are interacting with a system that has its own energy management processes already running. The result depends entirely on intensity and duration. At low intensity, adding energy to a living system stimulates it — biological processes accelerate, healing rates increase, physical capacity heightens. The field is working with the biological system, giving it more of what it already uses. At higher intensity, the biological system's regulatory mechanisms begin to fail — not because the energy is wrong in kind, but because the structures that use energy have maximum operating speeds, and overdriving them past those limits produces damage from the inside. The difference between stimulating a biological system and overdriving it is the line between what the Order can do that is useful and what it can do that is terrible. That line is not always visible in advance, and misjudging it has consequences that cannot be undone.

Blood's role in this dynamic is more complex than other biological fluids because it carries two simultaneous field pathways at once. Water, which constitutes the bulk of blood by volume, responds to the field through the thermal pathway — energy addition warms the fluid, accelerating the biological processes the blood supports, which is the basis of field-assisted healing at low intensity. Iron, carried in hemoglobin throughout the blood, responds to the field as a metal conductor — energy addition at medium intensity begins to affect the hemoglobin's iron directly, which is a qualitatively different interaction from the water pathway and affects the blood's oxygen-carrying function rather than its thermal behavior. These two pathways run simultaneously, but they do not activate at the same intensity. At low intensity the water pathway dominates and the iron pathway is negligible: field medicine works in this register. At medium intensity the iron pathway becomes significant and begins competing with or overriding the water pathway's effects: this is where healing crosses into a specific form of internal damage that presents differently from simple thermal overload. The threshold between the two registers is not dramatic from the outside. It is an intensity distinction that experienced practitioners learn to feel and calibrate, and that novices often do not know exists.

## Attunement and the Vein — the biological relationship

Attunement is a biological sensitivity to the Vein phenomenon across its full concentration range. Attuned people possess physical structures that are coupled to the phenomenon the way the inner ear is coupled to pressure or the eye to light. This is a sense. It operates at concentrations far below the threshold where Coalition instruments can detect anything, which is what makes it irreplaceable and irreplicable. It is instinctual, embodied, and in its trained form capable of extraordinary precision. It is also, like all senses, a source of vulnerability when the signal it is calibrated for becomes overwhelming.

The attunement mechanism has two distinct components that must both be present for the sense to function. The first is physical: skeletal and connective tissue act as the antenna. Bone in living organisms is piezoelectric — it generates and responds to mechanical signals — and in attuned practitioners this property is coupled to the field, producing resonance that the body's structure receives directly. This is not a metaphor for sensitivity. The skeleton is the physical receiving structure. The second component is neurological: specialized coupling structures translate the mechanical resonance from the skeletal antenna into something the practitioner can perceive and act on. Without the neurological translator, the mechanical signal arrives and produces nothing interpretable. Without the skeletal antenna, the neurological structures have nothing to receive. Non-attuned people have the same piezoelectric bone and receive the same mechanical signal from field-dense environments — they simply have no neurological coupling to translate it into perception. The field moves through them in this register as it moves through everything else; they feel nothing, not because nothing is happening in their bodies, but because the translation step is absent.

The Order's mastery is built on this sensory relationship. Their knowledge of the Vein is transmitted through practice and lineage because the knowledge lives in the body — in the trained calibration of the attunement sense, in the accumulated feel of how the field behaves at different sites and intensities, in the physical memory of correct manipulation. This cannot be fully written down and handed to someone. It has to be learned through direct exposure under guidance, refined across years of practice, deepened across generations of accumulated cultural knowledge. It is precisely this that the Coalition cannot replicate, and why the Order's capabilities remain beyond what Coalition instruments can fully account for.

**Overexposure — the mechanism.** The attunement sense is a biological structure calibrated to a certain range of field intensity. At moderate intensities, the field is what the sense was built for — perceivable, workable, productive. At high intensities, the sense is overstimulated faster than it can process or recover, producing first fatigue, then pain, then damage to the coupling structures themselves. This is the same dynamic as any sensory overload: the capacity that makes perception possible becomes the vector for harm when the signal exceeds the range the system was designed for. The capacity that makes attunement useful is the same capacity that makes high field concentration dangerous — the biological structures that couple to the field have a maximum tolerable input range, and territory where field concentration exceeds that range is genuinely hostile to anyone who can perceive it. This is why Coalition territory, historically dense with substrate the Order never extracted, functioned as a biological barrier: not because it lacked the field, but because it had too much of it.

At the material level, overexposure in an attuned practitioner operates through the body's own composition. Blood carries two simultaneous field pathways — water and iron via hemoglobin — that respond at different intensities and produce different kinds of harm. At low intensity the water pathway dominates: thermal effects on the body's fluid, which is the same mechanism field medicine exploits in the healing register. At medium and high intensities the iron pathway activates, with field energy affecting hemoglobin's iron content directly. This is not a thermal effect. It is a metal-conduction effect operating inside the circulatory system, affecting oxygen-carrying function rather than tissue temperature. The two damage mechanisms compound each other and do not respond to the same countermeasures. A practitioner managing their exposure load is not managing a single variable — they are managing two pathways that scale differently with intensity, compound across duration, and interact with their current biological state in ways that experienced practitioners learn through training and cultural transmission, and that novices often discover first through a mistake they did not know they were making.

**Overexposure — the exposure model.** Damage from overexposure is governed by the interaction of three variables: intensity (how strong the field concentration is), duration (how long the exposure lasts), and the individual's current state (whether they are already fatigued or damaged, or at full capacity). These variables do not combine linearly. A brief extreme exposure may be survivable and fully recoverable — the system is overwhelmed but snaps back. A sustained moderate exposure may produce more permanent damage than a short extreme one, because the continuous stimulation prevents recovery and the cumulative load eventually breaks something. Practitioners learn to read their own exposure state the way a diver learns to read depth and bottom time — through training, through cultural transmission, and through the consequences of getting it wrong.

Recoverable damage is the default outcome when the exposure curve stays below the threshold where coupling structures are physically altered. The biological system needs time to quiet and repair, but it does repair. Permanent damage occurs when intensity and duration combine to cross the threshold where the structures themselves are changed in ways that do not fully heal. Because the attunement mechanism has two distinct components, permanent damage has two distinct expressions that can occur independently. Neurological damage — damage to the translating structures — produces loss of granularity first: the fine discrimination that defines mastery degrades before gross perception, because the most sensitive part of the system is the most vulnerable. A master who survives catastrophic neurological overexposure may retain broad field perception and gross manipulation while losing the precision that defined their rank. Structural damage — damage to the skeletal and connective tissue antenna — is a separate and additional pathway. A practitioner whose physical resonance structures are damaged is impaired even if their neurological coupling remains intact: the signal arrives at the translator degraded, partial, or distorted. The two damage types are clinically distinguishable to practitioners with sufficient experience, but the distinction is not always obvious from the outside, and recovery from each follows different timescales and trajectories. Whether a given practitioner can partially recover through long rehabilitation, or whether specific losses are fixed, is not uniform — it depends on the nature and severity of the damage and is best decided case by case as the story requires.

**Desensitization across generations.** The biological tolerance for high-intensity field exposure has increased across generations of Order practitioners, through a combination of selection pressure, cultural exposure management practices, and the gradual depletion of the most dangerous sites. This desensitization has a cost that the Order has not fully reckoned with: a less sensitive instrument is a coarser instrument. The Order of the current era can operate in territories its ancestors had to cede, but its finest practitioners may be developing mastery that is broader in range and shallower in precision than their equivalents of two hundred years ago. The Order's mastery has gotten broader and shallower at the same time. This is masked by the fact that the sites themselves have also become less intense, so the practical consequences are not immediately obvious — but it means the Order may be slowly losing something it does not know it is losing.

**Why unattuned people are not affected the same way.** Non-attuned people lack the biological coupling structures that make attunement possible. The field passes through them the way radio waves pass through a body — the waves are real and carry energy, but the body has no antenna for them and nothing happens at ordinary intensities. At extreme intensities, the field's effects on ordinary matter would apply to non-attuned bodies as they apply to any biological tissue — but this threshold is far higher than the threshold at which attuned people experience harm, because attuned harm comes through the coupling sense, not through the field's general effects on tissue.

This is also why attunement is not a binary. It is a spectrum of coupling strength. Someone with weak latent attunement might experience unease, disturbed sleep, or strange perceptual experiences near high-concentration sites without understanding why — a partial antenna, receiving noise rather than signal. The physical mechanism is now clearer: the skeletal antenna is present and functioning in the same way as in fully attuned practitioners, but the neurological coupling structures that would translate the mechanical signal are underdeveloped, absent, or dormant. The signal arrives. The translation does not follow. This accounts for why latent attunement can be triggered or activated by proximity to strong field concentrations: at sufficient intensity, the mechanical input through the skeletal antenna becomes strong enough to produce some response even through partial or incomplete neurological coupling — a threshold crossed by force rather than by trained sensitivity. The Coalition's territories almost certainly contain such people, living near old extraction zones with no framework for what they occasionally feel.

## How attunement works — the three phases

Attunement has a history of its own, and that history is as contested as the history of the war. Critically, both factions have not merely interpreted this history differently — they have actively constructed different histories that serve their current ideological needs. The Order has erased the non-attuned people who contributed to its early development, because acknowledging them would complicate the narrative that attunement is the natural basis of greatness. The Coalition has done the mirror version, rewriting or erasing attuned people who contributed to the liberation and the early scientific breakthroughs, because acknowledging them would complicate the founding identity of a non-attuned civilization that succeeded on its own terms. Historical truth in this world is not merely contested — it has been actively buried by both sides. But the two burials are not the same kind. The Order's erasure is centuries old, performed so gradually and so completely that there is no alternative record left to suppress — only absence, and the keepers of a more complete account who preserved it outside institutional view. The Coalition's erasure is far more recent, performed in the generations immediately after the liberation on documents that already existed, by people who understood what they were doing and why. Those people are gone. What they left behind is inconsistency: a documentary record that has been worked on, and therefore shows the signs of having been worked on. The Order's buried history requires a keeper. The Coalition's requires only a careful reader.

**Phase one: the random emergence.** In the earliest history of the world, attunement appeared rarely and unpredictably. The Order's official history remembers this period as the age of its founding figures — exceptional, hyper-attuned individuals who shaped early civilization and whose greatness validated everything the Order would later become. The non-attuned people who played meaningful roles in this period are not suppressed in the way an anxious institution suppresses a recent inconvenience. They are simply absent. The erasure happened across generations during the bloodline consolidation phase, as attunement was being institutionalized as the axis of hierarchy — not as a response to any specific threat, but as the ambient consequence of a civilization recording its history in the only terms it considered meaningful. By the time the consolidation was complete, there was no alternative record to destroy. What survives is not in the Order's archives. It is in the secret societies and isolated lineages that kept a more complete account precisely because they were operating outside institutional view — not as esoteric collectors, but as people who understood that the official history was a construction, and that knowing what it had constructed over mattered. The Coalition has no official history of this period at all, because its founding identity begins with the liberation — but the keepers of the Order's buried history hold figures from this era who would complicate the Order's narrative: attuned people who chose differently, non-attuned people whose contributions the Order's records simply stopped mentioning.

**Phase two: the bloodline consolidation.** Over generations, powerful attuned families used their position to consolidate attunement within their lineages — through strategic marriage, the rewriting of genealogical records, and the institutional exclusion of those who couldn't demonstrate the right ancestry. Attunement became officially hereditary. The biological reality underpinning this was probabilistic rather than absolute: both components of the attunement mechanism — the skeletal antenna and the neurological coupling structures — show strong heritable tendency without strict inheritance, operating more like height or skin tone than blood type. Attuned parentage raised the probability of attuned offspring substantially enough that controlling lineages really did concentrate attunement expression across generations. It did not guarantee it, which is why genealogical rewriting was necessary — to absorb the failures the biology produced and attribute them to impure ancestry rather than to the mechanism's inherent variability. This is the phase that hardened the Order's social hierarchy into its current form and produced the conditions for the liberation: a society where position was determined by birth, where the non-attuned majority was systematically marginalized, and where the people at the bottom had been there long enough to stop believing it was natural.

**Phase three: the latent truth.** Attunement is not as strictly hereditary as the bloodline phase claimed. It is latent in a broader population — meaning spontaneous attunement emergence occurs in people with no documented attuned ancestry, at a rate that has not been constant across history. The phenomenon is real and currently ongoing. Its institutional and public recognition is not.

In the early history of the world the rate was negligible: rare enough to be genuinely exceptional, the kind of occurrence remembered as a singular figure rather than a pattern. The bloodline consolidation made sense as policy precisely because spontaneous emergence was rare enough that controlling lineages really did control attunement, for all practical purposes. The Order was not suppressing a flood. It was maintaining a near-monopoly that the biology mostly supported.

That rate has been climbing across centuries — slowly, almost imperceptibly on a generational timescale, but measurably across the long view. The causes are not fully understood by anyone and may not be fully isolable — the gradual spread of latent biological material through a population that was never as cleanly separated as the Order's records claimed, the long-term effects of generations living adjacent to Vein field energy, or simply the expression of something that was always latent at low probability. There is no single cause to find. The world is simply changing at a rate slower than anyone was tracking. The current era sits at approximately one percent spontaneous emergence in populations without documented attuned ancestry — still rare enough to be individually manageable, common enough to be a structural fact that every institution has a policy for.

For the Order, spontaneous emergence in unexpected lineages is managed through institutional absorption: someone manifesting attunement from an undocumented background gets explained genealogically if possible, or quietly absorbed into the lower tiers of the hierarchy if not. The records have been rewritten before. At one percent this is uncomfortable but containable.

For the Coalition the problem is structurally different and more acute. The Coalition's founding identity requires that attunement and Coalition membership are — if not mutually exclusive — at least categorically distinct. The current operational response is effectively geographic: discovered attuned individuals in the Coalition's general population are pushed toward the territorial margins, the outskirts where Vein field density is lowest and their capacity is most suppressed. Some go voluntarily before discovery, having learned to read the signs of their own emergence and understood what disclosure means. The undiscovered ones live inside Coalition society carrying something they cannot acknowledge. At negligible historical rates the geography solution worked. At one percent and climbing the margins are filling up — the outskirts communities of exiled and self-exiled attuned Coalition members are large enough now to have their own internal culture and their own grievances. They are not organized. Not yet.

The historically relevant ones — attuned Coalition members who contributed to the liberation, to the early scientific breakthroughs, to military operations — are not in the outskirts. They are absent from the record entirely. Their contributions remain; their biology has been rewritten or omitted. For the Order, acknowledging latent emergence would mean the hierarchy is built partly on a biological fiction. For the Coalition, acknowledging it means that some of its most celebrated founding figures may have been attuned, and that the Coalition has been doing the same thing to its own history that it accused the Order of doing. The truth isn't unknown so much as suppressed: someone, somewhere, kept records that the official histories buried.

The signal for how widespread latent emergence actually is exists in Coalition administrative records — not as a direct count of attuned individuals, but as a pattern in population movement. Attuned people move away from Vein-intense zones as their attunement wakes up, the biological barrier making high-concentration territories increasingly uncomfortable before any formal discovery occurs. This produces detectable migration patterns in regional and local demographic records. The attunement connection is not labelled in those records; the administrators tracking the movements see population flows and assign economic or environmental explanations. Assembly would require cross-referencing migration data against Vein concentration maps and making the interpretive leap that movement away from high-concentration zones is an attunement signal — a cross-disciplinary analytical act that nobody has yet performed. The data is distributed across multiple administrative tiers with no single owner and no one with both the access and the framework to connect it. A secondary layer of inertia reinforces this: a formal finding that attunement is broadly distributed would destabilize the Coalition government by undermining the founding purists and the non-attuned identity that holds the current political coalition together. The Order has no equivalent picture — their framework attributes attunement to specific bloodlines, and their myth structure renders contrary evidence invisible before it can be named. The revelation path is investigative rather than a discovery from scratch: the evidence exists, but reaching it requires someone with the right analytical framework, access across multiple data sets, and the disposition to ask a question the institutions around them have every reason not to ask.

## The two factions — overview

The factions do not have final names yet [OPEN: name them when their cultures have more shape]. For now they are called the **Attuned Order** and the **Forge Coalition** as working placeholders only.

### The Attuned Order

A civilization organized around attunement as the primary axis of social belonging and hierarchy. To be attuned is to have status, institutional access, and cultural identity. To lack attunement within the Order is to be marginal — present in the society but excluded from its most important institutions, unable to advance past certain ceilings regardless of talent or effort.

The Order controls fewer Vein sites than the Coalition but uses them more efficiently. Their relationship to the Vein is instinctual and embodied — knowledge is transmitted through practice and lineage, refined across generations of direct contact, and produces capabilities that no Coalition instrument has successfully replicated. The Order can do things with the Vein that the Coalition's scientific framework cannot account for, because it cannot measure what an attuned person perceives from the inside.

**The power structure.** Formal authority in the Order has always resided with a shifting coalition of founding families — the old attuned lineages whose position predates any individual's rank and whose power base is land, alliance, and accumulated political influence rather than attunement capacity alone. Not every founding family holds influence at any given time; the composition of the dominant coalition shifts with political maneuvering, which means there is always a group of founding families currently outside power with an interest in the current coalition failing. Dissent in the Order is often indistinguishable from positioning. A family arguing against the current war strategy may genuinely believe what it is saying, or it may be waiting for the coalition to fracture. There is usually no way to tell from outside, and sometimes no way to tell from inside either.

Alongside political authority, and in permanent entanglement with it, sits the Order's religious arm — the institution responsible for sacralizing the Vein and maintaining the doctrinal framework that legitimates the entire social hierarchy. The founding families created the religious arm deliberately, granting doctrinal authority to figures outside the great lineages precisely because an institution that appeared independent of family interest was more effective at managing the broader population than one that was visibly a family instrument. This was a rational act of governance. Its consequence, which the families did not fully anticipate, is that belief became real. The families modeled the religious arm on the same logic that makes the secret societies stable — provide something people need, control access to it, and the dependency holds. What they did not account for is that religious legitimacy derives from believers, not from patrons. The two instruments run on completely different dependency structures, and by the time the difference became clear, the religious arm had already become something the families could influence but no longer direct. Once the general population genuinely held the faith rather than simply being administered it, the religious arm acquired something the families could not manufacture themselves: legitimacy that flows from conviction rather than force. The relationship shifted from instrument to quid pro quo — the families provide resources and protection, the religious arm provides doctrinal cover and mass loyalty, and neither can afford to break the arrangement openly. The religious arm recruits across all social strata, with lineage carrying no formal weight; advancement within it runs on a combination of cunning, doctrinal mastery, and the kind of zealotism that reads as genuine to other believers. This makes it the one institution in Order society where a person of no bloodline distinction can accumulate real power — which is partly why it attracts the most capable and driven people who find every other avenue closed to them, and partly why its senior figures are not a population the founding families can easily manage.

The military has historically occupied the least autonomous position of the three. The Order has rarely needed serious sustained military capacity — conflicts with the Coalition since the liberation have followed a recognizable pattern of limited engagement and geographic resolution, never requiring full mobilization. A military that does not fight at scale does not accumulate the institutional prestige that would let it challenge political authority, and the founding families have reinforced this by placing family members in senior command positions as a matter of course. The military's formal leadership is therefore split between commanders who hold their positions through family connection and commanders who rose through demonstrated competence, and these two groups share rank without sharing authority or outlook. In practice, military command operates with significant field autonomy — not as a grant of deliberative standing but as a consequence of the political structure's slowness. Commanders who need decisions faster than the family consensus mechanism can produce them act, commit forces, and seek ratification after the fact. The families tolerate this because the alternative is paralysis. The fiction maintained by both sides is that the commanders are always executing family intent rather than substituting their own judgment. That fiction holds until a commander does something consequential enough that it cannot be absorbed.

The secret societies that operate beneath all three formal branches have no official existence and no formal power. What they have is capability — drawn from the Order's internal underclass, the high-born whose incapacity is worth concealing and the low-born whose unusual capacity the institution has no framework to reward — and the accumulated leverage that comes from being the instrument through which the formal structure handles what it cannot handle officially. They are used by the founding families to monitor and manage the other branches: watching which military commanders are building loyalty structures that run to themselves rather than to the families, keeping pressure on religious figures whose independence has grown inconvenient, handling the things that cannot be resolved through any visible process. The distinction that matters to them is not whether a commander acts without waiting for orders — the families have made their peace with that — but whether a commander is converting operational success into something that outlasts the operation: personal loyalty structures, political positioning, a reputation that runs to themselves rather than to the family whose insignia they wear. Field autonomy in service of the mission is tolerated. Field autonomy in service of the self is what they are watching for. Their interventions scale to what the situation requires — administrative friction for a commander accumulating the wrong kind of reputation, louder and more violent when something needs to disappear rather than be redirected. In both cases the mechanism is the same: the action is legible as something else. A reassignment reads as routine. A death reads as battlefield attrition. The noise of war is a particularly useful cover, and they are not above generating the noise they need. The societies choose not to convert that power into formal authority — not because they lack the capability but because visibility would end them. As long as they appear to be a tool of the founding families, they are protected by the families' interest in keeping useful tools. The moment they appear to be a rival power center, they become a target. They are choosing the long game, and the arrangement is stable because every member capable of breaking it understands exactly why not to.

**The Order's internal underclass** — people born into documented attuned lineages whose own attunement is weak, absent, or insufficient by the hierarchy's standards — is a small cohort, never large enough to exert collective pressure, but structurally irresolvable. They contradict both axes of the Order's legitimating logic simultaneously: the bloodline says they should have capacity; the biology says they don't. Expelling them would require acknowledging that the bloodline logic is imperfect. Fully absorbing them would require admitting a two-tier system the hierarchy officially doesn't recognize. So they persist in permanent ambiguous status — present, lineage-legitimate, and unable to do the thing that legitimacy is supposed to confer. How they relate to that position depends almost entirely on how it was managed: the high-born whose incapacity was quietly handled, their family shielded from institutional scrutiny through connection or intervention, tend toward a complicated and compromised loyalty — aware that their position depends on a fiction being maintained, embedded enough in Order structures to find Coalition ideology genuinely foreign. The low-born in the same position, without the connections to manage it cleanly, encounter the full weight of the hierarchy's ambiguity and are more likely to arrive at bitterness — though even then, the Order's ideology is effective at framing incapacity as personal failure rather than systemic injustice, which means Coalition sympathy is not automatic. The secret societies draw quietly from both ends of this group: high-born individuals of notable lineage whose incapacity is worth concealing for the family's sake, and rare low-born individuals who exhibit unusual sensitivity or talent that the institution has no framework to reward. For the former the societies provide protection, at a cost in loyalty and dependency that can never be repaid through official channels. For the latter they provide the only path upward that exists outside the hierarchy's terms. Neither group is numerous. Both matter beyond their numbers.

**The institutional ceiling and its cultural weight.** The prohibition on pushing beyond established states of matter in Vein manipulation — the ceiling that the Order has maintained since its founding catastrophe — is carried formally by the religious arm, which mythologizes it as sacred doctrine and invokes the catastrophe history as the reason no practitioner should reach further than the institution sanctions. But the ceiling has been transmitted long enough and completely enough that it no longer requires active religious maintenance to hold. It has become ambient — absorbed into the general assumptions Order practitioners carry without knowing where those assumptions came from. A practitioner who explicitly rejects the religious framing still feels instinctive unease when approaching that boundary, and experiences that unease as their own prudence rather than received instruction. The ceiling has colonized the vocabulary of secular reasoning. Overriding it requires not just rejecting the doctrine but recognizing and overriding a reaction that does not announce itself as religious — which is a much harder and lonelier thing to do, and which is precisely why the taboo remains effective against people who have consciously decided they don't believe it.

The ceiling has held not through active policing but through a combination of mechanisms that cover different populations. The genuinely talented who push far enough are noticed — by the secret societies if no one else — and either absorbed or quietly removed before what they've found can propagate. The merely ambitious without guidance encounter the real difficulty of the thing and often produce the kind of accident the institution then uses as a contemporary example, more useful than the mythologized catastrophe because it is recent and documentable. The cultural transmission handles the rest: most practitioners never push hard enough against the ambient unease to discover it is learned rather than innate.

**How the Order fails to see what it needs to see.** The Order's institutional blindness to the current strategic situation is not a choice any of its senior figures consciously made. It is produced by the ordinary operation of the institution's own incentive structures. A practitioner who notices the operational boundary shifting — who can work a site they could not work two years ago, who encounters Coalition presence in territory that was safely interior before — faces a choice about how to frame that observation when reporting it upward. Framing it as a minor fluctuation is safe: it confirms the existing picture, requires no action, and does not imply that the people who set current strategy made a mistake. Framing it as evidence of a structural shift is risky: it implies the current approach is inadequate, which is an accusation that travels upward and lands on people with power over the observer's future. So the information is softened at each layer of the reporting structure — not through conspiracy, but through the ordinary operation of self-interest in a hierarchy where delivering uncomfortable conclusions has reliable costs and uncertain rewards. By the time anything reaches the founding families, the pattern that would be visible in the aggregate has been disaggregated into a collection of minor fluctuations that individually do not demand attention.

The complacency mechanism finishes what the reporting structure starts. If an observation survives the softening and arrives at the senior level in a form that still carries urgency, the institution has a ready-made response that does not require engaging with the substance: this is the argument of someone who has not absorbed the founding lesson, who is reaching for escalation before restraint has been exhausted. That response is available to any senior figure with enough cultural standing to invoke it, and invoking it is costless because the founding catastrophe doctrine — carried by the religious arm, absorbed into ambient culture — arrives pre-legitimated. It does not need to be argued for. The institution's deepest wisdom and its structural incentives are aligned in the same direction, which means the Order is not merely choosing to ignore what it is being told. It has built a system that ensures the telling never fully reaches it.

The people with the clearest picture of the actual strategic situation are the risen military commanders, who are close enough to the operational reality to see the pattern the reporting structure is disaggregating, and the secret societies, whose cross-cutting visibility gives them access to information that never enters the formal reporting chain at all. Neither has deliberative standing in the institutions that set Order policy. Whether that changes before the situation becomes undeniable — and what form that change takes — is where the current war's internal drama lives.

**The founding figures and the myth structure.** The Order's founding figures have been mythologized in person, deed, and as the attributed source of all institutionally-sanctioned knowledge. The myth is not a passive accumulation — it is a selective filter. New discoveries are retroactively attributed to the founders only when the Order chooses to legitimize and institutionalize them; knowledge that remains in the secret societies, that contradicts existing doctrine, or that the founding families decline to sanction never enters the myth at all. The result is a knowledge base that is simultaneously inflated and incomplete: the founders grow in stature with each generation as newly legitimized discoveries are absorbed into their legend, while the actual accumulated understanding of the Order's practitioners and secret societies exceeds the myth without any mechanism to acknowledge that fact. The secret societies hold what the myth omits — unsanctioned knowledge including material that would erode the founding account if surfaced. The gap between what the societies hold and what the myth claims is itself a map of the institutional choices the Order has made across generations. The religious arm may hold founding-era records as a consequence of its fracture from the founding families — a negotiated acquisition whose content and completeness reflect the terms of that separation rather than a complete archive. No single keeper maintains this distortion, and no conspiracy sustains it; it is the ordinary product of an institution that attributes legitimacy to origin rather than discovery, operating across enough time for the accumulation to become invisible.

### The Forge Coalition

A civilization that began as a displaced and marginalized non-attuned population and developed technology out of necessity. The Coalition's founding logic — that what the Order calls natural hierarchy is a constructed one, and that the Vein belongs to no one by birthright — is the ideological residue of a liberation movement now three or four generations old. The people alive today inherited both the victory and the ideology; most of them relate to the founding conflict the way people relate to history they were taught rather than history they lived.

The Coalition controls the majority of Vein sites, occupying territories the Order historically ceded because high Vein concentrations were biologically hostile to attuned people. Their relationship to the Vein is systematic and external — scientific rather than instinctual, documented rather than transmitted through practice. They extract from sites the Order never fully developed, and they do so at scale. The cost is that their extraction is faster and less refined, and removes the substrate that sustains the underlying field, which makes their contribution to the long-term depletion problem structurally more severe than the Order's.

The Coalition's most celebrated founding figures are the leaders of the liberation and the scientists and inventors who made it possible. The attuned people who contributed to both — defectors from the Order, Coalition-born individuals who manifested attunement and chose to stay and serve — have been quietly rewritten out of the official record over generations, rendered non-attuned in the histories, or simply omitted. The erasure was performed in the generations immediately following the liberation, when the founding ideology was still being consolidated and the people doing the rewriting understood what they were doing and why. Those people are gone. What they left behind is a documentary record that has been worked on — technical accounts whose attributions don't match the biographies attached to them, commemorative histories that contradict earlier operational records in specific and non-random ways, figures who appear in founding-era documents and quietly vanish from later ones. The Coalition's buried history is not destroyed. It is inconsistent, and inconsistency is findable by anyone looking carefully enough. No institution has assembled the fragments — the primary barrier is inertia and the wrong questions not being asked, with a secondary layer of political disincentive: a finding that attuned people built the Coalition would destabilize the founding identity the current government depends on. What exists alongside this passive neglect is a layer of individual protection. People connected to celebrated founding figures or powerful families quietly manage specific pieces of the history that touch them — suppressing a document here, redirecting an inquiry there — independently and without coordination. The suppression is therefore uneven: some fragments are actively guarded, others sit in untouched archives. The mechanism parallels the Order's relationship to its own inconvenient history: ambient distortion sustained by individual interest rather than conspiracy. The Order holds corroborating records from the liberation period — military intelligence documenting attuned Coalition operatives as enemy combatants — without recognizing it as Coalition buried history or knowing the Coalition erased the same people from its own record. Post-liberation, the Order has minimal visibility into Coalition internal life; what they hold is a snapshot that ends at the liberation and does not update. Cross-referencing Order and Coalition archives would be definitive, but that requires cross-faction access that is its own problem.

**The power structure.** Formal authority in the Coalition is distributed across three tiers of elected government — federal, regional, and local — bound together by a constitution that predates and supersedes all of them. The constitution is the Coalition's founding act, drafted in the immediate aftermath of the liberation by the revolutionary leadership in negotiation with the populations already established in Vein-dense territories. It encodes what the Coalition fought for in terms specific enough to constrain future majorities and general enough to have survived three generations of political argument about what it means. Both the constraining and the arguing are still ongoing.

The families and lineages that trace direct descent from the liberation's leadership — revolutionary commanders, founding scientists, indigenous territorial leaders — carry cultural weight that has not fully converted into formal political power and has not fully dissipated either. A small number of dynasties have maintained recognizable political presence across generations, their names functioning as shorthand for particular values or factions rather than as claims to hereditary authority. Most Coalition citizens relate to these figures the way people relate to faces on currency — present, familiar, and not especially relevant to daily political life. The exceptions are the hardliners on both founding sides who maintain the distinction deliberately, invoking revolutionary or indigenous origin as a legitimating credential when it is useful and as an accusation when it is not. They are a minority. They are loud enough to matter in close votes.

Government operates across three tiers with distinct domains and a clear hierarchy of authority. The federal level sets constitutional direction, manages the war, and makes decisions whose consequences cross regional boundaries — trade, treaty, military coordination, the constitutional court. Regional governments administer everything within their borders that the federal level has not claimed, which in practice is most of what affects daily life: infrastructure, local economies, civil order, the management of Vein sites within their territory. City and village governments handle the immediate — services, local disputes, the granular texture of how people actually live. The principle is that authority should sit at the lowest level capable of handling it, with higher tiers intervening only when the scale of a decision exceeds what the tier below can contain. Movement between tiers flows upward only through election at the lower level first — no one reaches regional office without having held local office, and no one reaches federal office without having held regional office. The exceptions are rare and constitutionally defined: specific appointed positions where technical expertise is judged more important than electoral experience, subject to confirmation by the elected body they serve.

Each tier of government maintains its own military force, sized for the scale of threats that tier is expected to manage. Local forces handle civil order and site security. Regional forces handle territorial defense and can be called on by the federal level when the situation exceeds local capacity. The federal military handles the war and coordinates across regions when operations require it. In sustained conflict the federal military absorbs regional forces into joint operations, and that absorption is where the friction lives. Regionally raised forces carry the political culture of the governments that built them. Two regional armies that represent constituencies with sufficiently different views on the war's prosecution, its costs, or its aims can be formally coordinated while being practically difficult to deploy alongside each other. This has not yet produced open insubordination. It has produced enough friction in joint operations that federal commanders have learned to treat force composition as a political variable, not just a logistical one.

The body responsible for resolving constitutional disputes was not part of the original constitutional design. It was created after the distributed interpretation system produced contradictions intractable enough that the political cost of leaving them unresolved exceeded the political cost of creating a dedicated institution. Its early decisions established precedent from scratch, written entirely by appointees of whoever held power at the moment of its creation, and that founding layer of precedent still shapes how the document is read. The current composition is half appointed by the elected federal government and half elected directly by the general population. The appointed half answers to the coalition that confirmed them. The elected half has campaigned on constitutional positions, which means constitutional interpretation has become a public political question in a way it wasn't when the body was entirely insulated from popular pressure. Appointments to it are among the most contested political acts in the Coalition. The Coalition builds institutions the way it builds everything else — when the problem becomes undeniable, not before. The court is the clearest example: a fix for consequences the founders did not anticipate, now old enough to have developed its own interests, its own precedent layer, and its own account of why it was always necessary.

**The political landscape.** Coalition politics organizes itself around four live tensions: what to do with attuned people emerging within the general population, how aggressively to prosecute the war against the Order, how fast to push the scientific research frontier and at what acceptable risk, and who bears the costs of industrial extraction. These tensions do not map cleanly onto a single axis — a politician's position on one does not reliably predict their position on the others — but they cluster into recognizable tendencies that form, break, and re-form as coalitions depending on circumstance. The Coalition represents more of its range of views during peacetime. During conflict it consolidates around whatever the dominant tendency can hold together.

**The current government.** The Coalition enters the current war with a government whose agenda is set by its technocrat tendency — the inheritors of the founding scientific identity, who believe that non-attuned capability through systematic research and industrial development is both the Coalition's defining achievement and its path to winning. Their position is that the war is a problem of applied capability: build the right instruments, push the research frontier fast enough, and the Order's individual superiority becomes irrelevant the way it became irrelevant during the liberation. They are dominant enough to set the agenda. They are not dominant enough to pass anything significant alone.

Their coalition partners are the isolationists, who want the war ended through negotiation before its costs accumulate past the point of recovery, and the founding purists, who want it won but won the original way — through apparatus and doctrine, without the compromises that fast science or attuned integration would require. These two tendencies do not agree with each other on much beyond their shared unease with the technocrat agenda's direction and pace. What they can do together, when the war goes badly enough or the technocrat program produces something alarming enough, is block. And if they can draw enough of the underrepresented tendencies into temporary alignment — the workers bearing extraction costs, the regional governments whose forces are being absorbed into federal operations, the indigenous conservatives watching industry expand toward their territories — they can redirect.

Growing underneath the current government is the expansionist tendency, fed by each incremental advance in Coalition science and military capability. Where the technocrats see growing capability as leverage for a favorable settlement, the expansionists see it as the argument for finishing the war decisively. The technocrat leadership has so far restrained that pressure, mindful of its coalition partners. That restraint becomes harder to maintain with each Coalition military success. The industry giants operate across all tendencies as a lobby rather than a faction — they have no ideological position, only an interest. That interest is the protection of existing industrial infrastructure from disruption, which means they back whoever is governing and useful while quietly working against any research program that would obsolete what they have already built. In the current government this produces a specific contradiction: they fund the Technocrats because Technocrat industrial expansion means contracts, and they undermine Technocrat science because Technocrat science is the thing most likely to make their infrastructure obsolete. They are the dominant government's most reliable financial supporters and its most consistent internal saboteurs simultaneously.

War policy divides the Coalition on strategy. Constitutional interpretation divides it on process. The attuned question divides it on identity — on what the Coalition actually is, what it was founded to be, and whether those two things are still the same. That is why it is the deepest fracture, not the loudest one.

**The Coalition's deepest ongoing internal fracture** is the management of attuned people born within its current population. This is not a historical problem the Coalition solved — it is a structural necessity that has gotten harder as spontaneous emergence rates have climbed. Discovered attuned individuals are pushed to the territorial margins, toward outskirts communities where Vein field density is lowest. Undiscovered ones remain inside Coalition society carrying something they cannot disclose. Historically significant attuned Coalition members — those who contributed to the liberation and the early scientific breakthroughs — are not in the outskirts. They are simply gone from the record. The accumulated weight of these responses, across generations, is one of the Coalition's unresolved moral debts. And the outskirts communities that have grown up from generations of this policy are no longer a minor footnote — they are a population with their own culture, their own relationship to the Vein, and their own account of what the Coalition owes them.

**What the Coalition's structure costs the people inside it.** The Coalition's internal drama does not live in its institutions. It lives in what the institutions cost the people inside them.

Authority in the Coalition is formally democratic but practically filtered through perceived competence. A regional commander earns operational latitude by demonstrating results; a federal official earns trust from above by managing their constituency without creating problems that escalate. The mandate from the people is real — elections happen, votes count, representatives answer for their decisions — but what gets decided before the vote, who gets to frame the question, and whose competence is visible to whom are all functions of position and information control. As communications technology has made deliberation more public, this filtering has become harder to hide and easier to exploit. The person who controls the narrative of what competence looks like in a given situation has more power than the person who is actually competent. Both of them know it.

The constitutional court's stated mandate is interpretation independent of political interest. Nobody inside it fully believes this is achievable, and the structure ensures it isn't. The appointed half owes their position to whoever held federal power at the moment of their confirmation — their independence is real only to the extent that their appointers' interests and the constitution's text happen to align. The elected half campaigned on constitutional positions, which means they arrived with commitments already made to a constituency that will hold them to those commitments. What both halves actually want, underneath the mandate, is for the document to mean what they need it to mean — and each half has a different reason for needing different things. The tension between them is not ideological disagreement. It is two different kinds of captured independence pretending to be the same kind of principled interpretation.

The Coalition's military doctrine is an extension of its political landscape — how the war should be fought is not a purely tactical question, it is an ideological one, and a commander's methods are legible as a political position whether they intend them to be or not. A commander who wins through unconventional means that contradict the dominant tendency's philosophy is a political problem even if they are a military asset. A commander whose orthodox approach fails is easier to absorb institutionally than one whose unorthodox approach succeeds, because success through the wrong methods implies the wrong methods work — which is an argument the current government may not want made. This tension compounds when federal operations absorb regional forces. A regional army is built by a regional government with a specific political culture — its commanders were promoted, its doctrine was shaped, and its soldiers were recruited inside that culture. Placing it alongside a force built by a government with opposing views on the war's prosecution does not produce a unified army. It produces two armies sharing a chain of command, each watching the other's methods and reporting home. The hero and the zero of any given campaign are not determined solely by results. They are determined by whose results are convenient, whose methods are defensible, and who has the political relationships to survive the interpretation of what they did and why. A Coalition soldier who wins the wrong way can find themselves more isolated in victory than they would have been in defeat.

The Coalition's military also contains a fault line that runs beneath rank and doctrine: the difference between soldiers who joined for ideological reasons and those who joined for material ones. The ideological soldier needs the war to mean something — a campaign that contradicts the values they enlisted for is a personal crisis, not just an order to follow. The mercenary soldier, whether motivated by money, prestige, or escape from circumstances with no better exit, needs the war to continue producing the conditions that make their position worth having. A negotiated settlement is a threat to one and a relief to the other. Both climb ranks through the same mechanisms, arrive at the same command positions, and then discover that they have been fighting different wars the entire time.

The industry giants do not govern. They do not need to. Their influence operates at the level beneath governance — which research programs receive funding, which proposals stall in review, which scientists find their work suddenly difficult to publish or their positions quietly defunded. In the open, this is lobbying: legal, documented, a recognized feature of Coalition political life that everyone understands and nobody fully controls. In the dark, it is something closer to sabotage: obstacles that cannot be removed through legitimate means get removed through other means, and the people doing the removing maintain enough distance from their employers that the connection is plausible to deny. The operative working inside the technocrat government who is quietly working against the technocrat agenda is not a traitor in their own mind. They are protecting something that existed before the current government and will outlast it. That self-understanding does not make them less dangerous. It makes them harder to find, because people who believe they are right do not behave like people who know they are wrong.

### Population demographics — attuned distribution across time

The demographic composition of each faction has shifted significantly since the liberation, and the current numbers do not resemble the pre-liberation baseline.

**Pre-liberation Order.** Before the liberation, the Order's full population ran approximately 30% attuned to 70% non-attuned. This was classical minority rule: an attuned governing class exercising authority over a non-attuned majority twice its size. The conditions that made the liberation possible — accumulated grievance across generations of systematic marginalization, a majority large enough to sustain a mass departure — were produced by exactly this demographic structure.

**Current Order.** The liberation's mass departure transformed the Order's composition. The 70% non-attuned majority that was the liberation's demographic base is now largely the Coalition's population. The Order that remains runs approximately 70% attuned to 30% non-attuned — an inversion of the pre-liberation structure. The current attuned majority is not a pre-existing condition; it is a product of selective retention. What the departure selected for was everyone with enough to lose from leaving: people embedded in attuned family structures, people whose ideological investment in the hierarchy was genuine, people born into the Order after the split who never faced the choice. The 30% non-attuned who remain are not naive about Coalition society. They stayed through the liberation. The contact prohibition's risk for this population is not first exposure to a new idea but confirmation of a doubt they already manage and have chosen, for whatever reason, not to act on.

The Order has narrativized the demographic shift as clarification rather than catastrophe. The reading that circulates in Order institutional culture is that the non-attuned who remained are those who understood where they belonged — a retrospective legitimation that converts a demographic emergency into a statement about the natural order. This reading is not seriously contested within Order institutions. There is no official account of what the departure cost.

**Attuned lineage transmission.** Within established attuned Order lineages, the transmission rate for attunement runs approximately 70-80%. Both components of the attunement mechanism — the skeletal antenna and the neurological coupling structures — show strong heritable tendency without strict inheritance. The rate is high enough that the bloodline narrative feels internally accurate to most Order families: attuned parents produce attuned children as the reliable outcome, with non-attuned children as the exception requiring management rather than the pattern requiring explanation. This is what makes the hierarchy feel legitimate from inside — the biology mostly delivers what the ideology promises. The 20-30% transmission failures are the source population for the Order's internal underclass and the primary pool from which the secret societies draw their low-cost, high-dependency recruits.

**Current Coalition.** The Coalition's population runs approximately 95% non-attuned to 5% attuned. This demographic confirms the Coalition's founding identity: it really is, overwhelmingly, a non-attuned civilization. The 5% attuned traces to two sources — liberation-era Order defectors who chose the Coalition side and whose descendants carry documented attuned ancestry, and the accumulated spontaneous emergence from the Coalition's general population at the ~1% rate currently ongoing. Both lineages are present in the outskirts communities. The geographic marginalization strategy the Coalition built for smaller attuned numbers is under pressure at this scale; the communities are large enough to have developed their own internal culture and their own grievances.

## The two factions — relationship to the Vein as culture

The institutional logic of each faction's relationship to the Vein is documented elsewhere. What lives here is the folk layer — the ambient assumptions, superstitions, and emotional registers that ordinary people carry without knowing where they came from. This is the water each faction swims in before doctrine arrives.

### The Order — the Vein as intimate infrastructure

For an ordinary Order civilian, the Vein is not remarkable. It is present in most aspects of daily life the way running water or electricity is present in a modern city — not thought about, not examined, simply there. Lighting, heating, healing, food preparation, construction: the Vein threads through all of it at varying intensities, and the attuned people who maintain and operate those systems are as unremarkable as plumbers or electricians. The wonder has been domesticated by familiarity.

The exception is childhood. Before formal instruction arrives, children encounter the Vein through folk practice first. It is common for Order families to keep a small piece of substrate on a young child — a personal talisman, worn or carried, regarded with a mixture of pride and protective instinct. Not a weapon, not a tool: a companion object, present the way a religious symbol is present in cultures that use them, intimate and personal before it is doctrinal. Early attunement feats — a child shifting the state of something for the first time — are family events. Celebrated the way a first step is celebrated: the moment the world opened a little wider. Alongside the pride sits grandmother-rule caution, the practical folk wisdom that predates any formal training. Don't hold it when you're angry. Don't let it open in a closed space. Don't play with it near water. These rules exist because someone learned them the hard way and passed them down, and they are transmitted with the same matter-of-fact authority as don't run near the fire.

The deepest folk belief in Order culture is about death. The Vein takes the energy back. Everyone returns to the field when they die — this is general doctrine, held across the Order's population as ambient cultural assumption rather than argued theology. It makes death a return rather than an extinction, and it makes the Vein a cycle rather than a resource. Attuned practitioners carry a layered version of this belief: they expect not merely to return but to go somewhere better within that return. Their lived connection to the Vein — the felt intimacy of a field they perceive directly and have spent a lifetime working — makes reunion feel qualitatively different to them than it does to someone who has never touched it. This is intuited rather than reasoned. The religious arm carries both tiers as doctrine without having to argue for either; the general population absorbs the first as background cosmology, and attuned practitioners absorb the second as something closer to personal knowledge.

Neither tier is a physical hypothesis. Neither connects consciously to the question of whether depleted sites recover over time. But the belief inadvertently encodes something real: substrate and field energy do cycle, on timescales no individual has observed, through processes no one has formally named. The Order carries the shape of this truth as metaphysics. The civilizational consequence, rarely made explicit, is that Coalition extraction is not merely economically damaging — it interrupts the return. The energy that should cycle back through the field is instead removed, processed, burned. This gives Order grief about extraction a register that goes beyond territorial or economic loss into something closer to desecration, even among Order civilians who could not articulate why they feel it as strongly as they do. The Coalition has no equivalent belief and no framework that would produce one. Their relationship to the Vein is extractive by design; recovery is a concept their industrial logic has never needed to form.

Non-attuned people within Order society occupy the position of a person navigating a world not built for them. The analogy is not dramatic exclusion — it is the more ordinary experience of a world whose full version is consistently just out of reach. Daily life is manageable, adapted, accessible in its modified forms. The ceiling becomes visible only when something requires going higher: accessing upper-tier Order institutions, receiving full-intensity healing, participating in military operations that run deep on attunement. The non-attuned Order citizen can live a relatively full life until the moment they cannot, and the moment is often unexpected. Folk attitudes toward non-attuned people within the Order run through two registers that coexist without resolving. The default is pity — a charitable condescension that frames non-attunement as incompleteness, something that didn't develop properly, not the person's fault. Underneath the pity, as Coalition power has grown and non-attuned competence has become undeniable, sits fear — managed, rarely named, expressed as patronization that has grown slightly more careful without becoming less pervasive.

### The Coalition — the Vein as industrial infrastructure

For an ordinary Coalition civilian, the Vein is fuel. It goes in the boiler of important things. The lights in the parliament building, the apparatus in the major hospital, the systems running the large rail terminals and military installations — these run on Vein-derived energy, and most Coalition citizens know this the way a 1900s city-dweller knows the gasworks exists somewhere: as background fact, not lived knowledge. The Vein enters daily consciousness at the level of important infrastructure. It does not enter the home. Ordinary domestic life — heating, cooking, small commerce, local transport — runs on coal, steam, and muscle. The Vein is for things that matter enough to justify the apparatus cost.

What Coalition children are told about the Vein is almost entirely practical. Don't play near apparatus. Don't touch substrate. Stay back when the pressure is high. Tell an adult. Safety rules before anything else, delivered with the same register as warnings about open water or railway lines — real danger, matter-of-fact caution, nothing sacred about it.

The folk attitude toward accidents involving apparatus follows the same industrial logic. An accident is an engineering failure, not an act of nature or a force asserting itself. Something went wrong in the design, the maintenance, or the operation. Blame distributes along the chain of custody: the worker at the bottom is the default folk target, the least educated and least able to defend themselves publicly; technicians and foremen blame each other and the specifications they were given; engineers blame the budget constraints and the management decisions that overrode their recommendations; institutions blame the individuals who failed to follow procedure. The political layer sits on top of this and exploits whatever the existing blame distribution makes convenient. The Vein itself is not blamed. The Vein does not have intentions.

The folk origin of the Coalition's emotional relationship to attuned people predates any of this institutional logic. During the development period, the displaced non-attuned population witnessed Order practitioners doing things that had no explanation in any framework they possessed. Matter shifting state without visible cause. Healing that exceeded what bodies should be able to do. The uncanny wrongness of a world behaving differently for some people than for others. With no scientific framework to receive these observations, the mind reached for the nearest available category: these people are something more. Not different in kind from a human but further along — the next step of something, felt before it was named. That feeling was transmitted across generations. The scientific framework eventually arrived and gave it a different vocabulary — biological sensitivity, field coupling, measurable phenomenon. But folk belief does not update cleanly when the explanation comes. The old feeling persisted underneath the new language, and ordinary Coalition citizens carry both simultaneously: the school-learned explanation and the older wordless sense that attuned people are more somehow. The feeling is older and sits deeper, and it produces in Coalition culture a low ambient inferiority register that most people would deny if asked and that is exploitable by anyone who knows it is there — the Order, by performing the uncanny deliberately; Coalition political figures, by activating it as threat rather than awe when the population needs hardening against attuned citizens at the margins.

The folk explanation for why the Order is wrong runs through four registers that coexist at varying intensities depending on who is speaking and when. The Order is wasteful — sitting on the Vein and trickling it through a handful of special people when the Coalition gets more from it for more people. The Order is unnatural — building a civilization around one biological trait most people don't have is the aberration, not the baseline. The Order chose this — the treatment of non-attuned people as lesser was a decision, not an inevitability, and you don't get sympathy for consequences you chose. And the Order is stuck — not building toward anything, maintaining rather than progressing, repeating the same civilization while the Coalition moves. In a culture that treats engineering and science as moral virtues, stasis reads as failure. Being behind feels like a choice.

Attuned Coalition citizens living at the margins produce a specific and uncomfortable folk response that varies with proximity and history. People who have personal connection to someone at the margins — family, former colleagues, neighbours before the disclosure — carry a complicated individual feeling that rarely maps cleanly onto the cultural position. People without that connection default to a folk attitude that is less hostile than it is distancing: the sense that attuned Coalition citizens should find a way to be useful or find somewhere else to be, framed not as cruelty but as reasonableness. People whose family history runs through the liberation war carry something edged with resentment — not at the individual but at the position, the sense that some people get to be both things when most people only get to be one. And at the institutional extremes, in military and scientific circles where the Coalition's capability relative to the Order is a daily preoccupation, the folk attitude shades into something more instrumental: attuned capacity is a resource the Coalition is currently wasting on geography when it could be pointed at the problem. Most people are some combination of these at once, and which register dominates depends on the day, the news, and who is asking.

### What success feels like — the two material hierarchies

These are folk registers, not universal prescriptions. Individual characters deviate from them. The deviation is legible against the baseline, which is what gives it meaning, but nothing here is deterministic.

**Order.** Social position is determined at birth and movement along the ladder is exceptional in both directions — downward and upward. This is not experienced as injustice from inside Order culture. It is experienced as natural order: you are where you are because of what you are biologically, and the system is self-sealing enough that exceptions get explained away rather than acknowledged as exceptions. Someone who falls was probably always deficient. Someone who rises probably had hidden lineage. The system legitimates itself. The material hierarchy runs: Vein access first, then food, shelter, relations, self-realization. Vein access is not a medium for reaching the other values — it is a terminal value in itself. Using attunement to accomplish what mundane solutions could handle is not laziness or excess. It is correct living, the fullest expression of what a person with attunement is supposed to be. Success in Order culture is confirmation — validation of something already believed about yourself and your lineage. The emotional register is not the excitement of discovery but the relief of the system working for you rather than against you. Underneath the serenity Order culture projects is the knowledge, never spoken, that the floor exists and that some families have fallen through it. Reaching mastery, consolidating position, securing the next generation — these carry relief because the alternative was always present as an unspoken possibility. There is also a generational weight to it: an Order family confirming its position is not just validating the current generation but confirming that the story they have told about themselves for generations is still true.

Non-attuned Order citizens cannot map their experience onto this hierarchy. The Vein access tier is structurally closed to them, which means their folk sense of what a good life looks like runs closer to Coalition values — food, shelter, relations, self-realization, with the Vein as background infrastructure rather than terminal goal. They are ideologically Order and materially Coalition-adjacent, belonging fully to neither framework. Order culture has no language for their success because the hierarchy does not account for it. Achievement in domains the hierarchy does not recognize produces a specific quiet invisibility — present in the society, legible to other non-attuned people, unreadable to the attuned world surrounding them.

**Coalition.** The ladder is climbable but anchored to birth conditions and environment. The aspiration of making it is real and culturally central. The friction is also real, and the gap between what Coalition culture officially says success requires and what it practically requires is a source of persistent low-level cognitive dissonance that most Coalition citizens manage rather than resolve. People who climbed know what the anchor felt like. People who didn't have to explain their position to themselves in a culture that told them the ladder was open. The material hierarchy runs: food, shelter, relations — meaning both personal bonds and social standing — then self-realization. Vein access and money are not values in themselves. They are mediums, tools for moving between the real values, and evaluated instrumentally rather than intrinsically. Success in Coalition culture is arrival — reaching somewhere you were not guaranteed to reach. The emotional register is relief as much as satisfaction: the anchor didn't hold you all the way down. The anxiety that comes with arrival is its own specific quality — you got here, but you could not have arrived means you could also fall back, and that possibility does not fully quiet.

Attuned Coalition citizens living outside the margins — those who have not been disclosed or pushed out — carry a version of this material hierarchy with something missing underneath it. The field is faintly present in their experience in ways they have no framework to interpret and strong incentives not to examine. The folk success register of their culture tells them what to want and how to want it. The sensory experience running beneath that register tells them something else is there, something the hierarchy doesn't account for, something that feels less like an addition and more like a correction to a distortion they didn't know was there — the way glasses don't add something new so much as reveal that everything before was slightly wrong. Most learn not to look at it directly. The ones who do are the ones the Coalition's geography solution is designed to catch.

Both cultures, beneath their different presentations — Order serenity, Coalition optimism — are carrying more anxiety than their official registers acknowledge. The Order presents as assured because the system confirms what it already believes. The Coalition presents as forward-moving because progress is the moral frame that justifies everything it has done. Neither presentation is false. Neither is the whole story.

### What each side considers shameful

Shame is the enforcement mechanism of folk culture — the social cost attached to behaviors that threaten the assumptions a civilization runs on. What each faction finds shameful maps directly onto what each faction most needs to protect.

**Order.** Three categories of shame, each with its own register and its own enforcement mechanism.

Low or absent attunement is the foundational shame — not dramatic, not loudly named, but structurally present in every social interaction. It cannot be openly performed because Order culture simultaneously claims the system is natural and just, which means shaming someone for their biology would be acknowledging that the natural order produces unjust outcomes. So the shame is indirect. Pity with a slightly sharp edge. Exclusion framed as practical necessity. Opportunities that don't arrive with explanations attached. The person being shamed is expected to absorb it without naming it, and usually does, because naming it requires acknowledging a truth the culture cannot officially recognize.

Having to use mundane alternatives to Vein solutions is the behavioral shame — the Order equivalent of taking the bus when you own a car. It is not catastrophic on its own but it is legible as a signal: insufficient capacity, diminished status, or a failure of will. The consequence is that Order practitioners face real social pressure to perform Vein use even when mundane solutions would be more efficient. The performance of capability matters as much as the capability. This has operational consequences that the Order's military has never fully reckoned with — practitioners who use the Vein when they shouldn't, pushing past reasonable limits, because the alternative reads as weakness in front of people who matter.

Researching alternatives to the Vein as solutions to unresolved problems carries the most complex shame — three axes that escalate in severity depending on context and who is watching. The surface reason, stated politely, is that alternatives imply the Vein is insufficient — a kind of faithlessness, an admission that the civilization's foundational tool might not be adequate. This is the raised eyebrow, the questioning of judgment. The structural reason, felt but never fully named, is that alternatives redistribute capability. A solution that doesn't require attunement gives non-attuned and low-attuned Order citizens access to something the hierarchy currently reserves. Research into alternatives is not just intellectual heterodoxy — it is a potential ramp, and the people who oppose ramps rarely say so directly. They say the ramp is unnecessary, that the building is fine, that the people who need the ramp should have thought about that before. The third axis activates when Coalition methods are involved or implied: the researcher is no longer just wrong, they are thinking like the enemy. This last register sharpens across the course of the war as Coalition apparatus becomes more legible to Order observers. What starts as a raised eyebrow ends, at the limit, as a secret society problem.

**Coalition.** Three categories of shame with different social mechanics but the same underlying function — protecting the founding identity.

Being attuned, or being known to have close personal connection to attuned people, carries two distinct shames that often run together. For the attuned Coalition citizen, the shame is existential — you are the wrong kind of person inside your own civilization, carrying a biology that your culture's founding identity requires to be categorically separate from Coalition membership. The management strategy is concealment, and the cost of concealment is a specific kind of loneliness: performing normalcy continuously, reading every social situation for disclosure risk, learning to suppress the sensory register that the culture gave no vocabulary for. For the non-attuned Coalition citizen with close attuned connections, the shame is social — association as contamination, the folk sense that proximity to the wrong kind of person reflects on your own positioning. This second shame is softer than the first and more negotiable; people maintain private connections while publicly observing the appropriate distances, and most Coalition citizens know someone who is doing exactly this.

Developing a mystical or religious relationship to the Vein is the epistemological shame — the Coalition equivalent of superstition, and treated with the contempt that rationalist cultures reserve for their own apostates. The Coalition's founding identity is built on systematic knowledge replacing instinct and faith. Filling the Vein's genuine unknowns with religious or mystical explanation is god-of-gaps thinking, and it is shameful precisely because it implies you couldn't handle not knowing and reached for comfort instead of method. The Vein has real unknowns — the Order's capabilities remain partially inexplicable to Coalition science, and the folk experience of proximity to active substrate produces sensations that don't fit the official framework. The shameful response is to mythologize those gaps. The acceptable response is to treat them as engineering problems awaiting better instruments. People who privately have something like reverence for the Vein — and some do, particularly those who have worked closely with it for years — learn to keep that register entirely internal. It reads, if expressed, as Order-adjacent thinking, which compounds the shame considerably.

Downward social movement and lower-level stagnation are shame categories that the Coalition's official meritocracy produces but cannot fully acknowledge. Downward mobility is the active shame — something failed, the anchor held, the arrival was reversed. The culture's optimism about the ladder makes falling more personally humiliating than it would be in a society that acknowledged structural constraints openly, because in a Coalition framing the ladder is open and failure to stay on it is therefore individual. Stagnation at the lower levels is the heavier and less visible shame — the one the culture creates structurally and then denies. People born into constrained circumstances who do not climb carry the implicit weight of a meritocracy's judgment: the ladder was available and you didn't climb it. That judgment is ambient, rarely spoken, and almost impossible to argue against from inside the culture's own framework. The Coalition's founding liberation narrative makes this worse, not better — a civilization that fought for the right to be judged on merit has limited vocabulary for acknowledging that merit is not, in practice, the only variable.

### How each side relates to progress and change

**Order.** Progress in Order culture is not a direction — it is a depth. The ideal is not to move somewhere new but to achieve fuller, more precise, more complete mastery of what already exists. The Vein is the fixed center of that ambition. Getting better at it is the work of a lifetime and a lineage. Moving beyond it is transgression. This gives Order culture a conservative orientation that is not experienced as conservatism from the inside — it is experienced as seriousness, as the appropriate weight given to something that took generations to understand and could be lost in one.

The structural consequence is that Order knowledge is cyclic rather than linear. It accumulates in practitioners and lineages, transmitted through embodied practice that cannot be fully written down or institutionally preserved. When a lineage thins, when a master dies without transmitting fully, when the institution suppresses a branch of inquiry, knowledge submerges. It does not disappear permanently — the Vein is still there, and determined practitioners can rediscover what was lost — but the rediscovery takes generations and often arrives incomplete. The shape of Order knowledge over time is not a rising line but a series of waves: real advance followed by partial loss, peaks of individual or lineage mastery surrounded by water, with difficult passage between them. Individual practitioners can see this — a senior master watching their own lineage thin knows exactly what is going to be lost when they die, and the institution has no mechanism to do anything about it. The institution itself cannot see it, because the institution measures current capability against current need and finds nothing alarming. The grief is personal. The loss is civilizational.

Change within the Order is experienced as disruption to a working system unless it can be framed as restoration — returning to a purer or earlier form of what already exists. Changes that arrive framed as recovery of lost knowledge are absorbed more easily than changes framed as innovation. This is why the secret societies, who preserve knowledge the institution has suppressed, operate through the language of custodianship rather than the language of discovery. They are not doing something new. They are keeping something old. That framing is not purely strategic — it reflects a genuine Order psychology in which the past is the repository of value and the future is the direction from which threats arrive.

**Coalition.** Progress is a foundational moral value — not just instrumentally useful but constitutive of what the Coalition believes itself to be. A civilization that replaced instinct with knowledge, that built systematic understanding out of necessity and turned it into liberation, carries progress as identity. To move forward is to honor the founding. To stagnate is to betray it.

The structural consequence is that Coalition knowledge compounds. Written records transmit across generations without the lossy degradation of embodied transmission. Each generation inherits the prior generation's documented work and builds from it rather than reconstructing it. The shape of Coalition knowledge over time is broadly linear, punctuated by exponential periods when a foundational breakthrough unlocks a whole domain at once — the mechanical pressure system, the quartz transducer, whatever comes next. Between inflection points progress is steady and incremental. At the inflection points it lurches forward faster than institutions can absorb, which is why Coalition political and regulatory structures are always slightly behind Coalition capability. The industry giants exist partly because capability outran governance before governance existed to stop them. The research frontier is always operating in a space the constitutional framework hasn't fully reached yet.

Change in Coalition culture is welcomed in the abstract and negotiated in the specific. The general value of progress is rarely contested. What gets contested is whose progress, at whose cost, moving how fast, and who decides. The Coalition's four political tensions — attuned emergence, war prosecution, research speed, industrialization costs — are all variants of this negotiation. Coalition citizens are not against change; they are against changes that threaten what they have already arrived at. The liberation register that founded the culture has, across generations, calcified into a protection register: not building toward freedom anymore, but defending the freedom already won. Most Coalition citizens would not describe it this way. The founding rhetoric is still liberation. The operative psychology is closer to conservation of a different kind than the Order's — not conservation of hierarchy but conservation of arrival.

### What each side is actually fighting for

Both factions have an official account of why the war continues and a folk account that diverges from it in ways most people never fully articulate. The gap between the two is not hypocrisy — it is the ordinary drift that happens when a founding cause outlasts the conditions that produced it and institutions keep the rhetoric alive after the underlying psychology has shifted.

**Order.** The stated motivation is custodianship: protecting the civilization that grew from the Vein, maintaining the natural order that attunement makes possible, defending what is right against a faction that is doing something fundamentally wrong with something that isn't theirs. This is the version that gets spoken at funerals, invoked by senior figures, and transmitted to children as the reason their parents are away. It is not false. Most Order people who hold it believe it.

The actual motivation, underneath, has shifted across generations from confidence to grip. The Order is no longer fighting from a position of assured legitimacy — it is fighting to preserve a hierarchy that an increasing number of people inside it can no longer fully justify to themselves, against an enemy whose existence proves that the Order's foundational claim — that attunement is the natural basis of governance — is a choice rather than an inevitability. The barrier eroding, the Coalition's capability growing, the non-attuned world demonstrating competence the Order's framework has no category for: these are not just military threats. They are arguments. The Order's soldiers are fighting to prevent those arguments from being settled, which is a different thing from fighting to protect something sacred, and it produces a different kind of soldier — more rigid in doctrine, less able to absorb ambiguity, more dependent on the institutional framing holding because they have no personal framework to fall back on if it doesn't.

The Order folk who have noticed this shift — and some have, particularly among the risen military commanders and the secret societies — carry a specific kind of exhaustion. They are fighting for something they believe in less completely than they once did, on behalf of an institution that cannot acknowledge what it has become, and they have no language inside their own culture for that experience that doesn't read as betrayal.

**Coalition.** The stated motivation is defense of liberation: protecting what was won, honoring the founding cause, refusing to allow the Order to reassert dominion over people who fought and died to be free of it. This is the version in the speeches, on the monuments, in the curriculum. It connects the current war to the founding war and makes the current soldier a continuation of the founding hero. It is not false. The liberation was real, the founding cause was just, and the Order would reassert dominion if it could.

The actual motivation has calcified across generations from liberation into protection of arrival. The Coalition is not building toward a freer world anymore — it is defending the world it already has. The people fighting are not fighting to free anyone; they are fighting to prevent losing what was already won, which is a conservative motivation wearing emancipatory language. The founding rhetoric still sounds like it points forward. The operative psychology points backward, toward preservation. Most Coalition soldiers would find this framing uncomfortable if it were put to them directly, because it implies that the Coalition has become, in some structural sense, a different kind of Order — an established power protecting its position rather than a liberation movement building toward something. The founding identity depends on the Coalition being the side that fights for universal freedom. The current reality is a civilization that pushes attuned citizens to its margins, fights to hold territory it controls, and frames all of it in the language of the people it used to be.

The Coalition folk who have noticed the gap — soldiers who joined for ideological reasons and found something different when they arrived, citizens who remember when the founding language felt earned rather than inherited — carry their own version of that exhaustion. The cause is still defensible. The version of it they are actually enacting is harder to be proud of.

**The gap as character territory.** The stated vs actual gap is not primarily a political observation — it is a psychological one. Characters who have not noticed the gap yet are fighting cleanly, with the full weight of the stated version behind them. Characters who have noticed it are carrying something the culture gave them no vocabulary for: a commitment to a cause they believe in less completely than they present, in service of an institution they have partially seen through, with no available exit that doesn't feel like betrayal. That is the emotional territory where the most interesting choices live — not the moment of heroism or villainy, but the moment a character realizes what they are actually doing and has to decide what to do with that knowledge.

The war between the Order and the Coalition is not a disagreement about facts. It is a collision between two incompatible frameworks for what legitimacy means — frameworks so foundational that neither side can fully see the other's as a framework at all. It just looks like being wrong.

The Order's position is not, at its core, a political claim about governance or territory. It is a felt claim about belonging. The attuned relationship to the Vein is biological and reciprocal — the field moves through attuned bodies, practitioners perceive it directly, and the folk belief holds that their energy returns to it at death. From inside that experience, the Vein is not a resource to be owned. It is something closer to kin. The Order's sense of custodianship over the Vein does not feel like ownership in the property sense — it feels like the self-evident responsibility of the people whose bodies are tuned to something to be the ones who steward it. The Coalition's claim to the Vein reads, from inside Order culture, not as a competing political position but as a category error. You do not acquire a relationship to the Vein by extracting it. You do not steward it by burning it. What the Coalition is doing to the Vein is, in the Order's folk register, something between vandalism and desecration — and the folk belief that Coalition extraction interrupts the return cycle gives that feeling a cosmological weight most Order civilians could not articulate but feel as a real grievance.

The Coalition's position is equally foundational and equally pre-argumentative. If you did not choose to be attuned, you did not earn the Vein. Biology is not merit. The Order's sense of natural custodianship is, from inside Coalition culture, exactly the logic that produced the subjugation — the idea that an accident of birth confers legitimate dominion over a shared resource. The Coalition's counter-claim is not just political. It is moral. The Vein is part of the world. The world belongs to everyone who lives in it. Systematic knowledge and collective effort are legitimate relationships to a resource. Biological sensitivity is not.

These two positions cannot be negotiated into compatibility. A border can be drawn. Prisoner exchanges can be arranged. Agreements about extraction rates and territorial buffers can be signed. None of that touches the underlying question of whether the Order's felt custodianship or the Coalition's principle of universal access is the correct framework for what the Vein is and who gets to decide how it is used. Every settlement is a pause in a conflict the underlying conditions continue to produce — not because both sides are stubborn or because the right compromise hasn't been found, but because the conflict is not about anything a compromise can resolve. This is what makes the current war different in register from a territorial dispute or a resource competition. Those can end. This one can only be suspended.

### Cross-faction encounters — occupation and prisoners

The culture sections above describe how each faction relates to the Vein and to its own people. What follows is what happens when each side encounters the other's ordinary people up close — during occupation, detention, and the institutional processing of captured enemies. This is where ambient assumptions meet specific human beings, and where doctrine either holds or develops cracks.

**Order occupation of Coalition territory.** The Order's first priority in occupied Coalition territory is the apparatus. Not the people, not the governance structures, not the economy — the machines. Coalition extraction infrastructure is, in the Order's folk register, the physical instrument of desecration: the thing that pulls the Vein out, processes it, burns it. Dismantling it is not experienced as conquest. It is experienced as restoration — putting something right that was made wrong. This gives Order soldiers performing the work a felt moral clarity that pure territorial occupation would not provide, and it means apparatus sites are the first targets of any occupation, ahead of administrative centres or military installations.

The people who built and operated the apparatus are detained because their knowledge is instrumentally valuable, but the institutional interest in them is not unified. Three separate Order institutions want different things from the same prisoners, and none fully coordinates with the others. The military wants tactical intelligence: where the other installations are, what they can do, what the operational doctrine is. The secret societies want something deeper — what has the Coalition actually learned about the Vein through mechanical means, knowledge the Order's own cultural ceiling has prevented it from pursuing openly. The founding families and the religious arm want the machines destroyed and do not much care what is learned in the process; their interest in the operators ends when the last machine is dismantled. The same detention site, three concurrent extractions, each operating on its own timeline and reporting through its own channels.

**The killing of apparatus architects** — the engineers, designers, and senior operators who built or advanced Coalition Vein infrastructure — is not uniform Order policy. It varies with the political position of the commanding officer, mapping onto the Order's internal compass. A doctrinally pure commander kills apparatus architects as righteous action: these people built the instruments of desecration, and their removal is morally continuous with the dismantling of the machines themselves. A doctrinally pure commander operating from institutional mandate rather than family loyalty kills them as policy enforcement — the Order's position is that this technology should not exist, so the people who create it should not continue to. Both kill, but the felt register is different, and the second is more systematic. A pragmatic commander in either loyalty axis has reasons to keep architects alive — at least temporarily, at least the ones who know something no one else does. The family-loyal pragmatist may extract what they need and then transfer the prisoner to someone else for disposal, keeping distance between the intelligence and the killing. The institutionally driven pragmatist may argue for sustained detention and structured debriefing, which puts them in direct tension with the doctrinal wing. The political position does not merely determine whether the killing happens — it determines the timing, the justification, and what happens in the gap between capture and death. That gap is where character stories live.

**Contact prohibition.** The Order maintains a doctrinal prohibition on contact between its own non-attuned population and Coalition civilians. The stated reason is protection — Coalition people are dangerous, their ideas are contamination. The operative reason is that Coalition civilians are walking evidence that non-attuned people build functional civilizations, and that evidence cannot be allowed to reach the people inside the Order who would find it most compelling. The prohibition's ambition is total. Its feasibility depends on what fraction of the Order's population is non-attuned — a demographic question not yet resolved. If the non-attuned are a small minority, the prohibition is enforceable. If they are a large fraction, the prohibition becomes either operationally ruinous or selectively enforced, which means it leaks, and the leaking itself becomes a signal to the people it was designed to contain.

**Coalition occupation of Order territory.** The Coalition's first priority in occupied Order territory is knowledge extraction from attuned practitioners. The apparatus is the Coalition's strength; attunement is the gap in its understanding. Captured or detained practitioners represent access to information about the Vein that Coalition science has not yet reached through mechanical means — and that the Coalition's founding identity makes difficult to pursue through its own attuned citizens at the margins.

**Coalition interrogation culture.** The interrogation of attuned practitioners does not have a single character. It varies with which Coalition institution has custody — and for a senior practitioner, all three relevant institutions may compete for access simultaneously, producing a coordination failure that mirrors the Order's own three-institution conflict over detained apparatus operators.

Military interrogators want operational intelligence: deployment locations, installation capabilities, strategic doctrine. Their timeline is compressed — military intelligence degrades with distance from capture. The inferiority register is present but secondary; the military interrogator's primary relationship to the practitioner is as a source of time-sensitive information, not as a representative of superior capability. When the practitioner withholds or delays, military interrogators are the most likely to move toward coercion — not from ideology but from clock pressure. The specific failure mode is witnessing: when a practitioner demonstrates capability during interrogation rather than merely describing it, the clinical distance collapses in a way that questioning descriptions does not produce. Seeing what an attuned practitioner can do, from this close, is a different experience from being told about it.

Scientific interrogators are closest to the technical debriefing register because that is genuinely how they approach knowledge. Their interest is in what attunement actually perceives — what the field feels like from inside the sense, what practitioners can detect that Coalition instruments cannot, where Coalition mechanical theory diverges from what practitioners know empirically. This is the register most capable of producing voluntary extended cooperation: a practitioner who understands what the scientist wants, and who finds something in the exchange, may provide more through genuine engagement than coercion would extract. The inferiority register here manifests as fascination rather than anxiety. The failure mode runs the other direction: a sophisticated practitioner can exploit a scientist's real curiosity, shaping what gets learned and providing a version of Vein knowledge that is accurate in its parts and strategically curated in its whole. The scientist may not know they have been managed.

Industrial interrogators — representatives of the extraction industry or the apparatus supply infrastructure — want site knowledge: what the Order knows about specific deposit characteristics, extraction behavior, substrate concentrations, site geology. This is the most transactional register and the least personal. Industrial interest in a prisoner does not expire on a military timeline; site knowledge retains value long after operational intelligence has dated, making industrial interrogators the most likely to push for sustained long-term detention. The practitioner's site knowledge also maps directly to Order territorial interests, which gives practitioners the clearest structural reason to withhold it — making industrial interrogation the most likely to settle into extended low-intensity pressure rather than acute crisis.

A senior practitioner — one whose knowledge spans operational, Vein-science, and site dimensions — is a contested prisoner in the same structural sense that an Order institution finds a Coalition apparatus architect contested. Multiple Coalition interests want different things, on different timelines, using different methods, without a coordination mechanism that forces them to align. The practitioner in the middle has more room to maneuver than the Coalition intends to give.

**Coalition prisoner doctrine.** The Coalition handles captured Order personnel through a sharp doctrinal split based on attunement status. Attuned prisoners are intelligence assets — sustained interrogation, technical debriefing, long-term detention. Their value is what they know about the Vein, and the Coalition's institutional interest in them does not expire quickly.

Non-attuned Order prisoners receive something entirely different: an indoctrination and absorption pipeline. The Coalition's founding story is a liberation narrative — non-attuned people who escaped Order subjugation and built something of their own. A non-attuned Order prisoner is, in the Coalition's institutional logic, a version of their own ancestors: someone living under the same hierarchy the founders fought to escape, who has not yet had the opportunity or the framework to leave. The pitch is not subtle: you were on the wrong side of the same divide we fought to end. The Order's contact prohibition exists in part to prevent exactly this pipeline from working — and the existence of the prohibition, when non-attuned Order prisoners learn of it, becomes part of the pitch. Your own people kept you away from us. Ask yourself why.

The pitch succeeds in roughly one in five cases. Success is not uniform — it tracks with the prisoner's position within the Order. Non-attuned prisoners who experienced the contact prohibition as a constraint on their own ambitions are more susceptible than those who experienced it as ambient background. Those who hit the Order's attunement ceiling directly — who wanted something the Order's structure categorically refused them — are more susceptible than those who found workable paths within non-attuned life. Older prisoners with deeper institutional identities are harder to reach than younger ones with less invested. The Coalition calibrates the pipeline to these distinctions rather than applying a uniform approach.

Converts are absorbed into the Coalition's lower institutional tiers with significant monitoring. Their knowledge of Order internal structure makes them useful; their origin makes them permanently suspect. The Coalition's intelligence services maintain a working assumption that some fraction of converts are Order plants — a correct assumption, though identifying which ones requires resources that are not always applied. Full social trust is a generational outcome: converts' children, raised entirely within Coalition culture, carry no asterisk. The converts themselves do not fully shed theirs.

Non-converts are not handled uniformly. Some are exchanged in prisoner negotiations. Some are held in extended detention. A significant fraction are released in ways designed to let them carry their exposure back. A non-attuned Order prisoner who has spent months inside Coalition territory, seen what Coalition non-attuned people built, and returns unconverted, carries the evidence the contact prohibition was designed to prevent from circulating. The Order's practice of treating returned prisoners as contaminated regardless of stated loyalty amplifies this: someone the Order already suspects has diminished reason to perform loyalty, and their account of what they observed — credible precisely because they did not convert — reaches audiences the contact prohibition was meant to protect.

**Folk encounter psychology.** Institutional doctrine describes what each side is supposed to do when it encounters the other's people. What happens in the gut is what the doctrine was designed to manage. They are not the same thing.

Order non-attuned encountering Coalition civilians: the conditioning was calibrated to an abstraction. Coalition people are dangerous, their ideas are contamination, their civilization is an affront. The person is harder to fit into the abstraction. Coalition civilians eat, fear, grieve, and disagree with their neighbors — they are ordinary in ways the abstraction was not built to accommodate. The immediate gut response is usually the trained one. Then the person persists. This is precisely what the contact prohibition was designed to prevent: not argument, not doctrine, but the ordinary human recognition that makes ideology harder to sustain. What leaks through the prohibition is not Coalition ideas but Coalition evidence.

Order attuned encountering Coalition infrastructure operates on a different dissonance. Attuned soldiers and occupation forces are not subject to the contact prohibition in the same protective register — the prohibition was designed to shelter the non-attuned, and the attuned are presumed to have the identity security to encounter Coalition people without being destabilized. What they were not prepared for is the scale of what the apparatus produces. An attuned practitioner who has operated within the Order's framework, where Vein capability is an expression of personal biological gift, encountering a large Coalition extraction installation faces the cognitive friction of seeing systematic capability doing things their framework says it should not be able to do. The framework is demonstrably wrong about at least this. What the practitioner does with that observation is a personal decision, and the Order's doctrine provides no script for it.

Coalition encountering Order practitioners up close: the inferiority register does not resolve on personal contact — it often intensifies. An abstraction can be managed by the Coalition's founding ideology. A specific person who demonstrably can do what practitioners do, and who is also recognizably human — who is afraid and proud and sometimes more interesting than expected — is harder to dismiss. The uncanniness survives contact in a way it was supposed not to. Coalition soldiers and civilians who have been close to working practitioners often return changed in ways they cannot explain, and that the Coalition has no official vocabulary for, because the official vocabulary was built to deny there was anything to feel.

Coalition encountering Order non-attuned: the most disorienting encounter for Coalition people is not with practitioners. It is with Order non-attuned civilians. The founding story requires a person who is waiting to be freed — who knows on some level that the hierarchy is illegitimate, whose loyalty to the Order is provisional. The actual non-attuned Order civilian often has a full cultural identity, a community stake, relationships in which the Order's hierarchy is embedded rather than experienced as external constraint. The Coalition civilian who expected to find a version of their own ancestors finds instead someone who does not know they are supposed to want liberation, or who knows and does not agree. This does not disprove the Coalition's founding story. It complicates it in ways the Coalition's culture has no prepared frame for.

### Wartime distortion of folk culture

The culture described in the preceding sections is the peacetime baseline — the ambient water each faction swims in before war becomes the continuous condition of life. What war does to that baseline is not replacement. It is pressure. Each faction's ambient assumptions amplify in some directions, suppress in others, and occasionally bend into forms the peacetime version would not recognize as its own.

**Order.** The peacetime performance pressure — the social cost of using mundane alternatives to Vein solutions — does not relax under wartime conditions. It intensifies. The Order's hierarchy is under external challenge precisely when it needs to perform confidence. The institution cannot afford the admission that some things are better done without the Vein, because that admission in wartime reads not as tactical pragmatism but as civilizational doubt. Practitioners are pushed harder, longer, at higher intensities, for audiences that are watching. The Order's pattern of overextension — permanent damage accumulating in practitioners who push past their reasonable limits because the social cost of declining is too high — runs faster and deeper during wartime, and the institution has no mechanism to acknowledge this because acknowledging it would require acknowledging the performance pressure itself.

The folk belief that attuned practitioners return to the field at death accumulates a specific wartime weight. Every attuned practitioner killed near Coalition extraction infrastructure dies with the return interrupted — or accelerated into something the folk framework has no language for. Order communities that have lost practitioners to operations near extraction sites grieve with a quality of wrongness that outlasts ordinary mourning. The Vein should have taken them back. Something prevented it. That something is still operating.

Non-attuned Order citizens bearing wartime loads receive no corresponding recognition from the institution. The folk pity-condescension does not soften when their contributions become operationally necessary. It calcifies. The hierarchy that is already under external challenge needs internal coherence more than it needs accuracy — and acknowledging that non-attuned people are carrying specific domains of the war would mean acknowledging that the hierarchy was always underselling them, which is not an acknowledgment the institution can make without undermining the structure it is fighting to preserve. Non-attuned Order citizens who do essential wartime work do it in the same ambient invisibility as before. The institution absorbs their contribution without naming it. This is not individual hypocrisy. It is the ordinary consequence of a framework that has no category for the thing it is relying on.

The secret societies occupy a specific wartime tension. The knowledge they preserve — exotic states, suppressed capabilities, branches of inquiry the institution closed — becomes operationally relevant precisely when the institution is least able to acknowledge it. Acknowledging what the secret societies preserved means acknowledging the suppression; doing that under wartime pressure means conceding that the institution withheld capability from itself for political reasons, at cost. The institution cannot make that concession without undermining the authority it needs to prosecute the war. So the tension runs both directions simultaneously: practitioners who hold exotic state knowledge become more visibly valuable and more institutionally dangerous at the same time. The war is exactly the kind of crisis that could legitimate a demand for acknowledgment — which is exactly why the institution treats any such demand as the threat it actually is.

**Coalition.** The gap between the founding liberation rhetoric and the conservation-of-arrival psychology that underlies it narrows under wartime pressure — not because the underlying psychology becomes more honest, but because an enemy at the threshold makes defense and liberation feel like the same thing again. The threat provides the emotional content the rhetoric needs. A Coalition citizen in wartime does not feel like they are defending what they have; they feel like they are still fighting for what was won, because the alternative is having it taken back. The clarity is borrowed. From inside it, it does not feel borrowed.

This convergence holds at the level of folk culture without requiring examination. The persistent ideological tensions in Coalition politics — war prosecution, attuned emergence, research speed, industrialization costs — do not disappear, but they operate in a register where the founding frame is more available and less stale than it was in peacetime. The question of whether the Coalition is still becoming what it was founded to be, or merely defending what it calcified into, is a peacetime question. Wartime suspends it.

The attuned Coalition citizen at the margins continues to occupy the same structural position during wartime. The folk attitude does not move toward integration. What changes is the political valence of the question — not the answer. The contradiction between "we fight against hierarchy based on birth" and "we push attuned people to the geographic margins" is easier to avoid during wartime than during peacetime, because the enemy's existence provides continuous clarity that drowns out internal complexity. The contradiction is still there. It is easier not to notice.

The Coalition's folk framework that quietly assigns stagnation as personal failure has no account for casualties. The people the meritocracy was gently dismissing are bearing disproportionate military costs, and Coalition communities managing wartime grief have no prepared vocabulary for honoring people they had no prepared vocabulary for valuing. The usual resolution is promotion-in-death — the fallen become heroes in a register the living version was never offered. Whether that reads as genuine or as the same hypocrisy wearing different clothes is a personal and generational question.

What wartime does not suppress is the accumulation of witnessed experience. Coalition soldiers and civilians who encounter working practitioners up close — in interrogation, in occupation, in combat — come home with something the folk culture has no vocabulary for. The scientific framework provides the category: biological difference, measurable phenomenon. The gut-level register from direct observation is older and does not update when the category arrives. The veteran who has seen what a practitioner can do under pressure carries a specific dissonance between the institutional account and the unprocessed register from experience. This does not become a political problem during wartime. It becomes a peacetime problem, in the generation of veterans the war eventually produces.

## The historical sequence

*Note: the broad shape of this sequence is established. The internal logic of the development period — specifically what allowed scientific understanding to cross the threshold into practical application — will be sharpened once the physical nature of the substrate is finalized. Revisit when that decision is made.*

**The pre-conflict era.** Attuned people are a minority. High-concentration Vein territories are hostile to them due to overexposure sensitivity — the very sites richest in field energy are the most dangerous to the people most capable of using it. Non-attuned people — marginalized within an Order-dominated world — are pushed toward or take refuge in the Vein-dense regions the Order doesn't want to occupy. The Order doesn't monitor them closely, genuinely believing non-attuned people cannot threaten them. For a long time, the Order is right.

The Order's failure to extract from Vein-dense territories was not negligence. It was the entirely rational consequence of a civilization whose labor force and entire value system were built around attuned practitioners — the same people for whom high-concentration sites were biologically hostile. The Order had sufficient yield from moderate-concentration sites where practitioners could work without prohibitive exposure risk. Rotating attuned workers through the dense sites was not a practical alternative: the personal cost of operating at those concentrations was real, cumulative, and permanent at the extremes, and the Order's culture treated its practitioners as the irreplaceable axis of civilization rather than expendable extraction labor. From inside the Order's framework, burning through practitioners to work sites that yielded no more than safer alternatives was not a rational trade. The dense territories were left because the Order's own logic left them — not empty, but unworked, unmonitored, and beneath notice.

The non-attuned people who moved into those territories did not register as a threat for the same reason. The Order's entire model of power ran through attunement. A population without it, however large, was not a military or economic rival in any category the Order knew how to measure. They were present the way weather is present — a condition of the landscape, not an actor within it. The Order watched nothing because there was, by its own framework, nothing to watch.

**The development period.** The displaced non-attuned population does not find wasteland in the Vein-dense territories — they find environments that are strange, intense, and in some ways extraordinarily productive. The ambient field energy feeds the ecology. Strange plants. Unusual animals. Abundant growth alongside real danger. They cannot access the field instinctually, but they can observe what it does — to their instruments, their crops, their architecture, their bodies. They live adjacent to the substrate, handle it, build with it, notice what it does. Scientific understanding accumulates slowly through necessity rather than ambition. It is not one inventor's breakthrough but a multigenerational process of observation and experiment that eventually crosses the threshold into practical application — the threshold being the development of instruments sensitive enough to induce and measure state changes in substrate without direct field manipulation. The founding scientists and inventors of this period are the Coalition's most celebrated historical figures — and the attuned individuals among them have been quietly erased from the official record.

**The liberation war.** Three to four generations before the story begins. The non-attuned population — by now a developed society with its own culture, institutions, and a systematic relationship to the Vein — rises against the Order's political and social dominance. This is the founding conflict of the Coalition, and it began as a genuinely just cause: a subjugated people asserting the right to exist on equal terms. The Order's response was the response of a comfortable ruling class that could not believe the people below it were capable of serious threat — disproportionate, shocked, and ultimately unsuccessful. The Order lost more than it expected to. The Coalition won more than it was prepared for.

The Order's military disadvantage was not a failure of capability — their practitioners remained individually formidable throughout. It was a failure of geography. The Coalition was entrenched inside Vein-dense territories that were biologically hostile to attuned people. Attacking well-defended positions inside territory where your fighting force faces overexposure risk with every advance is not a winnable proposition regardless of individual superiority. The Order's capabilities could not be deployed where they were needed most. The Coalition did not need to match the Order's practitioners — it needed to hold ground the Order could not safely take, and it could. The war ended in partition because the Order could not dislodge an entrenched force from territory its own biology made hostile, not because the Coalition achieved parity.

What the Coalition brought to the war was not a separate weapons program built on top of its extraction technology — it was the extraction technology itself, reoriented. The apparatus developed across generations to hold substrate open, induce state changes, and extract at industrial scale was, from first principles, a system for forcing rapid and violent transitions in matter. It had been pointed at substrate because substrate was what the Coalition needed to process. Pointing it at the Order's extraction sites, its infrastructure, and the physical environments its practitioners depended on required doctrine and intent, not new technology. Coalition apparatus could destabilize substrate at a site faster than attuned practitioners could stabilize it, and it could do so from ranges the Order's practitioners — trained for direct field manipulation, not standoff engagement — had no framework to counter. The Order's individual superiority was neutralized not by matching it but by operating through methods it could not answer. The partition settled where each side's reach ended: the Coalition's apparatus defined a frontier the Order could not push through at acceptable cost; the Order's practitioners defined a frontier the Coalition could not push through at acceptable cost. The line between those two limits became the border.

The Coalition's industrial extraction at scale — the kind that meaningfully depletes field concentration and with it the biological barrier — came after partition, not during it. The liberation war's apparatus use was offensive and disruptive: destabilizing Order sites, destroying infrastructure, denying territory. It did not remove substrate. The systematic extraction that built the Coalition's peacetime economy, and that has been quietly eroding its own territorial advantage ever since, began once there was territory to administer and an economy to build.

**The aftermath and the current era.** The liberation war ended in a settlement neither side fully accepted — a partition of territories, a set of agreements that have been broken and renegotiated multiple times, a political architecture that was always a ceasefire rather than a peace. In the generations since, the military calculus has shifted. The two curves have been converging: attuned tolerance for high-concentration exposure increasing through biological adaptation and cultural practice, while Coalition extraction has been reducing intensity at worked sites. The biological barrier that made Coalition territory impenetrable during the liberation war is lower now than it was then, and it continues to drop. What was a Coalition geographic fortress is becoming contested ground. The conflict is no longer one of entrenched geographic advantage against superior individual capability — it is moving toward genuine power parity between matched forces with different strengths. That convergence is part of what makes the current war more existential than previous ones. The original conditions that produced the partition no longer fully hold.

Both factions have fractured internally across this period. The original ideological clarity of the founding conflict has blurred into power struggles, generational disconnects, and the moral complications that come from sustained institutions doing sustained things in the name of a founding cause they increasingly only reference rather than embody. ## The state of the world at the story's beginning

The founding liberation war is three to four generations past — historical rather than living memory for most characters. Veterans do not exist. What exists instead are grandchildren and great-grandchildren who were taught what it meant, which means both factions have had generations to mythologize, revise, and argue over what the founding conflict actually was and whether the current situation honors it.

The current war has a specific beginning that characters can remember or have been told about directly. It is not eternal — it has a cause, a trigger, and a shape that the characters relate to personally.

The trigger was an accident. Five children — three Coalition-registered, two Order-side with no equivalent documentation — were playing with a degraded Coalition apparatus left in genuinely ambiguous territory: ground the Order had claimed implicitly through repeated military dominance, but where Coalition operational history was evidenced by the apparatus itself. Two children died: one from each side, both attuned. The cause was an unknown interaction between the attuned children and the deteriorated equipment. No patrol was involved. No attack was ordered. No one intended it.

Neither faction's public account reflects this. The Order amplified the attuned deaths — Coalition technology killed attuned children, a framing the religious arm carried with full institutional weight. The Coalition amplified the territorial dimension — Order children, and attuned ones at that, present in contested ground — an encroachment narrative that displaced the question of who left dangerous materiel there and avoided the more difficult fact that their own dead child was attuned. That detail was never officially acknowledged. It became a folk rumor in border communities: known informally, institutionally invisible, never confirmed. The three surviving children hold the account that neither side's version contains. What happens to that account — and to them — is unresolved.

The escalation that followed the incident was institutional rather than inevitable. Someone saw the opportunity the incident created and moved. Who that was — a founding family, a field commander acting on field autonomy, the religious arm — is not settled history.

Both factions contain overlapping fractures: ideological disputes about the war's prosecution, generational divisions between those who remember the current war's beginning and those born into it, and opportunistic power struggles that use the war as cover. The conflict is not simply bilateral — it is several overlapping conflicts wearing factional colors.

Neither side has a path to permanent victory — only to more favorable configurations of a conflict that the underlying conditions continue to produce. Every settlement has been a pause. The current war will end the same way the last one did: not in resolution but in exhaustion, advantage, or manufactured consensus that the people in power need the fighting to stop. What gets called victory will depend entirely on who is left standing to name it.

The protagonist and their companions exist somewhere in this fracture landscape.

## Geography — the continent

The world is Earth-sized. One continent is relevant to the story's conflict; other landmasses exist but are outside the scope of the current war.

**The Vein layer.** The Vein exists as a geological stratum within the planet's crust, present across the continent at variable depth. It becomes accessible — surfaces, in practical terms — where tectonic processes, erosion, or glaciation have brought it within workable depth. The mechanism by which it surfaces is independent of mountain ranges: mountains are one exposure mechanism among several. Mountain ranges and thrust uplifts, rift valleys, ancient erosion surfaces, glacially scoured shield terrain, coastal cliff erosion, river gorges, and impact structures all produce surfacing zones. Mountains correlate with Vein exposure only where the layer is also shallow in that region; mountains in areas where the layer runs deep produce no significant Vein exposure regardless of their height. The continent has multiple mountain ranges of varying scale and geological age; their Vein significance varies independently of their size — a major range can be Vein-poor if the layer beneath it sits deep, while a modest coastal uplift can be Vein-intense if the layer there is shallow.

Field concentration at any surfacing zone is a function of depth. Where the layer surfaces shallow, concentration is high — biologically hostile to attuned practitioners, and the primary zone of Coalition economic and military advantage. Where the layer surfaces deeper, concentration is lower and Order practitioners can work it. This is the geological basis of faction territory: the Coalition holds the high-concentration zones the Order cannot safely enter; the Order holds the lower-concentration zones and the stable interior where the layer does not surface at workable depth at all. Neither faction's territory is uniformly settled or governed — both zones contain wilderness, independent communities, and stretches where factional administration is thin or absent. Faction territory designations describe operational range and biological access, not administrative control.

**Climate.** The continent's north-south axis runs from arctic and subarctic conditions in the north through temperate zones to Mediterranean and equatorial conditions in the south. The west-east axis runs from maritime conditions on the western coast — ocean-moderated, wetter, milder — to continental conditions in the east, with more extreme seasonal temperature swings and drier climate. These gradients are independent of faction territory but shape the character of each region.

**The seven regions.**

*Western margin.* Coalition safe zone. The continent's active geological margin, facing the western ocean. High Vein concentration where the layer surfaces along coastal uplifts, fault zones, and associated features. Maritime climate. The Coalition's most defensible core: field concentration remains hostile enough to Order practitioners that the biological barrier has not significantly eroded here, and the terrain has been Coalition-settled over generations.

*Southern belt.* Coalition safe zone. Elevated terrain running east-west across the continent's southern extent, where the Vein layer surfaces extensively across mountain uplifts and related geological features. Highest field concentrations on the continent overall. Equatorial climate at lower elevations, Mediterranean at the approaches. Order operational presence is negligible — the biological barrier here is the strongest remaining on the continent.

*Contested corridor.* The active war zone. L-shaped: a vertical arm running north-south along the eastern edge of the western margin, and a horizontal arm running east-west along the northern edge of the southern belt. This is the territory where Coalition extraction has lowered field concentration enough for Order practitioners to operate — the original geographic lock is gone. The front runs through this corridor. Its boundaries are not fixed political lines but depletion boundaries that advance inward as extraction continues at worked sites. Where the front is at any given time depends on when the concentration at a given site crossed the threshold; the corridor's shape maps the history of Coalition extraction.

*Order interior.* Order operational range. The continental heartland: stable geology, deep Vein layer inaccessible without major infrastructure, agricultural land, established cities. The Order works Vein sites in its eastern mountain ranges, where the layer surfaces at moderate concentration — workable by practitioners, not hostile. The founding families' political authority is strongest here. This is the Order's administrative and cultural core.

*Northern territories.* Order-claimed, thinly administered. Arctic and subarctic terrain; ancient glacially-scoured shield geology with low Vein concentration where the layer surfaces. The northern coast faces a cold arctic sea — the Order's northernmost coastal access, harsh and barely navigable, useful as a defensive flank but not as a trade or projection route. The Order claims this territory and maintains nominal infrastructure but does not govern it seriously — too remote, too marginal, insufficient Vein yield to justify the investment. The consequence of thin administration is predictable: religious arm outposts, independent communities, and organizations that require distance from central Order authority have established themselves here. The territory functions simultaneously as a strategic reserve — fallback ground if the front collapses significantly.

*Eastern seaboard.* Order operational range, conventionally administered. The continent's eastern coastal margin, facing the eastern sea. Continental to Mediterranean climate running south. The Order maintains scattered settlements here — fishing towns, minor ports, provincial outposts — but has not invested in maritime infrastructure. The coast is economically marginal within the Order's hierarchy. Isolationist policy means that what could be a significant trade coast is instead lightly occupied and lightly administered.

*SW coastal enclave.* Order-claimed, effectively autonomous. A significant settlement on the continent's southwestern coast, separated from Order interior territory by the Coalition's southern mountain belt. Unreachable overland without crossing Coalition or contested ground; accessible from the rest of the Order only by sea around the continent's southern tip. The Order's largest coastal settlement by population, but not by institutional priority. Its size is a consequence of neglect: the founding families never invested serious administrative attention in a city they could not easily reach, which created conditions for growth outside their framework. Lower attuned population density than Order interior cities. Informal power structures the Order's formal hierarchy does not govern. The coastal strip at the base of the southern mountain belt — if navigable at sea level — is one of the most strategically significant routes in the current war, connecting the enclave to the contested corridor.

**The front.** The active front follows the inner boundary of the contested corridor — the depletion threshold at which field concentration has dropped enough for Order practitioners to operate. It is not a static political line but a depletion boundary that advances inward as Coalition extraction continues at worked sites. The war is fought along this boundary; the Coalition's peacetime economy has been, through its ordinary operation, continuously generating new front.

## The world's technology — two tracks

The Coalition and the Order exist at a technology level that maps roughly to the band between early industrial and inter-war in real-world terms. Iron and steel construction. Coal as the dominant energy source, with internal combustion beginning to appear at the margins. Rail as the backbone of long-distance movement, horses still common for local transport and military logistics. Bolt-action or early semi-automatic weapons. Artillery as the dominant battlefield force. Telegraph and early telephone for communication. Surgery with anaesthetic but no antibiotics — infection kills more soldiers than wounds. Cities have gas or early electric lighting and running water in wealthier districts; the countryside lags a generation behind.

This is the baseline for both factions on what might be called the normal track — the material technology that develops independently of the Vein, through ordinary metallurgy, construction, agriculture, and industry. The Order and the Coalition are at roughly the same place on this track, for the same reason: both descend from the same pre-conflict civilization, and neither has had reason to develop general technology faster than the other.

The Coalition has a second track running in parallel. Call it the Vein track. It is not uniform — the acceleration concentrates wherever substrate interaction gave the development-period population empirical shortcuts that normal material science wouldn't have found yet. The field moves matter along the energy continuum: a population living adjacent to active substrate observed phase transitions, anomalous structural behavior, and state changes their normal tech level had no framework for but could see and eventually reproduce. Materials science is the first domain that accelerated, because the Vein was simultaneously the laboratory and the instrument. Precision instrumentation followed, because the apparatus requires calibration and sensitivity that bled into other domains. Energy concentration at extraction sites followed from that.

The gap between the two tracks is real, visible, and structural. Vein sites feel like a different century from the towns surrounding them. The infrastructure is stranger, more precise, harder to read as industrial in the familiar sense. Ordinary Coalition citizens experience this as texture — the weird work happens out there — without a framework for why. Order observers who are paying attention find it unsettling: the apparatus is anachronistically sophisticated relative to everything else the Coalition builds, and the gap is widening. The industry giants and the research frontier experience it as a political fault line, for reasons that are not identical but that point at the same underlying fact.

**The Vein currently functions as fuel.** It is a located, depletable resource. Power concentrates at extraction sites. Substrate must be worked where it is found; the energy does not distribute the way electricity distributes through wire. The Coalition's economy is built entirely on this model, which is why the extraction infrastructure exists at the scale it does, and why the industry giants' position is worth protecting.

The research frontier is pursuing something different without having fully named it: an instrumental relationship to the field phenomenon that does not require consuming the substrate to extract value from it. If that work succeeds, substrate stops being coal and starts being something closer to a power grid. Extraction infrastructure becomes obsolete. The Order's strategic rationale — that individual practitioners are irreplaceable because the field requires a biological interface — also collapses, differently and more completely than the Order has modeled.

Neither side has articulated this as the stakes of the current war. The war is being fought over territory, doctrine, and identity. The fuel-to-electricity transition is the thing underneath all of that — the reason the current conflict is more existential than previous ones, not because anyone planned it that way, but because the research trajectory is now close enough that its outcome is becoming legible to people who know what to look for. ## Vein-adjacent minerals

The Vein does not produce new elements. The periodic table of this world is the same as the real one. Field exposure cannot change the atomic composition of matter — only its state and energy level.

What it can do, over geological timescales, is change the conditions under which minerals form. Minerals are not just elements — they are elements in specific crystalline structures, produced under specific conditions of pressure, temperature, and chemical environment. The same element produces radically different minerals depending on formation conditions. The ambient field expression at a Vein site, persistent across deep time, means the local geology formed under conditions that do not exist anywhere else: a continuous low-level energy addition affecting pressure gradients, temperature, and the kinetics of mineral crystallization. This does not produce exotic matter. It produces real-world mineral compounds in crystalline phases that ordinarily only form under conditions this world's geology could not otherwise sustain.

These phases are not stable in ordinary environments. Remove a Vein-adjacent mineral from field proximity and it slowly transitions toward its ordinary stable configuration — the same way a stretched spring, released, returns to rest. Keep it within field proximity and it holds its high-energy structure indefinitely, maintained by the same ambient conditions that produced it.

The practical consequence is that these minerals interact with active field energy differently from ordinary rock. Their crystalline geometry already sits closer to a transition threshold than ordinary matter does. The field has less work to do to affect them. They are not substrate — they contain no accumulated field energy of their own — but they are not inert in the way that granite or iron ore is inert. A vessel lined with a Vein-adjacent mineral phase holds substrate differently than a vessel lined with ordinary stone. A tool made from one behaves differently in field-active conditions than the same tool made from its ordinary counterpart.

The development-period population encountered these minerals as a local anomaly before they had any framework for what the Vein was: a rock that held an edge longer than it should, a building material that settled differently, a mineral that behaved oddly when worked near substrate deposits. That anomaly was one of the early handles on the Vein's existence — something observable to non-attuned people during ordinary life in Vein-dense territory, before anyone had instruments or theory. The connection between those anomalous minerals and the substrate's presence was one of the threads the development-period population eventually followed toward systematic apparatus work.

The Coalition's apparatus incorporates Vein-adjacent mineral phases throughout — as vessel linings, as precision instrument surfaces, as the containment layer that makes high-intensity sustained operation possible. Whether the current-era Coalition has a fully correct theoretical account of why those materials work the way they do is a separate question. They may be running effective procedures on an incomplete theory, the way early metallurgists produced good steel long before anyone understood what carbon was doing to iron.

## The Coalition's apparatus

The apparatus is the mechanical means by which non-attuned people hold substrate open, active, and extractable without attunement. It is the foundation of the Coalition's civilization — not just an extraction tool but the physical embodiment of the Coalition's founding claim: that the Vein belongs to no one by birthright, and that systematic knowledge can do what instinctual gift does.

**The founding problem.** Substrate has a thermodynamic tendency toward closure. Its high-energy exposed state bleeds concentration into the surrounding environment, and the material resolves toward the closed state the moment active maintenance stops. An Order practitioner holds substrate open through attunement — direct biological coupling to the field, counteracting the closure gradient continuously. The Coalition has no such coupling. Their founding engineering problem was therefore: how do you hold substrate open mechanically, against its own physics, using only material systems that cannot perceive the field at all?

Everything else in the apparatus follows from this problem. The pressure system that solved it is the hardest component to build, the first thing that fails under overload, and the thing the Order cannot replicate in reverse — they have never needed a mechanical answer because they have always had a biological one.

**Core architecture.** Coalition apparatus has four functional layers.

The innermost layer is the substrate chamber, lined with silicate or oxide Vein-phase mineral. These materials perform dual roles simultaneously: they contain the substrate's field expression within the chamber while also partially conducting it into the extraction system. This dual-role behavior — containment and conduction from a single material — was the discovery that made sustained extraction viable. Before it, every early vessel design fought an irresolvable tension between insulation (which contained the field but prevented extraction) and conduction (which enabled extraction but failed to contain). Vein-phase lining dissolved that tension. Whether the development-period engineers understood why it worked or simply observed that it did is the kind of question the Coalition's historians argue about.

Surrounding the Vein-phase lining is the structural housing — granite at site scale, porcelain at component scale. Both are active insulators rather than passive ones: their dense crystalline structures resist field transmission rather than merely failing to conduct it. Granite handles the bulk physical loads and the large-scale containment that porcelain cannot form into. Porcelain handles the precision component housings that granite cannot be worked into. The pairing is not arbitrary; it reflects the scale at which each material's properties are useful.

The thermal extraction layer is the copper conduit network that draws field-induced heat from the chamber and routes it to useful work. Field energy conducted into the copper expresses as heat at the conduit's termination points, which is the primary means by which the apparatus does anything at all. Between the high-intensity chamber end and the work-output end, bronze damping stages reduce peak intensity to manageable levels — bronze conducts at roughly fifteen to twenty percent less intensity than copper, absorbing that fraction into its alloy structure rather than transmitting it. Lead thermal fuses sit at the critical transition points: when field intensity exceeds safe operating parameters, lead elements transition to liquid, absorbing the excess energy into the phase change rather than transmitting it onward. A lead fuse failing is not a malfunction — it is the system working as designed. Replacing spent lead elements is routine maintenance, as standard as topping a water tank.

Useful work emerges from the conduit network in two configurations, neither primary — different operations call for different apparatus geometries. The steam-pressure configuration routes the conduit network through a water system: field-induced heat boils water, steam drives mechanical work, and the apparatus functions as a very strange boiler. The direct-heat configuration terminates the conduit at a specific processing location, delivering concentrated field-induced heat for smelting, material transformation, or precision thermal work. The core architecture is identical between configurations; only the termination geometry differs.

**The operator interface.** Coalition apparatus operators do not perceive the field. They perceive what the field is doing to their instruments, and from that they infer field state. The interface is a reading system rather than a control system — mechanical controls for pressure, cooling rate, and extraction rate change the material conditions the field operates in, which changes field behavior indirectly. There is no direct field manipulation. Every adjustment is a material adjustment whose field consequence the operator predicts from experience.

The reading system has multiple simultaneous layers. The quartz transducer array is the primary field measurement instrument: field energy applied to quartz produces mechanical vibration proportional to intensity, which is readable as a continuous output. Mercury instruments give directional and intensity information through surface tension response — the mercury surface reads too active or strangely still in ways that map onto field direction and concentration. Tin indicator elements give threshold information: when field intensity reaches the point where tin transitions to liquid, the operator knows exactly where they are relative to the safe operating ceiling. Pyrite elements read accumulation over time — oxidation rate near active substrate scales with field intensity, and a pyrite indicator that has oxidized faster than the logged parameters suggest tells the operator the apparatus has been running harder than recorded. Glass components give time-to-failure information through progressive softening — a glass element beginning to deform is advance warning that the surrounding thermal load is approaching a threshold.

An experienced operator reads all of these simultaneously as a unified picture of field state. A novice reads them as separate, unrelated warning signs. The gap between them is not access to different information — both see the same instruments — but the pattern recognition that converts individual material behaviors into a coherent real-time model of what the field is doing inside the apparatus. This is the Coalition's mastery gradient: not attunement depth, but indicator literacy. It is a learnable skill rather than a biological one, which is precisely the point.

**The development arc.** Coalition apparatus reached its current form through three historical phases, each building on the previous without discarding it.

The first phase is the passive apparatus of the development period. Substrate held open by mechanical pressure, energy harvested through thermal conduction into metal, indicators discovered empirically through failure patterns rather than designed in. The critical measurement breakthrough of this phase was mercury: someone already working with early apparatus noticed that mercury near active substrate behaved anomalously, its surface responding to field intensity and direction in ways that made the field legible as a measurable system rather than a phenomenon. Before mercury, apparatus operation was managed by feel and by component failure. After it, operators had a real-time instrument. This is the transition from craft to systematic science — not a single invention but a single material that made the field readable for the first time.

The second phase is the active apparatus of the liberation war era. Quartz transducer arrays added real-time field measurement to the passive thermal system. Feedback-controlled mechanical components — valves, pressure regulators, damping stages — were designed to respond to transducer readings rather than being set once and left. The apparatus could now anticipate and react to field fluctuations rather than only responding to failures. This is the apparatus that fought the liberation war: a mature version of the passive system with measurement and active response layered on top.

The third phase is the combined system that the current research frontier is developing. The quartz transducer array has two operating directions: forward (field energy producing mechanical vibration — measurement) and reverse (mechanical stress on quartz producing directed field perturbation — expression). The combined system runs both simultaneously — measuring field state through forward transduction while perturbing it through coordinated reverse transduction across multiple array elements in specific geometric configurations. The result is a feedback loop: measure, perturb, measure the response, adjust the perturbation. The apparatus can now shape field behavior rather than only harvesting its thermal expression.

This is not attunement. The hard ceiling of the combined system is mechanical response time. An Order master perceives the field continuously and adjusts in real time — the coupling is biological and instantaneous. The Coalition's combined system samples, computes, and actuates in discrete steps, each requiring the time mechanical systems need. At large scale and low intensity the gap is negligible: Coalition apparatus matches or exceeds what Order practitioners can produce. At fine grain and high intensity — the surgery end of the spectrum — the gap is the difference between a felt continuous sense and a clockwork approximation of one. The Coalition excels where scale is the variable. The Order excels where precision at the human level is the variable. Neither obsoletes the other, which is what makes the current war unresolvable by capability development alone.

The strategic consequence of the Phase 3 work is not that extraction infrastructure becomes obsolete. The sites still matter, the substrate still matters, apparatus is still required. What becomes obsolete is uncontrolled passive extraction specifically — the boiler and forge model that the industry giants' infrastructure is built around. The transition is not from extraction to something else but from extraction-only to extraction-plus-direction, restructuring who controls the sites and what they can do with them. This is why the industry giants are investing in slowing the research frontier down. They are not protecting against being replaced. They are protecting against being restructured, which is a subtler position and a more defensible one.

**The Coalition capability arc.** Coalition capabilities tend to develop in a characteristic sequence: fixed installation first, then vehicular, then personal scale. The stationary installation is typically the most powerful instance of any given capability — the largest energy budget, the most apparatus, the least weight constraint. The personal version is the least powerful but the most deployable. Each stage of miniaturization is earned by everything learned at the previous scale, and each stage costs something: the personal version of any Coalition capability is always working against a weight budget that the installation version does not face.

This is a strong tendency, not an absolute rule. Exceptions exist and are valid when they have good reasons. But every case where a Coalition capability reaches personal scale before installation scale, or skips a stage, warrants examination — the arc reflects the Coalition's fundamental infrastructural nature, and departures from it need to be explained by the specific physics or economics of that capability, not assumed. The most powerful Coalition asset is almost always the one that doesn't move.

**What it is like to be at a working Vein site.** The experience of a Coalition extraction site is not mystical. It is industrial. The dominant sensory register is thermal and mechanical, not uncanny.

The soundscape of a working site is layered and legible to anyone who has spent time there. Quartz transducer installations emit a faint continuous resonance during high-intensity operation — below hearing threshold at normal distances, audible as a low persistent tone when you are close to the array. Copper and iron conduits expanding and contracting under thermal cycling produce rhythmic ticking that experienced personnel use to gauge heat load without checking instruments. The water cooling system contributes a constant background of moving fluid and periodic steam venting — the sound changes character when pressure builds, and workers notice the change before any gauge registers it. Glass indicator panels under stress produce faint cracking. Pyrite elements oxidizing near active substrate hiss softly. A character who has worked at Vein sites long enough orients by sound before they see anything — they hear what normal sounds like, which means they hear trouble before it shows on indicators.

The thermal environment is the primary physical hazard for non-attuned workers. Copper conduits run too hot to touch during operation. Air shimmers near active apparatus. Work schedules rotate around heat load — certain maintenance tasks are only possible during cooldown cycles when component temperatures drop to something manageable. Protective clothing at a Vein site is not field protection. Non-attuned workers have no field exposure risk at ordinary intensities. The protection is against burns, steam, and the thermal signature of metal components that have been conducting field energy for hours. Physical exhaustion at a working site is thermal and mechanical — workers are hot, sweaty, thirsty, managing burn risk. The labor is demanding in the way foundry or boilerhouse labor is demanding, not in the way combat is demanding.

The visual layer is the slowest but the most information-rich. Tin fittings that have crossed the field intensity threshold look different from those that haven't — a color and texture change that persists after the fact. Pyrite elements accumulate oxidation in patterns that record long-term exposure history. Glass indicator panels develop stress patterns — surface crazing, opacity changes, hairline fractures — that map the thermal and field load the installation has absorbed over time. Lead fuses that have been replaced tell you where the apparatus has been pushed hard. A person who knows what to look for can read the operational history of an installation from its material condition without touching anything, without asking anyone, and without access to the official logs.

**Operator expertise.** The three levels of apparatus operator knowledge represent genuinely different relationships to the same physical system.

A novice operator knows procedures: which indicator states require immediate shutdown, how to execute standard operating sequences, what individual indicators mean when read in isolation, and the basic thermal safety rules that prevent burn injuries. Their relationship to the apparatus is rote — they know what to do, not why.

An experienced operator knows the apparatus as a system. They read the full indicator array as a unified picture of field state rather than as a collection of separate warning signs — quartz transducer output, tin color, pyrite accumulation, glass stress, mercury surface all feeding a single integrated sense of what the field is doing and where the installation stands relative to its operating limits. They know the early warning signs that precede indicator thresholds: tin coloration that precedes liquid transition, glass stress patterns that develop before visible crazing, mercury behavior that shifts before intensity crosses a measurement band. They know the specific quirks of the installations they work — this unit runs hot in the third conduit run, that one has a sluggish pressure response at high bronze loading. Their relationship to the apparatus is relational — they know this machine, not just machines in general.

A master operator knows why. They understand the material physics behind every component behavior — why bronze damps at the rate it does in a given configuration, why the field expresses where it does in a given geometry, why a particular failure cascade follows the sequence it follows. They can predict which component fails next in a degradation pattern before any indicator registers the problem. They can substitute materials when a component is unavailable and know exactly what the substitution costs in terms of performance and safety margin. They can coax maximum performance from an installation while staying inside failure thresholds, because they understand where those thresholds actually are rather than where the procedures say they are. A master operator reads matter the way an Order practitioner reads the field — as a continuous present-tense signal rather than a set of categories to check.

The gap between novice and master is not attunement or any biological gift. It is accumulated material knowledge, pattern recognition trained across years of operation, and the willingness to understand rather than just follow. This means the gap is teachable and reproducible in a way the Order's mastery gradient is not — which is both the Coalition's advantage and, to the Order, the most unsettling thing about them.

**Material-state intelligence.** The legibility of apparatus operational history from material condition has consequences that extend beyond maintenance and safety. It means installations cannot conceal what they have been doing from anyone who knows how to read them.

The Coalition's military and regulatory apparatus has formal inspection protocols built entirely on this principle. An inspector arriving at a site after an incident does not need testimony. Glass indicator panels showing sustained high-intensity stress terminated abruptly tell a different story than panels showing normal operating wear — and the difference is visible without touching anything. Pyrite accumulation inconsistent with logged operating hours means either the logs are falsified or something happened that wasn't logged. Lead fuses replaced more recently than maintenance records indicate means someone was pushing the apparatus to overload and covering the evidence. Material condition is harder to falsify than documentation because every replacement leaves its own trace, and experienced inspectors know what replaced indicators look like versus aged ones.

Order field operatives who understand apparatus mechanics read Coalition sites as tactical intelligence. Indicator wear patterns reveal which installations have been running at high intensity recently, which have been idle, and which have been pushed beyond their design parameters — information that maps Coalition operational priorities without requiring any intelligence source inside the installation. A site that shows heavy recent wear on the extraction conduits and freshly replaced lead fuses was doing something significant in the last operational cycle. A site whose glass panels show no stress accumulation despite being listed as active in Order intelligence estimates has either been inactive or has had its indicators replaced to conceal activity. Neither possibility is neutral information.

The glass indicator panel is the most readable of all: its failure mode is progressive rather than threshold-crossing, which means it accumulates a continuous record of stress rather than simply flipping states. An experienced reader can estimate how many high-intensity cycles an installation has run, how recently it ran hard, and whether the stress pattern is consistent with the official operational profile. Coalition personnel with access to indicator replacement supplies and the knowledge to use them can interfere with this record — but replacement itself leaves traces, and the absence of expected wear on old components is as informative as the presence of unexpected wear on new ones.

## What is not yet decided

These are genuine open questions, not oversights. They will be resolved as the world develops.

- What geological conditions produce Vein sites, and what does that imply about site distribution, site proximity, and whether new sites could form or be artificially created? The current placeholder — "something in the deep geology" — will cause problems when writing about site discovery, contested territories, and Coalition expansion strategy.
- What does Vein overexposure look like from the outside — what do observers see when a practitioner is being harmed by the field? The internal mechanism is established; the external presentation is not.
- What is the industry giants' full operational picture — who specifically leads them, how they are organized, what their relationship to the constitutional court and regional governments looks like, and how their sabotage operations actually function? They are the Coalition's closest equivalent to the Order's secret societies in terms of operating beneath the visible power structure and warrant equivalent development before character work involving them begins.
- What is the secret societies' full operational picture — their internal hierarchy, how members are recruited and what they know about each other, how they communicate across the Order's branches, and what their relationship to the founding catastrophe history actually is? The broad strokes are established. The detail needed to write characters who are members, who encounter them, or who are their targets requires a dedicated session before that character work begins.
- What is the Order's institutional age and the world's deep history timeline? The desensitization mechanism established in the attunement section — whether it is biological, cultural, or both — depends on this decision. Biological evolutionary pressure requires deep time; cultural and technical loss can operate across centuries. Do not resolve the desensitization mechanism's nature until the timeline is established.
- What was the mechanism of the bloodline consolidation — specifically, how was genealogical rewriting coordinated at the scale required to make attunement appear hereditary across the whole Order? This implies organizational continuity and capability that sits in tension with the founding families' current description as a shifting coalition. Whether the consolidation was performed by a predecessor institution, an earlier and more unified family bloc, or something else has implications for the Order's deep institutional history and should be resolved alongside the timeline question.
- What are the final names of the factions, the resource, and attunement?
- Are there people or groups outside the two main factions — neutral territories, a third party, displaced populations within each faction's borders?
- What specific atrocities has each side committed, and how does each faction officially relate to them?
- What does daily life look like for ordinary people on each side, beneath the level of the elite?
- What is the extent and mechanism of attunement in wildlife — the possibility is confirmed, the details are not yet established.
- What are the exotic states of matter beyond plasma, and when does the Coalition's research frontier reach them relative to the story's timeline?
- At high matter form grades — supercompressed gas phase and above — can refined substrate recreate ambient field intensity equivalent to a natural high-concentration site? If so, this would affect the biological barrier that defines Coalition territorial advantage. Flag for military doctrine development before personal-scale gas-phase substrate is written into active use.
- Does gas-phase substrate at personal scale converge with the exotic state research frontier? Both are approaching the same energy threshold from different directions. Whether they meet at personal scale within story-relevant timescales is a horizon question — the same category as transmutation, a potential story endpoint rather than a current capability.
