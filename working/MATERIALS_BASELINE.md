## The governing principle (FIXED)
The field moves matter along the energy continuum — toward greater energy/freedom or lesser energy/constraint.
This is the same operation regardless of what matter is being acted on.
Variety of effects comes from variety of matter, not from the field doing different things.

## Material responses — what is established

### Stone / earth (FIXED)
- Energy addition: thermal expansion → surface spalling at high intensity → liquefaction at extreme
- Energy removal: contraction → embrittlement → fracture under stresses warm rock would absorb

### Metal (FIXED)
- Conducts added energy rapidly through its structure
- Functions as a natural conduit
- Energy pushed into one end expresses at the other
- Implication: detectable at distance from substrate — no direct contact required

### Water (FIXED)
- Dramatic volume change at state transition: liquid → steam = ~1700× expansion
- Energy removal produces ice
- Present everywhere: soil, air humidity, living tissue
- Functions as efficient intermediary for energy effects at scale
- Not a Vein material — a passive intermediary

### Air (FIXED)
- Localized energy addition → pressure differentials → airflow
- High intensity → concussive effects
- Creates conditions for combustion if fuel present

### Living tissue (FIXED)
- Low intensity addition: stimulates biological processes, healing, capacity increase
- Higher intensity: regulatory mechanisms fail, damage from inside
- Interacts with the system's own energy management processes

## Constraints that must survive any new material decisions

1. The field is invisible — no sensory access for non-attuned people except consequences
2. Secondary effects scale with concentration — stronger near dense substrate
3. Coalition instruments can detect at extraction concentrations, nothing below
4. Effects cascade — one material affects adjacent materials
5. Non-attuned people are not harmed at ordinary field intensities (no biological antenna)
6. Coalition apparatus must be buildable by a non-attuned population using observed secondary effects as their starting data

## Vein effect profiles — decided

### Iron (wrought)
**Conductivity:** high
**State-change threshold:** med (~1540°C equiv.)
**Failure mode:** gradual, readable

**Low intensity:** Uniform warming along the field path from contact end to expression end. Sub-damaging — a tool held at the non-contact end becomes warm in the hand. Useful for delivering controlled heat to a specific location through an iron structure. No visible color change at this level.

**Medium intensity:** Warming becomes structural. Iron expands unevenly along the field path, producing stress at geometry changes — joints, welds, bends, thin cross-sections. These are the first failure points. Visible color change at the expression end: dull red, indistinguishable from ordinary heat. A practitioner or operator watching the expression end has a readable intensity gauge.

**High intensity:** Rapid approach to transition threshold. Liquefaction begins at the expression end while the contact end may remain solid — the material is simultaneously solid and liquid along its length. Useful for specific operations; catastrophically dangerous if uncontrolled. The expression end runs liquid while the contact end is still held or clamped.

**Failure mode:** Gradual and readable. Iron gives warning through color change and visible deformation before catastrophic failure. This is why it is the workhorse apparatus metal — it fails slowly enough to be managed by an attentive operator.

**Apparatus role:** Primary structural and conduction material. Most early Coalition apparatus is iron-based. Replaced by steel in precision components as Coalition metallurgy advances, but never fully displaced at infrastructure scale.

**Story texture:** An iron apparatus component under sustained field exposure smells like a forge even when no fire is present. The expression end is always the hot end. Operators learn to read iron color the way smiths do — the difference between black, dull red, bright red, and orange maps directly onto field intensity levels.


### Copper
**Conductivity:** high+
**State-change threshold:** low (~1085°C equiv.)
**Failure mode:** fast, concentrated

**Low intensity:** Expression end warms faster and more intensely than iron for the same field input. Response is nearly immediate rather than building gradually. Where iron gives warmth, copper gives heat. Already useful at low intensity for precision heat delivery.

**Medium intensity:** Expression end becomes a concentrated point of intense heat rather than uniform warming. Copper focuses field expression rather than distributing it — field energy arrives at a specific location rather than spreading through the structure. This concentration is what makes copper the precision instrument metal. The trade-off: the expression point runs hotter than the equivalent iron component at the same input, requiring more careful management.

**High intensity:** Transition threshold approached faster than iron with a compressed warning window. Color change from functional to near-transition is rapid — seconds rather than minutes. When the liquid transition comes it is clean: copper melts at the expression end without the deformation phase iron shows. The expression end simply becomes liquid.

**Failure mode:** Fast and concentrated. Short readable window compared to iron. An operator has seconds to respond to a copper component approaching failure, not minutes. Copper does not deform gracefully — it holds and then fails.

**Apparatus role:** Precision expression points, instrument tips, final delivery components. Paired with iron infrastructure — iron carries the field along the structure, copper delivers it at the target location. Most story-present apparatus is iron frame with copper terminals. Supply chain control over copper is apparatus supply chain control.

**Story texture:** Copper apparatus components run hotter at the tip than iron ones at the same field level. Experienced operators can feel the difference through insulated handles — iron warms the whole tool, copper concentrates the heat at the working end. A copper component failing without warning is the most common cause of apparatus accidents at story-present.


### Bronze (copper-tin)
**Conductivity:** high
**State-change threshold:** low-med
**Failure mode:** marginally more readable than copper

Behaves like a dampened copper — roughly 15-20% less intensity at the expression end due to tin content disrupting the copper lattice. The concentration effect is present but softer. Failure window is marginally wider than pure copper: the alloy introduces enough structural variation that slight deformation precedes melting, giving a brief visible warning pure copper doesn't provide.

**Apparatus role:** The development-period apparatus metal. Bronze was available before reliable copper supply chains existed — copper-rich enough to conduct well, workable with stone-age and early iron-age tools. The historical transition from bronze to copper expression points in Coalition apparatus tracks directly with metallurgical and supply chain development. Bronze components in old or field apparatus are a legible sign of early manufacture.


### Steel (carbon)
**Conductivity:** high
**State-change threshold:** med — higher than iron, more resilient
**Failure mode:** gradual, readable — wider safe operating range than iron

Conducts identically to iron at low intensity. Diverges at medium and high: the more uniform grain structure resists deformation longer, so geometry-failure points — bends, welds, thin sections — hold under field stress that would visibly stress iron. Same warning character as iron, same color progression, but the window starts later and lasts longer. At high intensity approaches liquefaction the same way as iron but requires more field input to reach it.

**Apparatus role:** The upgrade path from iron. Same operating logic, wider safe range. Transition from iron to steel in precision apparatus components tracks with Coalition industrial metallurgy advancing on the normal track and bleeding into the Vein track.

---

### Cast iron
**Conductivity:** high
**Failure mode:** catastrophic, no deformation warning

Conducts well but brittle — fractures rather than deforms under field stress. Skips the deformation warning phase entirely. Infrastructure and housing use only. Never used for apparatus components subject to field surges.


### Silver
**Conductivity:** high+
**State-change threshold:** low
**Failure mode:** fast, concentrated — same character as copper

Near-identical field behavior to copper. The distinction is stability: silver does not corrode or degrade under chemical exposure or humidity, so a silver component behaves the same on day one and year ten. Used where copper would degrade over time — high-humidity environments, chemical exposure, apparatus that cannot be regularly serviced. Rarer and more expensive than copper; reserved for components where long-term behavioral consistency justifies the cost.

---

### Gold
**Conductivity:** high
**State-change threshold:** low
**Failure mode:** fast, concentrated — slightly less intense than copper

Slightly lower peak conductivity than copper but fully non-corroding under any conditions encountered at story-present. The calibration metal — a gold component is the reference point everything else is measured against. Used for precision standards, critical junction points, and any component where behavioral consistency over the full operational life of the apparatus matters more than peak performance. The cost means gold appears only at the most critical nodes of high-value apparatus.


### Tin
**Conductivity:** med
**State-change threshold:** low (~232°C equiv.) — lowest of any common story-present metal
**Failure mode:** visible, predictable, non-catastrophic

Transitions to liquid at the lowest field intensity of any common metal. Useless as a structural or conduction material for this reason — but uniquely valuable as an indicator. A tin component showing transition signs means the field is at a known, calibrated level. Built-in intensity gauge. Apparatus incorporating tin indicator elements gives operators a readable warning before iron or copper components approach their own thresholds.

**Apparatus role:** Field intensity indicator. Tin elements are placed at known positions in apparatus specifically to transition first and visibly — the operator sees tin going liquid and knows exactly where the field is. The cheapest and most reliable measurement tool available at story-present.

---

### Lead
**Conductivity:** med
**State-change threshold:** very low (~327°C equiv.)
**Failure mode:** absorptive — the phase change consumes field energy

Transitions to liquid at low field intensity, slightly above tin. The key property: the phase change itself — solid to liquid — consumes field energy rather than transmitting it. A lead component transitioning under field overload absorbs the excess energy into the phase transition, protecting adjacent components. Effectively a field fuse: sacrificial, replaceable, absorbs the surge.

**Apparatus role:** Field fuse and surge absorber. Lead elements placed at vulnerable points in apparatus transition under overload conditions, consuming excess field energy before it reaches copper or iron components. Replacing a lead fuse element is routine maintenance. Replacing a copper expression point that failed without protection is not.

**Story texture:** Experienced apparatus operators carry spare lead elements the way a smith carries spare tools. A pouch of small lead components — rods, discs, formed pieces — is as standard as any other apparatus kit item.


### Zinc
**Conductivity:** med
**State-change threshold:** low (~420°C equiv.)
**Failure mode:** transitional — zinc layer fails first, iron beneath protected

Moderate conductivity, low threshold. On its own, unremarkable — transitions to liquid under modest field intensity without the precision of copper or the structural utility of iron. Its significance is in combination.

**Galvanized iron composite response:** A zinc coat on iron creates a layered field response. The zinc layer, having a lower transition threshold, reaches its state change before the iron beneath. Under field overload the zinc transitions first — absorbing energy into the phase change and partially protecting the iron core. Not as controlled as a deliberate lead fuse, but a real effect. Galvanized iron is incidentally field-resistant in a way that preceded any understanding of why.

**Historical implication:** The development-period population galvanized iron for corrosion resistance, not field protection. They would have noticed that galvanized components lasted longer under apparatus conditions than ungalvanized ones without being able to explain it. The field-protection property was an accidental discovery, probably attributed to the galvanizing process in general rather than to the zinc specifically. Whether the current-era Coalition has correctly identified the mechanism is an open question.

---

### Phosphorus
**Conductivity:** low (white/red) — semi-conductive potential (black, not yet story-present)
**State-change threshold:** near zero (white) — spontaneous combustion amplified by field
**Forms:** white (reactive, dangerous), red (stable), black (semi-conductive, frontier)

**The glow:** White phosphorus produces cold light through slow oxidation — the real-world source of will-o'-the-wisp phenomena. Field energy accelerates this oxidation reaction. Phosphorus compounds near active substrate glow more intensely than they should, without heat or flame. Not a new mechanism — existing chemistry amplified by field presence. A Vein site with phosphorus in local geology is faintly luminescent at night, visible at a distance, requiring no instruments and no attunement.

**Discovery path implication:** This is a strong candidate for the first observable signal that told the development-period population something was different about Vein-dense ground. Light from the earth at night, concentrated near substrate deposits, visible before any framework for the Vein existed. The will-o'-the-wisp as the founding observation of Coalition science.

**Military hazard:** White phosphorus weapons exist at story-present — early industrial / inter-war band. Field energy near active substrate or Order practitioners amplifies the combustion reaction unpredictably. Coalition forces using phosphorus munitions near Order-active positions are amplifying their own weapons. Order practitioners near Coalition phosphorus stores face a specific threat that conventional field doctrine doesn't account for.

**The structural argument:** White → red → black phosphorus mirrors the carbon argument exactly. Same element, three structural configurations, radically different properties. White: reactive, glowing, dangerous. Red: stable, ordinary. Black: semi-conductive, layered like graphite — the frontier form. Phosphorus independently confirms that structure governs field behavior as much as composition does. Black phosphorus at the research frontier is another route toward the semiconductor question and the fuel-to-electricity transition.

## Vein-adjacent phases — framework (decided)

The four Vein-adjacent phases are not production materials. They are research materials — geographically anchored to sites, unstable when removed, and irreplaceable as the empirical foundation of Coalition apparatus development. Their role is to demonstrate what is possible. Everything the Coalition builds at scale is an attempt to replicate in portable stable materials what the phases showed could be done.

**The phases as a complete early toolkit:**
Each phase solved a specific engineering problem that would otherwise have taken generations of normal material development to work around:

- Sulfide phase: the accidental entry point — found while mining copper and iron, not while searching for field materials. Compresses the starting point of Coalition apparatus development.
- Silicate phase: containment that participates in the field circuit. Removes the hardest early vessel design problem — how to hold substrate open inside a container without the container being a field dead zone.
- Carbonate phase: self-reinforcing insulation. The more intense the field inside, the better the wall resists it. Makes sustained high-intensity operation viable at an early stage.
- Oxide phase: non-metallic high conductivity. Apparatus precision and miniaturization arrive before metalworking alone could have produced them.

**Why Coalition apparatus is anachronistically sophisticated:**
Not because the Coalition is smarter or worked harder. Because the phases compressed three or four generations of normal engineering problem-solving into one. The phases are the material reason the Coalition became a technological civilization rather than just a displaced population that learned to extract substrate.

**Geographic anchoring as strategic constraint:**
Phases only exist at Vein sites and revert when removed. Coalition apparatus sophistication is therefore site-dependent — the best materials for working the resource only exist where the resource is. Sites are not just where the substrate is. They are where the enabling materials for working the substrate exist. This is a permanent strategic geography constraint.

**The democratization problem:**
The Coalition's research frontier is running a specific experimental program: find or engineer a stable material that does what the phases do without reverting. Apply field conditions intense enough to lock a material into a high-energy phase permanently, then remove it from the field and see if it holds. Every failed attempt produces a material that reverts. A successful attempt produces the democratization material — the threshold between the Vein as located fuel and the Vein as distributed power. The industry giants have every reason to suppress that success when it comes.

### Silicate Vein-phase
**Conductivity:** med
**Threshold:** low — pre-stressed lattice
**Stability:** reverts slowly when removed from field proximity
**Tech unlock:** combined containment and conduction in a single material

Ordinary containment ceramics are low- insulators — they resist field transmission entirely, which means a ceramic vessel is a field dead zone. Silicate Vein-phase conducts partially while also containing — the vessel lining becomes part of the field circuit rather than a passive barrier. This removes the foundational engineering problem of early apparatus: how to keep substrate active inside a container. The silicate phase vessel participates in keeping it active.

**Apparatus role:** Primary vessel lining material at Vein sites. Not portable — must be worked and used at the site. The knowledge of how to configure silicate-lined vessels is portable; the material itself is not.

**Research implication:** Silicate Vein-phase demonstrated that a single material can perform dual roles — containment and conduction simultaneously. The Coalition has been trying to replicate this in stable portable materials ever since. No stable equivalent exists at story-present.

---

### Oxide Vein-phase
**Conductivity:** med-high
**Threshold:** low
**Stability:** reverts when removed, faster than silicate phase
**Tech unlock:** non-metallic high conductivity — precision and miniaturization

The most apparatus-relevant phase. Achieves near-copper conductivity as a mineral rather than a metal — meaning it can be ground, shaped, applied as a coating, formed into thin films and complex geometries that metalworking cannot produce. This is what makes site-based apparatus visibly more sophisticated than anything built elsewhere: the expression points, instrument surfaces, and precision elements are mineral rather than metal, taking forms that look almost manufactured rather than forged.

**Apparatus role:** Precision expression surfaces, instrument faces, complex internal geometries at Vein sites. The material that makes early Coalition apparatus look anachronistically precise. Rarer than silicate phase — not every site has it in workable quantities.

**Research implication:** Oxide Vein-phase demonstrated that high conductivity does not require metal. The Coalition's semiconductor research path — currently theoretical — is downstream of this demonstration. Black phosphorus and eventually silicon are the portable stable materials that might replicate what oxide phase showed was possible.

---

### Sulfide Vein-phase
**Conductivity:** med
**Threshold:** low-med
**Stability:** reverts gradually, similar rate to silicate
**Tech unlock:** the accidental entry point — found before it was sought

Co-occurs with copper and iron ore deposits. A development-period population mining for metal encounters sulfide Vein-phase in the same excavation without looking for it. The first field-responsive mineral the Coalition encountered was found through ordinary extraction activity, not through deliberate investigation of the Vein. This compressed the starting point of apparatus development significantly — the Coalition had its first anomalous mineral before it had any framework for anomalous minerals.

**Apparatus role:** Secondary conduction and bridging material at sites. Less precise than oxide phase, less stable than silicate. Historically significant as the entry point. Current-era apparatus uses it where oxide phase is unavailable.

**Historical implication:** The sulfide phase discovery is probably the moment the development-period population first understood that the ground near substrate produced materials with unusual properties — not just unusual ecology and unusual substrate behavior, but unusual rocks. This likely preceded the silicate and oxide discoveries by at least a generation.

---

### Carbonate Vein-phase
**Conductivity:** low-
**Threshold:** med
**Stability:** most stable of the four phases — reverts slowest when removed
**Tech unlock:** self-reinforcing containment — the field strengthens the insulator

Ordinary insulators degrade under sustained field exposure — the thing they're containing works against them over time. Carbonate Vein-phase inverts this: the ambient field conditions that sustain its high-energy crystalline phase also strengthen its insulating property. Higher field intensity inside the vessel means better insulation at the wall. The thing you're containing makes the container work better.

This made sustained high-intensity operations viable at an early stage. Without it, early apparatus can only run briefly at high intensity before containment degrades. With it, early apparatus can push harder and longer.

**Apparatus role:** Vessel walls, chamber linings, sustained-operation containment at Vein sites. The most stable phase means carbonate Vein-phase components last longest before reverting — an apparatus element lined with carbonate phase will hold its properties for days after removal from field proximity, longer than any other phase material. Still reverts. Just slower.

**Research implication:** Carbonate Vein-phase demonstrated that dynamic insulation — insulation that responds to and strengthens under the conditions it's insulating against — is achievable. The Coalition has no stable portable equivalent. Engineered ceramics approach static insulation; none approach dynamic insulation. This remains one of the open problems at the research frontier.

## Insulators — framework (decided)

All low- materials actively resist field transmission rather than merely failing to conduct. Differentiated by failure mode and insulation power under working conditions.

**Insulation stack — intact state:**
Rubber (peak) > Granite > Porcelain > Terracotta > Tar/pitch

**Insulation stack — sustained working conditions:**
Granite > Porcelain > Terracotta > Rubber (degraded) > Tar/pitch

**Failure modes:**
- Mechanical (granite): cracks under physical stress from surrounding materials deforming under field load. Sudden along crack lines.
- Thermal (ceramics): degrades when adjacent metal components run hot enough to affect it through ordinary heat transfer — not field transmission, just heat. Gradual and readable.
- Sustained degradation (rubber): slowly softens and loses insulating property under prolonged high-intensity exposure. Drifts from low- toward low over service life.
- Thermal/flow (tar): softens and flows under heat from adjacent components. Lowest ceiling of the group.

**Apparatus implication:** Well-designed apparatus combines both orderings. Granite and porcelain structure for sustained containment. Rubber seals at joints for flexibility and peak insulation at connection points most vulnerable to field leakage. Tar fills gaps in early apparatus before rubber is available — the presence of tar seals is a legible sign of early or field-expedient manufacture.

---

### Granite
**Conductivity:** low-
**Failure mode:** mechanical — sudden along crack lines
**Insulation rank:** second intact, first sustained

The Coalition's primary large-scale containment material. Dense crystalline structure actively resists field transmission uniformly across its mass — no grain pathways, no porosity for the field to exploit. Reliable indefinitely under sustained operation as long as physical integrity holds.

The vulnerability is mechanical rather than thermal or field-related. When surrounding materials deform under field load — iron expanding, ceramic cracking from heat — the stresses transfer into granite and find existing fault lines. A granite chamber wall that has been in service through many high-intensity operations develops micro-fractures along those lines. The insulation holds until a fracture propagates far enough to create a continuous pathway. When it fails it fails suddenly — not a gradual drift but a crack that opens a channel.

**Apparatus role:** Chamber walls, site-scale containment, the physical structure of extraction infrastructure. Not workable into complex geometries — granite's role is bulk containment, not precision insulation. The rock walls of an extraction chamber are doing real field management work.

**Story texture:** Old extraction chambers show the history of their use in the crack patterns on the walls. An experienced operator reads granite the way a navigator reads rock strata — the fracture lines tell you how many high-intensity operations the chamber has absorbed and where the next failure is likely to propagate.

---

### Fired clay / terracotta
**Conductivity:** low-
**Failure mode:** thermal — gradual, readable
**Insulation rank:** fourth intact, third sustained

The earliest reliable portable insulator — available at stone age, workable into any shape, consistent enough for apparatus use. Lower insulation ceiling than granite or porcelain but the failure mode is gradual and readable: as adjacent metal components run hotter, the ceramic shows it — color change, surface crazing, eventual spalling. An operator watching terracotta components has advance warning before failure.

Porous compared to porcelain, which means slightly less uniform field resistance — the field finds the most porous paths first, producing uneven insulation across a terracotta component over time. Not a problem at low intensity. Becomes significant at sustained medium intensity.

**Apparatus role:** Component-scale insulation in early and field apparatus. Vessel bodies, component housings, junction insulation. The development-period population built their first apparatus from this before porcelain was available. Terracotta apparatus components are the earliest generation — functional, legible failures, limited ceiling.

---

### Porcelain / stoneware
**Conductivity:** low-
**Failure mode:** thermal — gradual, higher ceiling than terracotta
**Insulation rank:** third intact, second sustained

The refined version of terracotta. Denser, less porous, more uniform — field resistance is more consistent across the component surface and the thermal failure ceiling is significantly higher. The same heat that crazes terracotta leaves porcelain intact. Porcelain components in apparatus last longer, fail later, and give more consistent insulation across their service life.

The trade-off is workability — porcelain requires more sophisticated kiln technology than terracotta, placing it at iron age rather than stone age availability. Coalition apparatus transitions from terracotta to porcelain as a legible marker of technical advancement. A porcelain-heavy apparatus is a later and more capable generation than a terracotta-heavy one.

**Apparatus role:** Preferred component insulation at story-present. Vessel bodies, precision housings, sustained-operation containment at the component level. Pairs with granite at larger scale — porcelain handles the shaped components, granite handles the structural containment.

---

### Rubber (natural)
**Conductivity:** low-
**Failure mode:** sustained degradation — drifts from low- toward low over service life
**Insulation rank:** first intact, fourth sustained

Peak insulation value of any material in the taxonomy at story-present. The field finds no pathway through intact rubber — it is the most complete barrier available. This makes it irreplaceable for joints, seals, and gaskets: the connection points between components where field leakage is most likely and where rigid materials cannot seal completely.

The limitation is service life under working conditions. Prolonged high-intensity exposure slowly softens and degrades the rubber matrix — the insulating property drifts, the flexibility that makes it useful at joints becomes limpness, eventually the seal fails. A rubber component in a high-intensity apparatus has a finite service life that experienced operators track and replace proactively.

Rubber is also the only flexible low- material. Every other insulator in the taxonomy is rigid. This makes rubber structurally irreplaceable regardless of its degradation ceiling — no rigid material can seal a moving joint or conform to an irregular surface the way rubber does.

**Apparatus role:** Primary joint sealant, gaskets, flexible connections. Strategic apparatus resource — natural rubber supply is geographically constrained, making it a Coalition logistics concern. Experienced apparatus operators carry replacement rubber seals as standard kit.

**Story texture:** The smell of degrading rubber is a reliable indicator that apparatus has been running hard. Operators learn to check rubber seals first when diagnosing field leakage — a softened or tacky seal is the most common source of unexpected field behavior in otherwise sound apparatus.

---

### Tar / pitch / bitumen
**Conductivity:** low-
**Failure mode:** thermal/flow — softens and flows at low heat ceiling
**Insulation rank:** fifth intact, fifth sustained

The pre-rubber sealant. Naturally occurring, available at stone age, used for waterproofing and adhesion long before anyone understood its field properties. When the development-period population built their first apparatus they sealed the joints with tar — not because they understood it as a field insulator but because it was what you used to seal things. The field insulation was accidental.

Lower insulation value than any other low- material and the lowest failure ceiling — heat from adjacent metal components softens tar well before it would affect terracotta or porcelain. A tar-sealed joint in a running apparatus will begin to flow and lose its seal under sustained medium intensity. This means early apparatus had a specific and predictable failure mode: joints leak as the operation runs longer and hotter.

**Apparatus role:** Gap-filler and joint sealant in early and expedient apparatus. Not a primary insulating material — fills the spaces between better insulators rather than replacing them. Presence of tar seals in apparatus is a legible sign of early manufacture, field repair, or expedient construction. An apparatus with tar seals is either old or improvised.

## Transducer materials — framework (decided)

A bidirectional transducer converts between field energy and mechanical energy in both directions. Every other material in the taxonomy is a one-way street — conducts, insulates, absorbs, or reads at the surface. Transducer materials are categorically different: field energy produces mechanical response, and mechanical stress near active substrate produces field perturbation.

This property derives from asymmetric crystalline structure — the same physical basis as real-world piezoelectricity. The decision: this analogue exists in the world. The field-mechanical transduction property is real.

**Implications:**
- Measurement: field intensity readable as mechanical quantity — vibration amplitude, resonance frequency. More continuous and precise than threshold indicators like tin.
- Generation: controlled mechanical stress on a transducer material near active substrate perturbs the field. A non-attuned person can interact with the field without attunement and without substrate contact — weakly, imprecisely, but really.
- Synchronization: multiple transducer instruments near the same substrate deposit resonate together.
- Communication: field perturbation may be detectable by another transducer instrument elsewhere in the same deposit. Not wireless communication — more like two tuning forks on the same table.

**The quartz spectrum:**
The property exists across a family of crystalline minerals at different intensities. Quartz is the democratized version — present everywhere, in sand, in granite, in most rock formations. The development-period population was accidentally interacting with the field through quartz tools and quartz-rich stone long before they understood what they were doing. Tourmaline is the high-performance rare version — stronger effect, sought out once the principle is understood. Beryl shows the effect weakly — hints at it without confirming it alone.

### Quartz / silicates
**Conductivity:** low-med
**Threshold:** med-high
**Transducer property:** bidirectional field-mechanical

At low field intensity near active substrate, quartz vibrates at very low amplitude — imperceptible to touch, below hearing threshold. No visible effect. A non-attuned person holding a quartz tool near active substrate feels nothing unusual.

At medium intensity, the vibration becomes audible as a faint resonance — a hum or ring that has no apparent source. The frequency is characteristic of the substrate deposit's current field intensity. Experienced operators learn to read quartz resonance the way sailors read wind — as continuous environmental information rather than discrete events.

At high intensity, the vibration becomes physically forceful. Quartz instruments near active substrate at high intensity may crack along crystalline planes from the mechanical stress of the resonance itself — a failure mode that looks like ordinary fracture but happens without any physical impact.

**The reverse direction:** Mechanical stress on quartz near active substrate perturbs the field. Striking a quartz instrument, grinding quartz-rich stone, even the ordinary mechanical stress of walking on quartz-bearing ground near a deposit — all produce small field perturbations. The development-period population was doing this constantly without knowing it. The Order's practitioners near quartz-bearing terrain are working in an environment with more ambient field noise than they would encounter on clean rock.

**Apparatus role:** Primary precision measurement material once the transducer property is understood. Quartz instruments give continuous field intensity readings rather than threshold events. Before the principle is understood, quartz instruments near substrate behave anomalously in ways that are noticed but not explained — the first generation of Coalition measurement apparatus probably incorporated quartz accidentally before deliberately.

**Tourmaline:** Rarer, stronger effect. The high-performance transducer. Sought out by the research frontier once the quartz principle is established. Produces stronger mechanical response at lower field intensity — more sensitive, more useful for detecting weak or depleted fields.

**Story texture:** The hum of quartz near active substrate is one of the ambient sounds of a Coalition extraction site. New operators find it unsettling. Experienced operators stop hearing it consciously and start using it as background intensity information — the pitch and volume are data even when not actively attended to.

### Bone (dry)
**Conductivity:** low
**Threshold:** med-high — calcium phosphate mineral
**Transducer property:** bidirectional field-mechanical — connects to attunement mechanism

Real-world bone is genuinely piezoelectric — mechanical stress produces electrical signals that guide bone remodeling. This property carries into the world. Dry bone near active substrate vibrates faintly in response to field energy. The effect is weaker than quartz — bone is less crystallographically ordered — but present.

The implication for living bone in attuned practitioners is the load-bearing decision:

**Attunement is not purely neurological.** The document currently describes attunement as almost certainly neurological coupling structures. This is incomplete rather than wrong. The neurological structures are the interpreter. The skeletal and connective tissue — which are piezoelectric — are part of the antenna. An attuned practitioner's body is physically resonating with the field at a structural level, not just perceiving it neurologically. The whole architecture of the body is participating.

**Non-attuned people** have the same piezoelectric bone. They receive the mechanical signal but have no neurological coupling to read it. Weak latent attunement may be a partial neurological coupling — the person feels vague unease, physical resonance, or unexplained fatigue near active substrate because their interpreter is partially online but not fully calibrated. The signal is arriving. Nothing is translating it.

**Attunement damage implications:** WORLD.md describes permanent damage as loss of granularity in coupling structures — almost certainly neurological. If the physical architecture is also involved, severe overexposure may produce structural damage beyond neurological sensitivity loss. The body having been resonated past its mechanical tolerance. This is a refactor target — not a contradiction of existing canon but an expansion that adds texture to what permanent damage actually looks like and feels like from the inside.

**Apparatus role:** Dry bone has no apparatus role. Its significance is entirely in what it implies about the biology of attunement and the physical mechanism of field coupling.

**Story texture:** An attuned practitioner near active substrate at high intensity may experience physical resonance — a vibration they feel in their bones literally, not metaphorically — alongside the neurological field perception. The two sensations are distinct. The neurological perception is the trained, precise, directional sense. The physical resonance is crude, omnidirectional, overwhelming at extremes. Overexposure events may involve both simultaneously.

### Pyrite / sulfides
**Conductivity:** med
**Threshold:** med
**Detection role:** surface oxidation as field-intensity indicator

Pyrite oxidizes in the real world to produce characteristic iridescent tarnish. Field energy accelerates this oxidation reaction — the same mechanism as phosphorus glow, just slower and less dramatic. Fresh pyrite near active substrate shows accelerated tarnishing before the field is strong enough to affect most other materials. The tarnish rate and color progression map onto field intensity — faint gold iridescence at low intensity, deeper bronze and purple at medium, rapid blackening at high.

This makes pyrite a passive historical record as well as a current indicator. A piece of pyrite pulled from near a substrate deposit shows in its tarnish pattern how long it has been exposed and at what approximate intensity. An experienced operator can read site history from pyrite the way a geologist reads strata.

**Apparatus role:** Passive exposure recorder and ambient intensity indicator. Pyrite near the walls of an extraction chamber accumulates a tarnish record of the chamber's operational history. Not precise enough for calibration — tin and quartz serve that role — but useful for site assessment before instruments are deployed.

**Story texture:** The iridescent shimmer of heavily tarnished pyrite is a visual signature of worked Vein sites. Coalition extraction infrastructure that has been running for years is surrounded by pyrite that has gone through the full tarnish sequence and blackened. A site with fresh-gold pyrite is either new or recently cleaned — both are operationally significant.

### Glass
**Conductivity:** low
**Threshold:** med — liquid transition visible
**Detection role:** progressive visible failure as intensity gauge

Glass at low field intensity shows nothing. No vibration, no color change, no surface effect. This is one of its apparatus uses — glass components in an apparatus assembly confirm by their inertness that the local field intensity is below medium. Negative evidence is information.

At medium intensity, the first visible effect appears at the thinnest cross-sections — a barely perceptible softening of sharp edges, a slight loss of optical clarity at the thinnest point. This is the warning phase. An operator watching glass components at this stage has time to respond.

At high intensity, visible flow begins at the thinnest point first, then propagates toward thicker sections. The glass does not fail uniformly — it fails at the point of maximum field exposure first, which maps the field gradient spatially. Where the glass begins to flow is where the field is most intense. The failure pattern is a field map.

**Apparatus role:** Passive field gradient indicator. Glass components placed at known positions in apparatus show not just that high intensity has been reached but where the field is concentrating. More informative than tin, which only indicates threshold crossing without spatial information. Less sensitive than quartz, which responds continuously.

**Story texture:** A glass vessel that has been through a high-intensity operation shows the history in its form — slightly rounded edges, a barely perceptible sag at the thinnest point. Experienced operators read this the way they read iron color or pyrite tarnish. New glass is perfectly sharp. Worked glass is subtly not.

### Obsidian
**Conductivity:** low
**Threshold:** med-high — volcanic glass, more ordered than manufactured glass
**Detection role:** edge fracture pattern as field gradient map

Obsidian holds longer than glass under field exposure — its volcanic formation produced a more uniform amorphous structure than kiln-fired glass, giving it a higher effective threshold before bulk softening begins. The difference matters in the field: where glass begins softening at medium intensity, obsidian holds into the high range.

The readable element is the edge. Obsidian fractures with extraordinary sharpness in normal conditions — the sharpest natural edge available at story-present. Near active substrate at medium intensity, micro-fractures develop along the sharpest edges first, before any bulk effect appears. The edges go dull in a pattern that maps onto the local field gradient — the sharpest edges facing the highest-intensity direction fracture first.

This makes obsidian a directional field indicator. Not just how strong the field is but which way it is strongest. An obsidian blade near active substrate develops micro-fracturing on the face toward the substrate before the opposite face shows any effect. The fracture pattern points.

**Apparatus role:** Directional field indicator at high intensity ranges where glass has already failed. Obsidian components remain functional as indicators at intensities that have already destroyed glass components. Niche but irreplaceable in high-intensity work where directional field information is needed.

**Story texture:** Obsidian tools recovered from near Vein sites often show anomalous edge degradation that looks like heavy use but isn't — the micro-fracturing from field exposure. A practitioner or operator familiar with the pattern can distinguish field degradation from use wear. Obsidian from heavily worked sites is essentially never sharp along the substrate-facing edge.

### Diamond
**Conductivity:** high+ (thermal)
**Threshold:** extreme — maximum density lattice
**Role:** structural argument endpoint, precision focusing material (cut), future research material

Diamond is the proof case that structure governs field behavior as completely as composition. Same element as charcoal — same atoms, maximum crystalline order instead of amorphous disorder — and the field response is at the opposite extreme of the taxonomy.

**Raw diamond — diffuse conduction.** The cubic crystal structure has no preferred axis — bonds are identical in all directions. Field energy conducts rapidly and uniformly in all directions simultaneously. The expression is diffuse rather than pointed. At low and medium intensity, raw diamond near active substrate simply runs warm uniformly with no visible effect, no state change, no mechanical response. High conductivity but no directionality.

**Cut diamond — focusing.** Diamond cutting creates planar faces aligned with crystallographic lattice planes. These faces do to field energy what facets do to light — concentrate and redirect it through controlled geometry. A diamond cut with precise planar faces focuses field expression rather than diffusing it. The geometry does the work the intrinsic crystal structure doesn't. The focusing effect scales directly with the precision and quality of the cut.

This makes cut diamond the only focusing material in the taxonomy. Not because of its composition or intrinsic structure alone, but because diamond is hard enough to be cut with extreme precision, the cut faces are crystallographically clean, and the high conductivity means there is substantial field energy to direct once the geometry exists to direct it.

At story-present, diamond cutting is jewelry-grade — good enough to demonstrate the focusing effect, not yet precise enough to engineer it reliably for apparatus use. A well-cut diamond near active substrate produces a concentrated field expression along the axis the geometry defines. A poorly-cut diamond diffuses like raw diamond. The difference between a focusing instrument component and an expensive curiosity is entirely in the craftsmanship of the cut.

**Threshold:** Effectively unreachable at story-present. The field energy required to begin pushing diamond toward its next state would destroy all surrounding material first. Diamond has no practical failure mode under current apparatus conditions.

**The structural argument:** The carbon pair — charcoal and diamond — is the clearest demonstration in the taxonomy that structure governs field behavior. Every Coalition researcher who understands both ends of this pair has the conceptual key to the semiconductor question: engineered structure is a path to engineered field behavior. Diamond is not just a material. It is an argument.

**Story texture:** Raw diamond near active substrate is underwhelming — it runs warm and does nothing else visible. A researcher encountering it for the first time expecting dramatic behavior is disappointed. The focusing behavior of cut diamond is what makes it significant, and that only reveals itself when someone thinks to cut it correctly and test the result. The discovery of cut diamond focusing is probably a story-present research frontier event rather than established knowledge.

### Graphite
**Conductivity:** med-high (directional)
**Threshold:** med
**Role:** directional conductor, pointer toward semiconductor question

Graphite is the middle term of the carbon argument — between charcoal's amorphous absorption and diamond's maximum-order thermal conduction. Same element, layered sheet structure, and the field response is neither diffuse nor extreme. It is directional.

Graphite conducts field energy along its layers and insulates across them. The conductivity axis matters: a graphite component oriented with its layers parallel to the field path conducts at med-high. The same component rotated ninety degrees conducts at something closer to low. No other material in the taxonomy has a field response that depends on physical orientation.

This is the first hint in the taxonomy of what semiconductors eventually represent — a material whose field conductivity is not a fixed property but a variable one, controllable by how you position or configure it. Graphite's version of this is crude: rotation is not a precise control mechanism and graphite is not easy to work with in the orientations required. But the principle is present. The Coalition's research frontier that understands graphite's directional conductivity is one conceptual step closer to understanding that field conductivity can be engineered rather than merely selected.

At medium field intensity along the conductive axis, graphite behaves like a moderate metal — consistent conductivity, some thermal expression at the far end, no dramatic effects. At the same intensity across the insulating axis, almost nothing. The transition between these behaviors as orientation changes is continuous rather than stepped — the conductivity rotates smoothly.

**Apparatus role:** Marginal at story-present — graphite is known but not yet industrially refined, and working it in controlled orientations is difficult. Useful in research contexts where the directional property can be studied. The apparatus application that will eventually follow from understanding it correctly is significant; the current-era application is limited.

**Story texture:** Coalition researchers who have worked with graphite in field conditions describe the orientation effect as uncanny — the same piece of material behaving completely differently depending on which way you hold it. The experience of rotating a graphite component and watching its field response change is one of the more immediately convincing demonstrations that structure governs behavior. Researchers who have seen this tend to become more interested in structural questions generally.

### Salt water / brine
**Conductivity:** med+
**Threshold:** very low
**Role:** modified water — environment-dependent field amplifier

Free water conducts field energy at med and transitions at very low threshold. Salt dissolved in water increases conductivity by disrupting the molecular structure in ways that make the fluid more responsive to field energy — the same mechanism that makes salt water electrically conductive in the real world. The effect scales with salinity: fresh water at med, brackish water at med+, concentrated brine approaching the lower end of high.

The environmental implication is significant. Sea water, sweat, tears, blood serum, and soil moisture in coastal or humid environments are all saline to varying degrees. A practitioner or apparatus operating near the sea has more conductive ambient fluid in everything around them — in the air as humid aerosol, in the ground, in their own body's surface moisture. The effective field conductivity of the environment is higher, which means both the capability ceiling and the ambient hazard are elevated.

**Tactical implication:** Coastal and humid environments amplify field effects relative to dry inland terrain. An Order practitioner fighting near the sea is working with more conductive environmental fluid — the same field input produces larger effects on surrounding matter. A Coalition apparatus operating in humid conditions runs hotter and requires more careful management than the same apparatus in dry conditions. Neither side's doctrine was written for the same environment as the other's, which means cross-terrain engagements produce surprises.

**Apparatus role:** Not a construction material. An environmental variable that apparatus design must account for. Site-specific calibration is required — apparatus calibrated in dry inland conditions will behave differently when deployed coastally. This is a known problem at story-present with no clean solution beyond recalibration.

**Story texture:** Experienced operators notice the difference immediately when moving between dry and coastal sites. The apparatus feels more responsive, more volatile — the same inputs produce stronger outputs. New operators in coastal conditions who haven't been told to expect this are the source of a specific category of accident.

### Blood
**Conductivity:** med
**Threshold:** low
**Role:** composite fluid — two simultaneous field pathways

Blood is the only fluid in the taxonomy that carries two simultaneous field pathways: water and iron. The water content follows the water model — field energy moves through it at med conductivity, transitions at low threshold, produces steam under overdriving. The iron content — hemoglobin — follows the metal conduction model, carrying field energy directionally through the iron-containing molecule chains.

These two pathways operate simultaneously and interact. At low field intensity, the water pathway dominates — blood near active substrate warms and the transition threshold is low, producing biological damage through the same internal steam mechanism as water in tissue. At medium intensity, the metal pathway becomes significant — field energy conducts through the hemoglobin in ways that affect the biological function of the molecule itself, not just through thermal damage. Hemoglobin's ability to carry oxygen depends on the iron's chemical state. Field energy conducted through the iron pathway can alter that state directly, producing a form of damage distinct from thermal overdriving.

**Combat and healing implications:** An Order practitioner working on a wound is working on a material with two simultaneous field pathways that interact differently at different intensities. Green-register healing at low intensity works primarily through the water pathway — stimulating biological processes by adding energy to the fluid. At higher intensity the iron pathway becomes active, and what is doing changes character. The practitioner who doesn't account for this — who pushes healing intensity past the point where the water pathway dominates — begins affecting the hemoglobin directly. The difference between healing and a specific and unusual form of internal damage is an intensity threshold that experienced practitioners learn to feel for and novices often don't know exists.

**Attunement and the body:** Blood circulates through an attuned practitioner's body constantly, carrying both pathways through every tissue. High field exposure affects the practitioner's own blood — the iron pathway conducting through hemoglobin, the water pathway producing internal thermal effects. This is part of the biological mechanism of overexposure damage that WORLD.md describes but doesn't fully account for at the material level. The attunement section refactor should note this.

## One-liner profiles — derived from governing principle

These entries have effects clear enough from the governing principle and established category logic that they don't require full profiles. One sentence on field behavior, one on functional role or hazard.

### Marble / calcite
Field energy addition produces predictable cleavage along crystallographic planes before thermal effects. More workable than granite but not a reliable insulator — cleaves rather than holds under field stress. Structural and decorative use only.

### Clay (unfired)
Wet clay buffers field energy through water content — the same mechanism as green wood, temporary and unreliable. Dry clay is friable and field finds grain pathways. Neither state has apparatus use; relevant as site terrain material and early construction substrate.

### Limestone / sandstone
Porosity means field energy finds grain pathways rather than being transmitted uniformly or resisted uniformly. Uneven, unpredictable response. Terrain material — no apparatus role.

### Flint / chert
Energy addition drives fracture before any thermal effect — the field finds the same pathways a knapper does, producing sharp-edged fragments. No thermal pathway, no insulation property. Edge tool material and site hazard under high field intensity.

### Corundum (ruby / sapphire)
Harder and more ordered than quartz — field response more uniform than ordinary stone, higher threshold before any visible effect. Trace element color variation may produce measurable field response variation between ruby and sapphire; worth testing but not yet established. Precision material candidate once the variation question is resolved.

### Beryl (emerald / aquamarine)
Hexagonal crystal structure gives weak transducer-family behavior — faint mechanical response to field energy, weaker than quartz, stronger than ordinary stone. Too weak to be a reliable detection material alone but consistent enough to confirm the transducer principle when encountered alongside quartz. Beryllium content open question.

### Carbonate gems (malachite etc.)
Carbon in compound form behaves like mineral rather than like diamond — field response follows the stone category, not the carbon category. Malachite's co-occurrence with copper deposits makes it a geological indicator for copper proximity, which is apparatus-relevant as a prospecting signal rather than a field material.

### Organic gems (amber, pearl)
Field interacts with both more like organic tissue than mineral — energy addition at low intensity stimulates rather than conducts, biological origin governs behavior. Amber's potential to preserve field-state signatures from its formation period is an open question with story implications not yet resolved.

### Wood (dry)
Low field conductivity but combustion pathway is reached before any structural phase change — fire risk near active substrate at medium intensity. Not an insulator because combustion failure mode is catastrophic and unpredictable. Construction material at distance from active field only.

### Wood (green / wet)
Water content absorbs field energy and buffers response — temporarily useful as a field barrier but not reliable under sustained exposure as steam formation weakens structure. The buffer effect is real and exploitable in the short term; it degrades as the wood dries under sustained field exposure.

### Leather / hide
Low conductivity, moderate threshold, degrades under sustained field exposure through desiccation under energy removal or surface damage under energy addition. Not apparatus material. Relevant for operator clothing and equipment near active field — leather degrades faster in working conditions than in ordinary use.

### Bone (dry)
See transducer section for full implications. One-liner field behavior: dry bone vibrates faintly near active substrate through piezoelectric response, weaker than quartz. No apparatus role; significance is entirely in what it implies about the attunement mechanism.

### Shell / chitin
Shell (calcium carbonate) responds like thin calcite — cleaves predictably under field stress, no reliable insulation. Chitin (protein polymer) responds more like leather — degrades under sustained exposure. Both are armor and tool materials whose field behavior matters for characters wearing or using them near active substrate.

### Plant fiber (linen, cotton)
Combustion threshold reached before any other field effect. Insulator only at very low intensity — useful for low-field-exposure operator clothing but not apparatus material. Burns predictably and quickly near medium-intensity field.

### Wool / animal fiber
Moisture retention buffers field slightly longer than dry plant fiber — smolders rather than combusts cleanly, giving marginally more warning before failure. Same functional limit as plant fiber: clothing material at low field intensity only.

### Water (in tissue)
Same transition physics as free water but constrained by cellular structure — the 1700x volume expansion at liquid-gas transition produces damage from inside living tissue rather than external pressure effects. This is the primary mechanism of overexposure tissue damage. See blood entry for the additional iron pathway in vascular tissue.

### Air / gas
Localized energy addition produces pressure differentials immediately at low threshold — airflow, then concussive effects at high intensity. Combustion enabler when fuel is present. Environmental variable rather than apparatus material; wind patterns near active substrate sites are affected by field-induced pressure gradients.

### Coal tar / early oil
Combustion threshold very low near active field — essentially the same hazard category as gunpowder but slower-igniting and harder to extinguish once lit. Ambient fuel hazard at Vein sites. Not a field material in any useful sense.

### Portland cement / concrete
Matrix resists field transmission while water trapped within cured concrete responds normally — internal steam formation under medium-to-high field intensity fractures the matrix from inside without visible external warning. Coalition structures built from concrete near active field have a specific and non-obvious failure mode.

### Gunpowder / black powder
Combustion threshold effectively zero near active field — ignites from ambient field energy at intensities that leave other materials unaffected. Shapes military doctrine entirely: Coalition forces cannot safely use powder-based weapons near Order-active practitioners, and Order practitioners near Coalition powder stores have an obvious tactical option. The mutual constraint is one of the defining features of how this war is fought.

### Brick / lime mortar
Composite material — brick faces resist field transmission while mortar joints create pathways. Field finds the joints. Unreliable as insulator at any scale where joint density is high. Site construction material; the joint pathway effect is a known weakness in Coalition extraction chamber construction that older sites have partially solved through mortar replacement with porcelain grout.

### Glass (full one-liner entry)
See detection/indicator profile above — full profile already written. One-liner: glass is a field-state indicator whose progressive softening from thinnest cross-section toward bulk maps the field gradient spatially. Negative evidence (intact glass) confirms field below medium intensity.
