# Refined substrate — four axes of development
*Companion to D005 · scores are 0–10, primitive → apex*

---

## The four axes

| Axis | Question being solved | Primitive end | Apex end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shape | Where does expression go? | Omnidirectional rubble | Composite protected orientation |
| Size | How much surface area per mass? | Irregular large chunks | Colloidal suspension |
| Matter form | How available is the energy? | Raw closed-state solid | Metastable intermediate, pre-primed |
| Concentration | How dense is the fuel? | Mixed ore with waste rock | Fully beneficiated, minimal waste |

---

## Progression by era

### Development period — pre-apparatus through phase 1

| Axis | Score | State |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | 1 | Graded rubble only. Deliberate fracture discovered empirically. No concept of orientation. |
| Size | 3–4 | Optimal grain size found through apparatus failure patterns. Grading screens in use. |
| Matter form | 0–1 | Not yet a named question. Raw substrate loaded as extracted. |
| Concentration | 2–3 | Crude hand-sorting of rich from poor material. No systematic beneficiation. |

Key discovery: finer grain means more intense expression — up to an optimal size. Too fine degrades performance. This is the first empirical size parameter.

---

### Liberation war era — phase 2 apparatus

| Axis | Score | State |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | 3 | Platelet aggregate developed. Stamp mills produce consistently flat fragments. Statistical self-orientation during chamber loading. |
| Size | 5–6 | Grading systematised. Optimal grain size known per apparatus type. Industrial-scale milling. |
| Matter form | 1 | Still not a named axis. Occasional anomalous performance from specific batches — not yet investigated. |
| Concentration | 5 | Industrial beneficiation in place. Significant improvement in fuel density. Waste processing begins. |

Key discovery: fragment shape affects statistical face orientation during packing. Platelet geometry produces passive directional bias at no operational cost.

---

### Current era — phase 3, research frontier beginning

| Axis | Score | State |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | 6 | Formed geometry unlocked for vehicle-scale components. Cleave-along-natural-planes technique. Precision refining facilities exist at limited scale. |
| Size | 7 | Full grading range in production. Powder grades for installation, formed grades for vehicle/personal. |
| Matter form | 2 | Beginning to be investigated. Anomalous batch performance now attributed to pre-activation state differences. No controlled production yet. D005 open. |
| Concentration | 7 | High-purity grades available. Tiered supply chain: bulk installation grade vs. precision personal grade. |

Key question: is matter form the missing variable that explains why some refined substrate outperforms equivalent-grade material on the other three axes?

---

### Frontier — projected, not yet reached

| Axis | Score | State |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | 9 | Composite architecture. Substrate elements embedded in Vein-phase matrix, internally oriented, mirror faces protected. Looks nothing like rock. |
| Size | 8–9 | Colloidal suspension achievable at lab scale. Apparatus architecture shifts to circulation chamber model. |
| Matter form | 6 | Metastable intermediate state achieved at lab scale. Pre-primed substrate requires less apparatus work per output unit. Handling risk: spontaneous release under damage or improper storage. |
| Concentration | 9 | Near-complete beneficiation. Waste fraction minimal. Personal-grade fuel viable as a manufactured product. |

Key implication: composite architecture makes refined substrate visually unidentifiable as substrate. Order intelligence cannot identify it on sight — must break it to find mirror faces inside.

## D005 dependency map

Matter form is the least developed axis and the most consequential unknown for D005. The appearance question — what does refined substrate look like — is largely answered by shape and size. The performance question — what does it enable — depends heavily on matter form.

The visual signature of each grade:

| Grade | Appearance |
|---|---|
| Installation bulk | Dense black granular fill. Irregular. Mirror faces visible on individual grains. Looks like processed ore. |
| Vehicle precision | Formed dark rods or discs. Clean geometric edges. Mirror faces oriented along the long axis, visible on end surfaces. Looks manufactured. |
| Personal composite | Dense black ceramic-like block or cartridge. No visible mirror faces on exterior. Slightly wrong weight. Looks inert. Mirror faces internal, only visible on fracture. |
| Frontier colloidal | Dark viscous suspension. Mercury-like surface tension anomalies. No solid form. Requires sealed circulation system. |
