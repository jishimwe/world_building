Vein Materials Taxonomy

Story-present materials and how each responds to the field: conductivity, state-change threshold, failure mode and apparatus role. The field moves matter along the energy continuum; the variety of effects comes from the variety of matter, not from the field doing different things.

Invariants

  • Metals conduct but do not store: conduction stops when the source is removed.
  • Bidirectional field-mechanical transduction comes only from asymmetric crystalline structure: the quartz family (weakly in beryl and dry bone). Every other material is one-way.
  • Matter form affects speed and efficiency; it does not unlock capability tiers.
Frameworks: insulators, transducers, Vein-adjacent phases

Insulation stack

All low− materials actively resist field transmission. They differ by failure mode and by insulation under working conditions.

Intact:

  1. Rubber (peak)
  2. Granite
  3. Porcelain
  4. Terracotta
  5. Tar / pitch

Sustained working conditions:

  1. Granite
  2. Porcelain
  3. Terracotta
  4. Rubber (degraded)
  5. Tar / pitch

Well-designed apparatus combines both orderings: granite and porcelain for sustained containment, rubber at joints.

Transducers

A bidirectional transducer converts between field energy and mechanical energy in both directions. Every other material is one-way: it conducts, insulates, absorbs or reads at the surface.

The property derives from asymmetric crystalline structure, the analogue of piezoelectricity. Quartz is the common version. Tourmaline is the rare, stronger version sought by the research frontier. Beryl shows the effect weakly. Dry bone shows it weakly, which ties into the attunement mechanism.

Uses: measurement (continuous, unlike threshold indicators), weak generation without attunement, synchronization, and deposit-local signalling ("two tuning forks on the same table").

Vein-adjacent phases

Research materials, not production materials. They exist only at Vein sites and revert when removed. Each solved one engineering problem and compressed several generations of normal development into one.

Site dependence is a permanent strategic-geography constraint: the best materials for working the resource exist only where the resource is.

The research frontier is searching for a stable material that does what the phases do without reverting. Success would mark the threshold between the Vein as located fuel and the Vein as distributed power.
