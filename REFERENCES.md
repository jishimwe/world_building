## What this file is for

References are not sources to copy from. They are a shared vocabulary — a way to point at a feeling, a structure, or a quality and say *that, but applied here*. When a design decision is unclear, come back to this file and ask which reference it should feel closer to.

---

## Threads

Before the list, these are the patterns that run across almost all references below. These threads are more useful than any individual entry.

**The world doesn't center you.** Events happen at scale. Factions have their own momentum. The protagonist is one force among many, not the axis everything revolves around. The story doesn't exist because of the player — it exists, and the player is caught inside it.

**Bonds under pressure.** The emotional core is always a relationship being tested by circumstances larger than the people in it. The bond doesn't resolve the circumstances. It just makes them bearable — or unbearable when it breaks.

**No clean moral sides.** Factions have reasons. The enemy isn't evil, they're wrong in a different direction. The player should be able to understand, if not sympathize with, every side of the conflict.

**Group texture.** The way people who know each other *talk* — the shorthand, the callbacks, the history living in every exchange. A group that has been through things together sounds different from a group that just met.

**The intimate inside the structural.** The most personal stories are the ones most shaped by forces the characters can't control. The war doesn't care about the relationship. The relationship happens anyway.

## Game references

### Final Fantasy Tactics
*Primary inspiration*
- Story structure and political complexity as backdrop to personal drama
- Named characters with distinct voices and histories embedded in a larger conflict
- Class system and job progression as player expression
- Tone: tragedy that earns its weight through character time, not event announcement

**Borrow:** Narrative ambition, character writing, the feeling that the world preceded chapter one.
**Avoid:** Pacing issues in the back half, protagonist as passive observer of other people's story.

---

### Tactics Ogre: Let Us Cling Together
*Primary inspiration*
- World-building with genuine political and historical texture
- Moral weight of faction choices — no side is simply right
- Permadeath as consequence, not punishment
- The feeling that the world has a history the player is only partially seeing

**Borrow:** World density, moral ambiguity, the sense that events have causes and consequences beyond the plot frame.
**Avoid:** Pacing, late-game system bloat.

---

### Fire Emblem series
*Relationship/support system reference*
- Support conversations that build character through small moments, not just plot beats
- Paired bonuses and combat interactions that make relationships mechanically legible
- The roster feeling personal through accumulated time and deployment choices

**Borrow:** Support system structure, the way small conversations add up to genuine attachment.
**Note:** Fire Emblem's support system can feel gamified and transactional — the goal here is something that feels more earned and less like a checklist.

---

### XCOM series
*Permadeath tension and squad attachment reference*
- The gap between "a soldier" and "Jenkins, who survived the mission in Shanghai" — named units accumulate meaning through survival
- Permadeath as a design tool for emotional investment, not difficulty
- The rescue/abort decision as a moment of genuine player anguish

**Borrow:** How attachment forms through shared survival, the emotional texture of loss when it's a unit you've carried for twenty missions.

---

### Baldur's Gate 1 & 2
*Reactive world and character depth reference*
- Companions with histories, opinions, and relationships to each other — not just to the player
- A world that reacts to who you bring and what you've done
- Character writing that treats party members as people with inner lives, not support functions

**Borrow:** Companion interiority, inter-party relationships, the sense that the world notices.

---

### Clair Obscur: Expedition 33
*Gameplay and story reference — 2025, Sandfall Interactive*
- A small, focused cast where every character's motivation is legible and earned — no extraneous backstory, no wasted time
- A world with deep history that the player arrives at mid-event, without scrolling exposition — the mystery of what came before is part of the texture
- Death and limited time as structural premises, not dramatic devices — the world is shaped by loss before the story begins
- Demonstrated that a focused 20–30 hour RPG can land harder than a 100-hour one: every scene deliberate, no lulls
- Solo dev / small team origin (30 people) — proof that this kind of ambition is achievable at that scale

**Borrow:** Cast focus and economy of character writing, dropping the player into a world mid-history, the emotional weight of a premise built around mortality and limited time.
**Note:** Combat is real-time/reactive — not a direct reference for the tactical layer, but the *feeling* of combat being rewarding and readable is worth studying.

---

### Disco Elysium
*Play this — add notes after*
- Possibly the best example of a game world that feels like it existed before chapter one and will continue after the ending
- Political texture that is present and specific without being didactic
- Writing that trusts the player to sit with complexity

**Borrow:** World texture, the feeling of arriving somewhere mid-history, political writing that doesn't simplify.

## Non-game references

### Arcane (TV, seasons 1–2)
*World-building and story structure*
- A world with deep history that the story reveals sideways, not through exposition
- Factions in conflict where every side has a point and a wound
- Character relationships that are the emotional engine of a story that is nominally about something else
- Visual and tonal commitment — the world has a distinct identity, not just a setting

**Note:** The League of Legends lore is not the reference here. Arcane works independently of it, and that independence is part of what makes it work.

---

### Dune (Frank Herbert)
*World-building reference*
- A world with geology, politics, religion, and economics that predate the story and will outlast it
- Factions with coherent internal logic, not just roles in the plot
- The feeling that every named thing has a history the text isn't fully telling you

---

### Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood
*Bond under pressure reference*
- A brotherly relationship that is the emotional anchor of a story operating at geopolitical scale
- The bond doesn't resolve the conflict — it's what makes the conflict bearable to experience
- Characters who are changed by what they go through, not just moved by it

---

### Hunter x Hunter
*World indifference reference*
- The world moves on from the main characters. Arcs end and the world doesn't pause.
- Power systems and factions exist independently of the protagonist's involvement
- The Chimera Ant arc specifically: events at a scale where the protagonists are one factor among many

---

### Gundam series
*Faction war and moral complexity reference*
- Wars with no clear good or bad side — only sides with reasons
- The emergence of third parties who complicate the binary
- Individual characters caught inside institutional conflicts they didn't choose
- War as a system that produces atrocities regardless of which side commits them

---

### Attack on Titan (seasons 1–3)
*Escalating revelation and faction complexity reference*
- A story that systematically expands its frame — what looked like one kind of story becomes another
- Factions revealed to have reasons that recontextualize earlier events
- The cost of survival as a persistent theme, not a one-time dramatic beat

**Note:** Seasons 1–3 only. The later seasons are a different reference problem entirely.

---

### Everything Everywhere All at Once
*Bonds under structural pressure reference*
- The intimate inside the cosmic — a story about a mother and daughter that is also about everything
- Mundane relationships carrying the full weight of the narrative
- The emotional resolution doesn't fix the circumstances — it changes how the characters stand inside them

---

### Good Kid, M.A.A.D City — Kendrick Lamar
*Tone and structure reference*
- A deeply personal story that is completely shaped by forces outside the narrator's control
- The environment is not backdrop — it is active, it has consequences, it makes demands
- Individual voice and identity persisting inside structural pressure
- The album works as a complete arc: you understand the world, you understand the person, you understand how the world shaped the person

---

### Critical Role — Campaigns 1 & 2
*Group dynamics and character texture reference*
- How a group of people who know each other talk — shorthand, callbacks, the history in every exchange
- Relationships that developed through play, not through scripted revelation
- The way loss lands differently when it follows genuine investment
- Campaign 2 specifically: a found family dynamic where the bonds are the story

---

### It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia / How I Met Your Mother
*Group dynamic reference*
- The texture of a group that has been together long enough to have shared language, running jokes, and unspoken grievances
- Characters who are fully known to each other and still capable of surprising each other
- The difference between how this group talks to each other versus how they talk to outsiders

**Note:** The tone here is obviously very different from the game. The reference is specifically the *dynamic* — the quality of familiarity — not the comedy or the content.
